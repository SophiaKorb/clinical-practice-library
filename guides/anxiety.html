School Access Toolkit

Anxiety Barrier → Support Cheat Sheet

For parents, recovery coaches, and school teams in Arizona

START HEREDo not choose supports from the diagnosis. Choose the school problem first. Find the matching barrier, mark a few supports, and bring the short codes to the team. The team must individualize the plan.

Use this guide in 4 moves

1. Notice: Write what happens at school, where it happens, and what seems to make it better or worse.

2. Match: Choose the functional barrier that best explains the access problem. More than one may fit.

3. Shortlist: Circle a few support codes, such as 3-B. Ask the student what is useful, realistic, or embarrassing.

4. Define and review: State who provides each support, where, when, how often, and how the team will know it happened and helped.

Barrier index

1. Worry blocks attention and working memory

2. Fear of mistakes blocks starting or finishing

3. Speaking or being observed blocks participation

4. Separation, arrival, or transitions delay entry

5. Avoidance reduces access to learning

6. Panic or physical symptoms interrupt the day

7. Testing anxiety distorts performance

IMPORTANTThese are options to discuss, not automatic entitlements. A support belongs in a plan only when evaluation data and the student’s needs support it. A student may need access accommodations and specialized instruction at the same time.

Barrier 1: Worry blocks attention and working memory

WHAT THIS MAY LOOK LIKEThe student misses instruction, rereads, freezes, or cannot hold steps in mind.

Clarify before choosing: In which classes, tasks, people, times, or settings does this happen? What changes when the demand or support changes?

Supports to discuss

PickCodeSupport and implementable wording
keep directions visible. Provide short written steps and a model.
Make public pressure. Use private checks instead of public correction.
preview the lesson structure. Show what will happen, how long it will last, and how it ends.
Give brief grounding access. Use a taught, time-limited strategy with a clear return cue.
check understanding after regulation. Ask for the first step once the student can process information.

Barrier 2: Fear of mistakes blocks starting or finishing

WHAT THIS MAY LOOK LIKEErasing, reassurance seeking, perfectionism, or refusal consumes work time.

Clarify before choosing: In which classes, tasks, people, times, or settings does this happen? What changes when the demand or support changes?

Supports to discuss

PickCodeSupport and implementable wording
Be clear about “done”. Provide a rubric, model, item limit, and stopping point.
limit repeated checking. Agree on a small number of checks, then prompt submission.
chunk and submit sections. Use brief checkpoints rather than one high-stakes finish.
give neutral feedback. Correct the specific task without global praise or alarm.
teach flexible responding. Use explicit instruction and supported practice with tolerable mistakes.

Barrier 3: Speaking or being observed blocks participation

WHAT THIS MAY LOOK LIKEThe student knows the answer but cannot respond in groups, presentations, or unfamiliar settings.

Clarify before choosing: In which classes, tasks, people, times, or settings does this happen? What changes when the demand or support changes?

Supports to discuss

PickCodeSupport and implementable wording
build a participation ladder. Move from private or recorded response toward the required skill in planned steps.
give preview and preparation. Provide questions, roles, or presentation expectations in advance.
Let the student an initial alternate response. Use written, paired, AAC, or recorded response while working toward the goal.
avoid surprise public performance. Do not use cold calling or unannounced reading when it is not essential.
measure progress by steps. Track the planned participation behavior, not whether anxiety disappears.

Barrier 4: Separation, arrival, or transitions delay entry

WHAT THIS MAY LOOK LIKEThe student cannot enter, remains with a caregiver, or misses class during transitions.

Clarify before choosing: In which classes, tasks, people, times, or settings does this happen? What changes when the demand or support changes?

Supports to discuss

PickCodeSupport and implementable wording
Try a brief arrival routine. Name the staff greeter, location, sequence, and caregiver exit.
Give a visual transition plan. Show the next activity and the first easy action.
Try a planned check-in. Schedule one short staff contact rather than unlimited reassurance.
reinforce approach. Notice completion of the agreed step, not escape from it.
review attendance data. Track arrival time, time in instruction, and which supports improve entry.

Barrier 5: Avoidance reduces access to learning

WHAT THIS MAY LOOK LIKEThe student escapes tests, classes, peers, or tasks and falls further behind.

Clarify before choosing: In which classes, tasks, people, times, or settings does this happen? What changes when the demand or support changes?

Supports to discuss

PickCodeSupport and implementable wording
Try graded exposure with qualified planning. Create small, repeatable steps toward participation; do not improvise flooding.
keep a return expectation. A break has a time, location, support, and next step.
adjust excess demand. Reduce unrelated sensory, language, or workload barriers while retaining the core goal.
prevent accidental reward for escape. Offer support before escalation and return to a doable step.
coordinate with treatment carefully. With consent, align school supports with clinical recommendations without making school attendance depend on therapy.

Barrier 6: Panic or physical symptoms interrupt the day

WHAT THIS MAY LOOK LIKENausea, dizziness, pain, rapid breathing, or fear leads to repeated nurse visits or leaving school.

Clarify before choosing: In which classes, tasks, people, times, or settings does this happen? What changes when the demand or support changes?

Supports to discuss

PickCodeSupport and implementable wording
create a symptom response plan. List health red flags, regulation steps, adult roles, duration, and return criteria.
Let the student brief access to water or restroom. Define access while protecting instruction and dignity.
Try one calm response. Staff avoid repeated questioning or escalating reassurance.
Give a low-stimulation recovery place. Name the location and maximum routine time before review.
track time out of instruction. Review patterns and effectiveness, not only number of visits.

Barrier 7: Testing anxiety distorts performance

WHAT THIS MAY LOOK LIKEBlanking, rushing, repeated checking, or physical symptoms hide learning.

Clarify before choosing: In which classes, tasks, people, times, or settings does this happen? What changes when the demand or support changes?

Supports to discuss

PickCodeSupport and implementable wording
Try familiar testing conditions. Specify location, routine, and eligible tests.
Be clear about extended time. State amount and whether it includes breaks.
segment the test. Give one section at a time with a brief reset.
Give a practice item. Confirm format comprehension before scoring begins.
avoid unnecessary time displays. Use private time cues if visible countdowns worsen performance.

Home support is separate from the school plan

BOUNDARYAn IEP or 504 plan governs school responsibilities, not parenting at home. Home observations can help explain patterns, but school barriers should be based on school access and performance.

Support small steps toward feared activities instead of making avoidance permanent.

Keep reassurance brief and consistent.

Coordinate with clinicians only with consent and a clear school purpose.

Vocabulary legend

Access: The student can reach, understand, participate in, and benefit from education.

Adverse educational impact: A disability-related effect on school performance, broadly understood; not limited to report-card grades.

Evaluation: Information collected in all suspected areas to identify needs and support eligibility decisions.

FAPE: Free appropriate public education. IDEA and Section 504 use related but not identical standards.

IDEA: Federal special-education law for eligible students who need special education and related services.

IEP: The written IDEA program with present levels, goals, services, supports, placement, and progress reporting.

MET: Arizona term often used for the multidisciplinary evaluation team and evaluation process.

PWN: Prior written notice under IDEA explaining a proposed or refused action and the reasons and data behind it.

Section 504: Federal civil-rights law barring disability discrimination and requiring individualized FAPE in public elementary and secondary schools.

SDI: Specially designed instruction: adapting content, method, or delivery to meet unique disability-related needs.
