Understanding New York IEPs

Independent educational resource · Created by Sophia Korb Cohon, PhD

Where to start

In New York, the Committee on Special Education (CSE) coordinates the school-age special education eligibility and IEP process. For eligible preschool-age children, the Committee on Preschool Special Education (CPSE) has a corresponding role. An Individualized Education Program (IEP) records individualized goals, services, supports, and other required provisions. NYSED prescribes a mandatory IEP form.

If you have concerns about a student's educational access, write down concrete examples, questions, and the supports already attempted. Contact the school district for its referral and evaluation procedures. Parents participate in the committee process and should receive required notices and safeguards.

Prepare for a CSE or CPSE meeting

  1. Gather schoolwork, evaluation reports, family observations, strengths, and concerns.
  2. Describe barriers in observable terms: what task, setting, or activity is hard, and what happens?
  3. Ask how evaluations address all suspected areas of need and how family information will be considered.
  4. Discuss the student's present performance, measurable annual goals, services, accommodations, implementation, and progress monitoring.
  5. Request the written recommendations and IEP, and record any unanswered questions for follow-up.

These are meeting-preparation suggestions, not a substitute for the formal referral process or individualized legal guidance.

IEP versus Section 504

An IEP is developed under IDEA and applicable New York special education requirements for eligible students. A Section 504 plan addresses disability-related access and nondiscrimination under a separate federal legal framework. A diagnosis by itself does not automatically determine eligibility for either; ask the school to evaluate the student's individual needs using the applicable criteria.

How to request help

  1. Write down what the student can do and where the barrier appears: lessons, reading, communication, behavior, attendance, or participation.
  2. Send a dated evaluation or support request to the district's special education contact and retain a copy.
  3. Ask which process applies, what information the team needs, and how to receive current parent-rights information.

New York uses CSE and CPSE committees, depending on age. Families should obtain current notices, review the prescribed state IEP form, and prepare questions about individual services and school placement.

Prepare for an IEP meeting

  • Bring evaluations, current schoolwork, teacher communications, and the child's strengths and interests.
  • Prepare questions about measurable goals, accommodations, related services, progress reporting, and who implements each support.
  • Ask how caregivers and the student can contribute, and request a copy of the written plan and follow-up steps.

If the family moves between states

Keep the IEP, eligibility and evaluation documents, related-service records, and progress reports. Contact the receiving district promptly and request a transition meeting if needed. IDEA addresses comparable services for students transferring with an IEP, while the new state may have distinct procedures.

For ongoing concerns, document specific events and consult the district and state education department's published parent-resource and dispute-resolution information.
