California Developmental Disability, Autism & Behavioral Health Resources

State-specific guide for clinicians, families, and support staff. Reviewed September 2026.

Regional Centers

California's Department of Developmental Services contracts with Regional Centers to assess eligibility and coordinate services for people with developmental disabilities.

Regional Centers provide eligibility assessment at no charge. Once a person is eligible, a service coordinator helps develop and monitor an Individual Program Plan and connect the person with services and supports.

For ongoing Lanterman Act eligibility, the developmental disability must begin before age 18, be expected to continue indefinitely, and constitute a substantial disability. Autism, intellectual disability, cerebral palsy, epilepsy, and certain closely related conditions are included in the statutory framework.

A diagnosis by itself does not guarantee Regional Center eligibility. Functional impact and the statutory criteria still matter.

Provisional eligibility for young children

California allows provisional Regional Center eligibility for some children from birth through age four without requiring a full developmental-disability diagnosis. The child must have a disability that is not solely physical and significant functional limitations in at least two major life activities.

A three- or four-year-old does not have to have received Early Start services first in order to be considered for provisional eligibility.

Early Start: birth to 36 months

Early Start is California's early-intervention system for infants and toddlers.

Children from birth through 36 months may qualify because of a developmental delay, an established risk condition, or high risk for substantial developmental disability.

Anyone may make a referral, including a parent, medical provider, family member, foster parent, neighbor, or child-care provider.

DDS states that after referral, the Regional Center or local education agency generally has 45 days to complete the evaluation/assessment process and, when the child is eligible, develop an Individualized Family Service Plan.

Early Start BabyLine: 800-515-BABY (800-515-2229)

Medi-Cal Behavioral Health Treatment for children

Medi-Cal covers medically necessary Behavioral Health Treatment for eligible members under age 21. This can include ABA and other evidence-based behavioral interventions and is not limited only to children with an autism diagnosis when medical necessity is established.

For children enrolled in a Medi-Cal managed care plan, BHT is obtained through the managed care plan.

For children with fee-for-service Medi-Cal, BHT may be coordinated through the local Regional Center. Since July 1, 2025, eligible fee-for-service members under 21 may also choose enrolled Medi-Cal Qualified Autism Service providers rather than going through the Regional Center.

A physician or psychologist determines medical necessity for BHT.

Medi-Cal specialty mental health

California's Specialty Mental Health Services are provided through county Mental Health Plans.

For children and youth who may need specialty mental-health treatment, families can contact their county Mental Health Plan and request an initial assessment. DHCS states that the county plan may not deny the request for an initial assessment to determine eligibility for specialty services.

For patients under 21, Medicaid's EPSDT requirements affect access and medical-necessity standards.

California school-linked behavioral health

California's Children and Youth Behavioral Health Initiative includes school-linked behavioral-health services for students in public schools and colleges.

The state describes services such as therapy and counseling, mental-health and substance-use screening, parent-child sessions, psychoeducation, care coordination/case management, and Certified Wellness Coach support. Current state materials describe these services as free to eligible students, without copays or deductibles.

Availability and the exact access path may vary by school, district, campus, health plan, and local implementation, so use the current DHCS program information when making a referral.

Clinician referral checklist

For a child with developmental concerns, consider whether the family should be referred to Early Start, the local Regional Center, the school district, or more than one system.

For an autistic or developmentally disabled child with Medi-Cal, separate these questions:

What developmental-disability services may be available through DDS/Regional Center?

What behavioral-health treatment is medically necessary and covered through Medi-Cal?

What educational supports are needed through the school?

What specialty mental-health needs should be routed to the county Mental Health Plan?

These systems can overlap, but eligibility for one does not automatically establish eligibility for another.

California service systems change frequently. Verify the current Regional Center, managed-care plan, county Mental Health Plan, school/district pathway, and program eligibility before promising a service or timeline.
