California School Access: IEP, Section 504 & Parent Rights

State-specific companion to the federal and Arizona school-access guides. General educational information, not legal advice. Reviewed September 2026.

The basic rule

A medical or psychological diagnosis can support school planning, but it does not automatically create an IEP or Section 504 plan. The school must consider how the condition affects the student at school and what educational supports are needed.

California IDEA timeline: referral, assessment plan, evaluation

After a referral for special-education assessment

California law requires a proposed assessment plan within 15 days of the referral when an assessment will be conducted. The parent-rights notice accompanies the assessment plan.

Parent review of the assessment plan

The parent has at least 15 days after receiving the proposed assessment plan to decide whether to consent. The initial assessment cannot begin without informed written parental consent.

After written consent

The assessment must be completed and an IEP developed within 60 days of written consent, subject to California's school-session and vacation rules and other lawful exceptions.

Parent-requested IEP meeting

When a parent makes a written request for an IEP meeting to review the IEP, California generally requires the meeting within 30 days, excluding specified school breaks.

Useful California-specific rights

Parents are members of the IEP team and must have an opportunity to participate in decisions about eligibility, assessment, placement, and FAPE.

California permits a parent or the local educational agency to audio-record an IEP team meeting when the other participants are notified at least 24 hours before the meeting.

Parents who disagree with a district assessment may request an Independent Educational Evaluation at public expense, subject to IDEA procedures.

Prior Written Notice should explain what the district proposes or refuses, why, the information used, alternatives considered, and how parents can obtain help understanding their rights.

Section 504 in California

Section 504 remains a federal civil-rights law. California schools must provide FAPE to eligible students under Section 504 even when the student does not qualify under IDEA.

Do not apply the California IDEA 15-day or 60-day timelines automatically to a Section 504 evaluation. Section 504 requires timely evaluation and procedural safeguards, but it does not use California's IDEA assessment-plan structure.

If there is a disagreement

California Department of Education state complaint

A state compliance complaint may address alleged violations of federal or California special-education law. CDE generally investigates a complete complaint within 60 days. The complaint generally must concern a violation occurring no more than one year before CDE receives it.

Mediation and due process

California makes mediation available for disagreements about identification, evaluation, placement, or FAPE. Due-process complaints are handled through the California Office of Administrative Hearings. Different filing deadlines and procedures apply, so families should check the current CDE/OAH information for the specific dispute.

California school system terms worth knowing

SELPA means Special Education Local Plan Area. California organizes special-education planning and some regional services through SELPAs. Families may hear both the school district and SELPA referenced when discussing special education.

California also operates Diagnostic Centers that may provide no-cost consultation and specialized educational assessment support to local educational agencies for students with complex learning or behavioral profiles after local resources have been used.

Practical family steps

Make requests in writing and save copies.

Describe school-observable barriers, not only diagnoses.

Ask what data support the school's conclusion.

Ask who will provide each support, where, how often, and how effectiveness will be monitored.

If the district refuses an evaluation, support, or placement request, ask for the refusal and rationale in writing when procedural notice is required.

Use the school's Section 504 coordinator for 504 questions and the district/SELPA special-education contacts for IDEA questions.

Bullying, neurodiversity, and cyberbullying

California Department of Education maintains current bullying-prevention training and resources, including material specifically addressing bullying of students who are neurodivergent or perceived as neurodivergent.

https://www.cde.ca.gov/ls/ss/se/bullyres.asp

California also published a 2026 model policy addressing certain off-campus cyberbullying when it is sufficiently severe or pervasive to create an intimidating or hostile educational environment. Use the current CDE page and the local LEA’s adopted policy when this is relevant.

https://www.cde.ca.gov/ls/ss/se/cyberbullying.asp

Important limitation

Timelines can be affected by school breaks, transfers, written extensions, year-end referrals, and case-specific procedural facts. Use current CDE guidance, local SELPA information, and qualified legal advice when the exact deadline or dispute procedure matters.
