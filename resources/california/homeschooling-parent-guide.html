Homeschooling in California – Parent Guide

A practical start-to-finish guide for families. General educational information, not legal advice. Reviewed September 2026.

First: choose the California pathway

California has several ways for a child to learn primarily at home.

Public charter school

The student remains a public-school student. The charter school is responsible for public-school requirements, and the student may receive public special-education and Section 504 services through the public system.

Public-school independent study

The student remains enrolled in public school and works under an independent-study structure with school oversight.

Existing private school

Some private schools offer online, independent-study, or home-based programs.

Home-based private school

The parent establishes a private school in the home and meets California private-school requirements, including the annual Private School Affidavit.

Credentialed private tutor

A separate California exemption allows instruction by a person with a valid California teaching credential for the grade taught. This route has its own instructional-time requirements.

Do not assume that every form of learning at home is legally the same.

Home-based private school checklist

1. Establish the home-based private school

Choose a school name and operate as a private full-time day school meeting the California Education Code requirements.

2. File the Private School Affidavit every year

Parents operating a home-based private school must file the Private School Affidavit with the California Department of Education each year.

The statutory filing period is October 1 through October 15. CDE currently keeps the online filing system open from August 1 through June 30 to accommodate new schools and home schools.

A new home school can file after it has an enrolled student. Filing the affidavit does not mean CDE has approved or accredited the school.

3. Complete the compulsory-attendance exemption process

California compulsory full-time education generally applies from ages 6 through 18 unless an exemption applies.

CDE states that filing the affidavit is one part of the exemption. The local public-school district's attendance supervisor or designee verifies that the private school has complied with the affidavit requirements.

CDE's current FAQ states that the family should provide a copy of the affidavit to the local district attendance supervisor for this verification.

4. Provide instruction in the required areas

A home-based private school must provide full-time instruction in English and offer the branches of study required in California public schools to the same general degree of depth.

CDE points families to California Education Code sections 51210 and 51220 and the state content standards and curriculum frameworks as planning resources.

The person teaching in a home-based private school must be capable of teaching. A California teaching credential is not the same requirement as the separate credentialed-tutor pathway.

Required records

California private/home schools must maintain:

Attendance records, including absences of a half day or more

Courses of study offered

Names and addresses of faculty

A record of the educational qualifications of each faculty member

CDE also strongly recommends keeping copies of annual Private School Affidavits and individual pupil records.

For practical purposes, also keep:

Course descriptions

Major curriculum and book lists

Work samples

Grades or mastery records

Outside-course transcripts

Assessment results

Special-education or clinical evaluation records

A high-school transcript

Testing

California does not require private home-school students to take state testing simply because they are homeschooled through a private school.

Families may choose private testing, curriculum-based assessments, or other measures to monitor progress.

A child enrolled in a public charter or public independent-study program is a public-school student, so public-school testing requirements are different.

Special education and disability support

California recognizes home schools as private elementary and secondary schools for IDEA equitable-services purposes.

The responsible local educational agency has Child Find duties for parentally placed private-school children with disabilities, including qualifying homeschool students.

However, a parentally placed private-school child does not have an individual right to receive all of the special-education and related services that would be available through a public-school IEP.

Some children may be selected to receive equitable services through a services plan based on the local proportionate-share process.

Ask the local district or SELPA:

Who is responsible for Child Find for my home school?

How do I request an evaluation?

What equitable services are currently available?

If services are offered, will my child have a services plan?

What would change if my child enrolled in public school?

If the child is enrolled in a public charter or public independent-study program rather than a private home school, the child remains in the public system and IDEA/FAPE responsibilities are different.

High school, credits, transcripts, and diplomas

California does not establish a statewide set of graduation or diploma requirements for private schools. A home-based private school may set its own graduation requirements.

That freedom comes with a practical responsibility: colleges, employers, military programs, technical programs, scholarship programs, and a future public high school may apply their own standards.

CDE specifically warns that a public school may decline to recognize homeschool credits when a student re-enrolls, depending on district placement and credit policies.

For high school, maintain a clear transcript with:

Course title

School year

Credits or units

Grade or mastery result

Outside institution, when applicable

Graduation date

School name and administrator/parent signature

Keep course descriptions for lab sciences, advanced courses, unusual electives, and any course likely to need outside verification.

College planning

Plan backward from likely postsecondary requirements.

For University of California admission, homeschool applicants may apply, but the path differs depending on whether the homeschool curriculum comes from an accredited school and whether the student receives an official diploma/transcript from that school. UC also describes admission by exception for some students who do not meet traditional admission requirements.

Do not wait until senior year to discover that a desired university expects specific coursework, documentation, or laboratory science evidence.

Socialization and community participation

A homeschool schedule should include opportunities to practice relationships, collaboration, independence, and community participation.

Useful settings can include:

Community college or enrichment classes

Libraries

Sports and recreation

Arts and music

Volunteer work

Faith communities

Homeschool groups or co-ops

Clubs

Part-time work for older teens

Community organizations

Quality matters more than recreating a conventional school day.

Homeschooling and mental health or neurodevelopmental needs

A child may function better outside a stressful school environment and still need treatment or skill building.

Continue working on communication, self-advocacy, flexibility, executive functioning, daily living skills, frustration tolerance, and age-appropriate independence where needed.

If the child was removed from school because of bullying, anxiety, sensory overload, or school refusal, make sure the homeschool plan does not accidentally turn avoidance into long-term isolation.

A simple weekly planning model

Choose a manageable set of academic priorities.

Use California standards as a reference, not as a daily checklist.

Build a predictable rhythm without copying a seven-hour school day.

Use shorter teaching blocks when attention or processing speed requires it.

Mix direct instruction, independent work, projects, reading, movement, and real-world learning.

Keep one record system for attendance, work, and progress.

Review progress every few weeks and adjust.

Credentialed tutor route

California also allows a private-tutor exemption when the instructor holds a valid California credential for the grade taught.

Under the current statute, tutoring under this exemption is provided for at least three hours a day for 175 days each calendar year, between 8:00 a.m. and 4:00 p.m., in the required branches of study and in English.

This is a different legal route from operating a home-based private school through a PSA.

Important limitation

California homeschool law depends on the educational pathway a family chooses. Charter school, independent study, private home school, private-school satellite program, and credentialed tutoring are not interchangeable. Verify current requirements with CDE, the local district, SELPA, college, or qualified legal adviser when the exact status matters.
