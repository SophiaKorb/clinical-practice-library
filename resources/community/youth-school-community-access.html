Youth, school and community support: an access map

Turn a broad referral into one practical next step that the young person chooses. This works for LGBTQ+ youth and transition-age young adults navigating school, services, work or community.

1. Find the immediate question

  • Offer a few directions and a real “something else” option: school safety, peers, caregiver connection, health support, education/work, housing, transportation, or identity-document questions.
  • Ask whom the young person wants involved. Supported communication, interpreters, and accessible materials may be necessary for meaningful participation.
  • Do not assume a single meaning of independence or that every youth wants the same transition goal.

2. Find a genuinely suitable pathway

  • For school belonging and peer connection, consider student-led alliances, a trusted school sponsor, or a local youth community group. CDC associates supportive school practices with improved connectedness.
  • For employment, postsecondary education, disability access, and healthcare transitions, use the previously approved Southern Arizona Youth & Transition-Age guide as the referral starting point.
  • For identity-document questions, offer current provider navigation; verify actual state and federal issuing-authority requirements independently before advising action.

3. Verify conditions before the referral

  • Ask the program for current age eligibility, registration, confidentiality, costs, hours, transportation, accessibility and whether a caregiver or school referral is required.
  • Do not infer eligibility from the term “transition-age.” School status, foster-care history, disability program rules and health coverage each have separate criteria.
  • For online communities, confirm moderation, age range and personal-data/privacy practices.

4. Make a youth-led handoff

  • Choose one next step: look at an event calendar together, write an email, make a call with consent, schedule an intake, or simply save a link for private review.
  • Agree on who will do what and how the youth will know whether the resource worked. Record permission and any restrictions on further outreach.
  • Afterward, check whether access actually happened and whether the person found it useful.
