Caregiver support that protects the young person's agency

A concrete, noncoercive approach to improving support at home—even when caregivers are unsure how to begin.

1. Focus on behaviors that can change

  • Listen respectfully. Express love and connection. Protect private information. Interrupt threats, ridicule, coercion or exclusion.
  • Ask the young person what support feels helpful. Do not require disclosure to other relatives or a specific identity label in exchange for care.
  • Family Acceptance Project resources describe family accepting and rejecting behaviors as modifiable actions associated with youth wellbeing.

2. Keep adult questions in an adult space

  • Caregivers can seek their own trusted education or consultation without asking the young person to absorb adult anxiety or justify their existence.
  • Separate respectful questions from repeated interrogation. Repair mistakes briefly rather than asking the child to console adults.
  • Useful first actions include using requested terms in agreed settings, welcoming friends, identifying a safe relative, or correcting a family member's mistreatment.

3. Choose when to meet together

  • Joint sessions should not automatically be the first step. Ask youth about willingness, realistic privacy, safety and goals.
  • When family conflict includes intimidation or threats, prioritize safety and individual support rather than enforcing a “balanced” confrontation.
  • Consult applicable law, guardianship rules and supervision regarding records, consent and information sharing.

4. Try a small experiment and review

  • Agree on one observable change for the week—for example, a caregiver listens without interrupting, a family establishes privacy rules, or a supportive adult accompanies the young person to a chosen event.
  • Ask both the youth and caregiver what happened, but give the young person's account of safety and autonomy particular attention.
  • Measure support by concrete interactions, connection and everyday wellbeing—not compelled agreement or disclosure.
