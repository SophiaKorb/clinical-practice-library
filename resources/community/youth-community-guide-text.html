Three decisions before making a referral
School status matters: Before withdrawal, GED/HSE, or diploma decisions, ask the IEP/transition team how the
change affects eligibility for remaining school services. A high-school diploma, age limit, and withdrawal are not
interchangeable.

Age ranges are program-specific: “Transition-age” is a navigation label, not a single legal eligibility category.
Verify age, school status, county, disability documentation, insurance, transportation, and enrollment before
promising a place.

1 Education, training & access
High-school IEP / transition planning team
PUBLIC SCHOOL | In-school youth; determine district service eligibility

What it does: Education, workplace goals, self-determination, assistive technology and community participation
goals within the IEP.

First step: Request the transition goals, graduation/exit plan, age-of-majority discussion, and introductions to
VR/DDD when appropriate.

Watch for: Do not withdraw for a GED without written school-team clarification about remaining services.
Official or program source

Project FOCUS — University of Arizona
SCHOOL-PARTNER TRANSITION PROGRAM | 18–21; I/DD and partner-school conditions

What it does: Inclusive university-based academics, internships, campus experience, and independence skills.

First step: Ask the current school transition team and Project FOCUS to check eligibility, school partnership, fees,
application timing, and student choice.

Watch for: Not open UArizona admission; Fall 2027 application shown with October 23, 2026 deadline already
past at review date. Confirm next intake.
Official or program source

PUBLIC COLLEGE | Program-specific

Pima Community College — Access & Disability Resources (ADR)
COLLEGE ACCESSIBILITY | Students and community members accessing PCC

What it does: Interactive accommodation process for disability-related barriers in PCC classes, activities or
assessments.

First step: Email ADRhelp@pima.edu or call 520-206-6688; ask to start the ADR request process.

Watch for: IEP/504 accommodations do not automatically transfer to college.
Official or program source

Pima Community College — TRiO SOAR
SELECTIVE COLLEGE STUDENT SUPPORT | PCC students meeting federal TRiO and program criteria

What it does: Intensive coaching, tutoring, academic/financial-aid and transfer support; distinct from ADR
accommodations.

First step: Review eligibility; call 520-206-7625 or email trio-soar@pima.edu.

Watch for: U.S. citizen/permanent resident and documented disability are required; program is not general
drop-in counseling.

UNIVERSITY ACCESSIBILITY | UArizona students

What it does: Provides an interactive accommodations process for coursework, campus activities and housing
access.

First step: Complete DRC affiliation or contact disability@arizona.edu to explore barriers and options.

Watch for: Campus belonging programs are separate from formal accommodation approvals.
Official or program source

2 Employment, earnings & financial navigation
Pima County ARIZONA@WORK Youth Employment Center
COUNTY WORKFORCE | 14–24; funded programs have separate criteria

What it does: Career exploration, resume/interview help, GED connection, paid work experience, and training
navigation.

First step: Contact 520-724-9649; ask for youth intake and accessible participation options.

Watch for: Starter document listed 520-724-9640; current Pima County page says 520-724-9649. Confirm actual
visit address.
Official or program source

Arizona Pre-Employment Transition Services (Pre-ETS)
STATE PUBLIC VR | 14–22 with documented disability; currently enrolled in education

What it does: Job exploration, work experiences, training options, job readiness and self-advocacy workshops.

First step: Use DES Pre-ETS provider directory and request form; school team can help.

Watch for: Students do NOT need to be enrolled as VR clients to obtain Pre-ETS.
Official or program source

Arizona Vocational Rehabilitation (VR)
STATE DISABILITY EMPLOYMENT | Eligibility based on disability-related employment barriers

What it does: Individualized disability-related preparation, job placement, accommodation and employment
retention supports.

First step: Request a VR referral through DES; explain desired work, barriers and current supports.

Watch for: Pre-ETS and a full VR program referral have different entry requirements.
Official or program source

Beacon Group
TUCSON DISABILITY EMPLOYMENT | Service/referral-specific; ask about youth transition services

What it does: Supported employment, skills training, job coaching, customized job development and transition
services.

First step: Call Tucson office 520-622-4874; ask which referral source and funding pathway apply.

Watch for: Tucson office: 308 W. Glenn St. Participation in specific job-placement programs can require VR, DDD,
AHCCCS/ALTCS or Ticket to Work.
Official or program source

DDD employment services
ARIZONA STATE DISABILITY SUPPORT | Eligible DDD members

What it does: Supported employment, job support aide and individualized pathways to integrated work.

First step: Ask the DDD Support Coordinator to discuss employment outcomes and coordination with VR.

Watch for: DDD eligibility and authorization are distinct from VR eligibility.
Official or program source

Benefits 2 Work / Arizona Disability Benefits 101
BENEFITS & WORK INCENTIVES | People with disability benefits considering work

What it does: Individualized benefits counseling on how earnings may affect SSI, SSDI, AHCCCS and other
programs.

First step: Call 1-866-304-WORK (9675); review DB101 and ask about a Work Incentive Consultant.

Watch for: Do not advise a young person to stop at a generic earnings limit; case-specific rules vary.
Official or program source

AZ ABLE
DISABILITY SAVINGS | AZ residents whose qualifying disability began before age 46

What it does: Person-owned qualified disability savings account that may protect access to means-tested
benefits.

First step: Review account rules, beneficiary decision-making and eligibility with AZ ABLE.

Watch for: A savings account does not replace individualized benefits or legal advice.
Official or program source

Autism Society of Southern Arizona — Navigating Autism
RESOURCE NAVIGATION | Autistic people of all ages

What it does: Connects autistic people and families to local providers, adult services and community supports.

First step: Call 520-770-1541 or use the Navigating Autism resource list with two concrete priorities.

Watch for: A referral navigator is not the same as a clinical assessment or guaranteed service slot.
Official or program source

Autism Society — Adult Social Club
PEER COMMUNITY | 18+ autistic adults

What it does: In-person and virtual peer connection, participant-choice games, discussions and social activities.

First step: See the live calendar / contact info@as-az.org before attending.

Watch for: Community group rather than social skills treatment; participation is voluntary.
Official or program source

UArizona Omnes / Disability Cultural Center
BELONGING AND CAMPUS COMMUNITY | UArizona campus community; event-specific

What it does: Disability culture, identity, peer community and events, separate from formal accommodations.

First step: Check current campus hours and program details before visiting; use UArizona DRC for
accommodation requests.

Watch for: Original starter identifies Omnes space; specific current events and community access unverified.
Official or program source


4 Housing, safety & basic needs
Youth On Their Own (YOTO)
EDUCATION + HOUSING INSTABILITY | Pima grades 6–12; under 22 for student program; alumni up to 24

What it does: Financial and material support for students who are homeless and unaccompanied through no
fault/choice of their own.

First step: Start through a school YOTO liaison or YOTO online application.

Watch for: Not a general housing program for all youth living with parents.
Official or program source

TPCH — Youth Need Help Directory / Coordinated Entry
HOUSING ACCESS | Homeless or imminently homeless youth; program criteria differ

What it does: Directory of Pima County youth housing, shelter, education, food and outreach; coordinated
assessment for housing referrals.

First step: Use TPCH directory; seek the nearest current coordinated-entry access point for housing assessment.

Watch for: No guarantee of immediate housing; emergency danger requires emergency services.
Official or program source

5 Behavioral health, medical & foster-care continuity
AHCCCS — Transition-Age Youth resources
STATE BEHAVIORAL HEALTH | Especially 14–21 planning; depends on member plan

What it does: Guidance for building adult-directed treatment plans, care-team continuity and benefit transitions.

First step: Ask the current health-plan/care team to document adult transition, goals, consent and next contact.

Watch for: Behavioral-health planning generally starts around 16; a member may request CFT continuity until
age 21.
Official or program source

Teen Lifeline
YOUTH CRISIS / PEER SUPPORT | Teens; families concerned for teens can call

What it does: 24/7 crisis counseling with teen peer counseling/text service available 3–9 p.m. Arizona time.

First step: Southern Arizona call 520-327-8336; text 602-248-8336; if there is immediate physical danger call 911.

Watch for: 988 is also available nationwide for mental-health and suicide crises.
Official or program source

Pima “You Are Not Alone” youth initiative
YOUTH MENTAL HEALTH | Pima County ages 24 and under

What it does: County-described youth mental-health access with several no-cost therapy sessions and skill-
building supports.

First step: Use the county-listed website / referral form and verify current eligibility and availability.

Watch for: County directory lists five free sessions; direct current program availability not independently

Official or program source

AHCCCS Young Adult Transitional Insurance (YATI)
FOSTER-CARE MEDICAL COVERAGE | Eligible former foster youth ages 18–26

What it does: Arizona health coverage for qualifying young adults formerly in foster care; not income-tested.

First step: Review AHCCCS eligibility and apply/check coverage if in qualifying foster care and AHCCCS-covered
on 18th birthday.

Watch for: Requires specific foster-care and Medicaid status on 18th birthday; not automatic for all youth.
Official or program source

Arizona DCS — voluntary extended foster care
FOSTER-CARE TRANSITION | Former foster youth; generally through 21

What it does: Voluntary continuation of certain foster-care services, supports and case management after 18 for
eligible youth.

First step: Discuss eligibility, current program rules and reentry questions with DCS caseworker/transition
specialist.

Watch for: Do not conflate foster-care extension through 21 with AHCCCS YATI health coverage through 26.
Official or program source

Arizona Youth Partnership — Starting Out Right
PREGNANT/PARENTING YOUTH | Young parents through age 21 per county resource index

What it does: Health, parenting education, and navigation for pregnant or parenting adolescents.

First step: Confirm current regional intake directly with the provider.

Watch for: Service summary comes from county index; provider-level currency and intake availability need
direct confirmation.
Official or program source

Source and review method
This edition reconstructs and extends the August 21, 2026
Southern_Arizona_Transition_Resources_Starter.docx. Direct program/government links appear under each
resource. Sources were reviewed on October 25, 2026 from public records; this is website-level evidence,
not a verified telephone intake, signed funding authorization, or a guarantee of program capacity. The
county Restorative Justice dashboard is an index and may contain older or duplicate listings. Service
availability and schedules change. Gender-specific, peer and identity-based groups are optional choices, not
universal referrals.

Unresolved internal pathway: the original included an in-house vocational services section with blank
contact and referral fields. That pathway is deliberately NOT presented as an established external
referral. Confirm the actual internal program with the relevant organization before use.
