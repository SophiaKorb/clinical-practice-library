DEVELOPMENTAL DISABILITY, AUTISM & BEHAVIORAL HEALTH – ARIZONA

State-specific guide for clinicians, families, and support staff. Reviewed September 2026.

Division of Developmental Disabilities (DDD)

Arizona’s Division of Developmental Disabilities supports eligible Arizona residents with qualifying developmental disabilities and significant functional needs.

For people age 6 and older, qualifying diagnoses include:

  • Autism Spectrum Disorder
  • Cerebral palsy
  • Down syndrome
  • Epilepsy
  • Intellectual disability

For age 6 through adulthood, the qualifying developmental disability must have begun before age 18, be likely to continue, and be associated with serious limitations in daily-life skills. Arizona DDD currently describes eligibility as requiring significant limitations in at least three areas such as self-care, receptive/expressive language, learning, self-direction, mobility, capacity for independent living, or economic self-sufficiency.

A diagnosis by itself does not guarantee DDD eligibility. Functional limitations and DDD’s documentation requirements still matter.

Children under age 6

Arizona uses broader developmental criteria for young children. Children under 6 may qualify based on an eligible diagnosis, risk for an eligible developmental disability, or qualifying developmental delay.

For ages 3 to 6, current DDD guidance includes a 50% delay in one developmental area or a 25% delay in two or more developmental areas as possible eligibility pathways.

DDD redetermination

DDD currently redetermines eligibility around the sixth and eighteenth birthdays. Families should plan ahead for updated medical, educational, adaptive-functioning, and other records when those transitions approach.

Arizona Early Intervention Program (AzEIP)

AzEIP is Arizona’s statewide IDEA Part C early-intervention system for infants and toddlers with disabilities or developmental delays.

Families can make a referral directly. Current DES information provides an online referral pathway and the AzEIP Central Referral number.

For a child from birth to age 3 with developmental concerns, AzEIP and DDD may both be relevant. DDD guidance suggests an AzEIP referral when a child is suspected of having a disability or delay, while families may also apply to DDD.

AHCCCS autism and behavioral-health services

AHCCCS maintains a current autism-services page with family and provider information on Autism Spectrum Disorder and Applied Behavior Analysis, including 2026 ABA policy updates.

For an AHCCCS-enrolled child, separate these questions:

  • What developmental-disability supports may be available through DDD?
  • What medically necessary physical or behavioral-health services are covered through the child’s AHCCCS health plan?
  • What educational supports are needed through the school?
  • Does the child need ABA or another behavioral-health treatment, and what current AHCCCS policy applies?
  • Are school-based behavioral-health services available locally?

Behavioral health in schools

AHCCCS covers medically necessary behavioral-health services for Medicaid-enrolled students, and Arizona has a statewide school behavioral-health initiative that supports access to services on school campuses.

School-based behavioral-health treatment is separate from special-education or Section 504 eligibility. A student may need one, the other, or both.

Clinician referral checklist

For a child with developmental concerns, consider whether the family should contact:

  • AzEIP, for an infant or toddler;
  • DDD, for developmental-disability eligibility and long-term supports;
  • the public school district, for Child Find, evaluation, IEP, or Section 504 questions;
  • the AHCCCS health plan, for medically necessary behavioral-health or autism treatment;
  • vocational rehabilitation or transition services for an older adolescent or adult when relevant.

Do not assume eligibility in one system establishes eligibility in another. DDD, AHCCCS, and school systems use different legal and functional criteria.
