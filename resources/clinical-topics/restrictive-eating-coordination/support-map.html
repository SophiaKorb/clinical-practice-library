Restrictive eating: medical coordination and supported participation — Visual support map

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Adults · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Restriction, missed meals, compensatory behavior or difficulty eating can occur across different presentations and body sizes. Weight alone cannot establish severity or medical stability. Ask about intake, change, symptoms, beliefs, access and function.

How the parts relate

Eating or intake has become difficult

Notice changes in eating, physical health, access and daily life. Do not infer cause or medical stability from appearance or body size.

TWO TRACKS — ASSESS IN PARALLELCheck medical safety now; explore possible causes and access needs alongside it.

Medical and nutritional safety

Assess physical stability, nutritional concerns, symptoms and possible medical contributors with qualified care.

Urgent concerns? Act without waiting.

Fainting, severe weakness, dehydration, chest symptoms or rapid deterioration require prompt medical assessment, or emergency care when indicated. Do not wait for the other track.

Psychological, sensory and access understanding

Explore fear, eating-disorder concerns, sensory differences, swallowing difficulties, food availability, culture and the person's account.

Keep multiple explanations open. This assessment never delays necessary medical care.

One coordinated plan — as findings become available

Agree who manages medical follow-up, specialist assessment, accommodations and chosen supports. Review both medical safety and everyday participation; adjust the plan as needed.

Both tracks can proceed together, but medical escalation does not depend on finishing a psychological or sensory explanation. Original discussion aid; not a validated risk screen or refeeding protocol.

Keep medical safety, the person's experience, and practical access needs visible at the same time. Agree a team contact and review plan without waiting for a complete explanation.

Clinician layer: what the map could miss

Eating-disorder process

Explore restriction, weight/shape beliefs, compensation, distress and history with trained assessment.

Sensory, fear or medical barrier

Texture, swallowing fear, gastrointestinal symptoms and other health concerns need appropriate specialty evaluation, not a presumed motive.

Food/access context

Money, safe food availability, cultural needs, disability and living conditions can constrain eating independently of symptoms.

Keep medical/nutritional risk assessment distinct from psychological formulation. Fear of weight gain, sensory or fear-of-consequence avoidance, low appetite, gastrointestinal illness, food insecurity and medication effects can require different pathways. NG69 is not a complete ARFID pathway. This set helps coordinate qualified care and participation; it offers no calorie targets, weight thresholds, refeeding instructions or self-directed meal prescription.

Worked example

Fictional case: Taylor has substantially reduced intake and feels dizzy at work. A colleague assumes they are medically safe because their body size does not match a stereotype of an eating disorder. Taylor reports nausea and fear of vomiting, not yet assessed weight/shape concerns, and cannot afford several suggested foods. They want help but fear being judged. Choose a coordination step.

Best supported: address physical symptoms and multiple explanations, confirm ownership, and preserve engagement.

Safety and scope

Fainting, severe weakness, dehydration, chest symptoms, rapid deterioration or other signs of medical instability require prompt medical assessment. Any body size can involve serious risk. Do not give refeeding or purging-management instructions from this tool.

Use these tools for communication, observation and coordination within your role. Follow your training, supervision and agency requirements for diagnosis and treatment. For a crisis, use local emergency services.
