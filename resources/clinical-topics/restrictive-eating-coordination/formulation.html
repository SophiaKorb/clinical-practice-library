Restrictive eating: medical coordination and supported participation — Clinician formulation guide

Adults · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Restriction, missed meals, compensatory behavior or difficulty eating can occur across different presentations and body sizes. Weight alone cannot establish severity or medical stability. Ask about intake, change, symptoms, beliefs, access and function.

Understanding the pattern

Keep medical/nutritional risk assessment distinct from psychological formulation. Fear of weight gain, sensory or fear-of-consequence avoidance, low appetite, gastrointestinal illness, food insecurity and medication effects can require different pathways. NG69 is not a complete ARFID pathway. This set helps coordinate qualified care and participation; it offers no calorie targets, weight thresholds, refeeding instructions or self-directed meal prescription.

Keep competing explanations open

Eating-disorder process

Explore restriction, weight/shape beliefs, compensation, distress and history with trained assessment.

Sensory, fear or medical barrier

Texture, swallowing fear, gastrointestinal symptoms and other health concerns need appropriate specialty evaluation, not a presumed motive.

Food/access context

Money, safe food availability, cultural needs, disability and living conditions can constrain eating independently of symptoms.

Ask the person

  • What makes eating difficult: availability, sensations, fear, appetite, beliefs or something else?
  • What has changed in intake or daily functioning?
  • What physical symptoms have you noticed, and who is evaluating them?
  • What help feels acceptable, and what has felt coercive or unhelpful?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Arrange coordinated assessment

Confirm who owns medical, nutritional and psychological assessment and how concerns reach them. Do not wait for a worksheet to establish stability.

Support the agreed clinical plan

Staff/care partners help with appointments, access and communication within role. Specialist clinicians determine treatment/nutritional management.

Review barriers and consent

Ask whether the plan can be used in real life and whether fear, overload, cost or shame is preventing participation.

Safety and scope

Fainting, severe weakness, dehydration, chest symptoms, rapid deterioration or other signs of medical instability require prompt medical assessment. Any body size can involve serious risk. Do not give refeeding or purging-management instructions from this tool.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
