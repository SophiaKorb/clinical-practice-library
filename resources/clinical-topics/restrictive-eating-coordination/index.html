Restrictive eating: medical coordination and supported participation

Adults · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Restriction, missed meals, compensatory behavior or difficulty eating can occur across different presentations and body sizes. Weight alone cannot establish severity or medical stability. Ask about intake, change, symptoms, beliefs, access and function.

Keep medical/nutritional risk assessment distinct from psychological formulation. Fear of weight gain, sensory or fear-of-consequence avoidance, low appetite, gastrointestinal illness, food insecurity and medication effects can require different pathways. NG69 is not a complete ARFID pathway. This set helps coordinate qualified care and participation; it offers no calorie targets, weight thresholds, refeeding instructions or self-directed meal prescription.

Practical pathway

  1. Reported difficultyIntake, symptoms, access
  2. Medical assessmentStability and contributors
  3. Clinical formulationBeliefs, fears, sensory factors
  4. Agreed team planRoles, communication, supports
  5. Participation reviewFeasibility, burden, deterioration

Medical stability and psychological or sensory formulation need parallel attention. Do not wait for a completed behavioral explanation before urgent medical assessment; one team coordinates the plan.

Safety and scope

Fainting, severe weakness, dehydration, chest symptoms, rapid deterioration or other signs of medical instability require prompt medical assessment. Any body size can involve serious risk. Do not give refeeding or purging-management instructions from this tool.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
