Restrictive eating: medical coordination and supported participation

Adults · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Restriction, missed meals, compensatory behavior or difficulty eating can occur across different presentations and body sizes. Weight alone cannot establish severity or medical stability. Ask about intake, change, symptoms, beliefs, access and function.

Keep medical/nutritional risk assessment distinct from psychological formulation. Fear of weight gain, sensory or fear-of-consequence avoidance, low appetite, gastrointestinal illness, food insecurity and medication effects can require different pathways. NG69 is not a complete ARFID pathway. This set helps coordinate qualified care and participation; it offers no calorie targets, weight thresholds, refeeding instructions or self-directed meal prescription.

Practical pathway

Eating or intake has become difficult

Notice changes in intake, health, comfort, access, and daily participation. Do not infer cause from appearance or weight.

START BOTH TRACKS TOGETHERMedical safety must not wait for a behavioral explanation.

TRACK A · Medical and nutritional safety

Assess medical stability, physical symptoms, nutrition concerns, and possible medical contributors with the qualified care team.

Urgent warning signs?

Fainting, dehydration, severe weakness, chest symptoms, or rapid deterioration: arrange prompt or emergency medical assessment as indicated. Do not wait for Track B.

TRACK B · Psychological, sensory and access understanding

Explore fear, eating-disorder concerns, sensory experience, swallowing difficulties, food access, culture, and the person's account. Assess collaboratively alongside Track A.

Not a prerequisite for medical care. The explanation can remain uncertain while safety needs are addressed.

ONE coordinated, person-centered plan

Share findings across the team; agree who handles medical follow-up, accommodations, appropriate specialist care, and supports the person accepts.

Review both tracks

Recheck medical stability and nutrition concerns as well as participation, feasibility, burden, deterioration, and what the person wants changed.

These assessments overlap in time—not two steps in a queue. Medical instability is assessed and addressed immediately, even if the psychological or sensory formulation is unfinished. This map is not a medical triage or refeeding protocol.

Safety and scope

Fainting, severe weakness, dehydration, chest symptoms, rapid deterioration or other signs of medical instability require prompt medical assessment. Any body size can involve serious risk. Do not give refeeding or purging-management instructions from this tool.

Use these tools for communication, observation and coordination within your role. Follow your training, supervision and agency requirements for diagnosis and treatment. For a crisis, use local emergency services.
