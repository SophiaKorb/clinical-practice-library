Child aggression: safety, function and caregiver coaching — Care-partner guide

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · School-age children and caregivers

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Describe neutrally

Avoid "manipulative" or "attention seeking" as established facts.

Check health and access

Try: "Could pain, understanding or communication be making this hard?"

Practice alternatives outside crisis

Use the trained plan and accessible communication, not demands for a new skill at peak distress.

Coordinate reliable care

Record what occurred and confirm follow-up with appropriate clinical/school teams.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Separate safe response during an event from prevention, skill teaching and formal assessment. ABC observations generate hypotheses; a qualified team conducts comprehensive FBA where indicated. Pain, sleep, language access, trauma, disability and coercive interactions may matter. Support plans should teach accessible alternatives and change antecedents/consequences appropriately, without provoking aggression, using humiliation or withholding basic needs. Caregiver coaching models vary in age, indication and required training.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Injury, weapons, abuse, imminent harm or inability to maintain safety requires urgent/local safeguarding response. Do not provoke unsafe behavior to test function, improvise restraint or use food/sleep deprivation or humiliation.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
