Child aggression: safety, function and caregiver coaching

School-age children and caregivers · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Hitting, throwing or threatening behavior needs a clear description, immediate safety response and a broader assessment of communication, health, demands, relationships and learning history. One event does not prove a function, diagnosis or parenting cause.

Separate safe response during an event from prevention, skill teaching and formal assessment. ABC observations generate hypotheses; a qualified team conducts comprehensive FBA where indicated. Pain, sleep, language access, trauma, disability and coercive interactions may matter. Support plans should teach accessible alternatives and change antecedents/consequences appropriately, without provoking aggression, using humiliation or withholding basic needs. Caregiver coaching models vary in age, indication and required training.

Practical pathway

  1. BeforeDemand, access, health, context
  2. Observed actionCamera-level description
  3. AfterResponses and immediate change
  4. Competing explanationsPain, skill, access, learning
  5. Support + skillSafer alternative and review

Before, observed action and after are descriptive observations. Compare explanations before selecting a safer support and skill; one ABC event does not establish behavioral function.

Safety and scope

Injury, weapons, abuse, imminent harm or inability to maintain safety requires urgent/local safeguarding response. Do not provoke unsafe behavior to test function, improvise restraint or use food/sleep deprivation or humiliation.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
