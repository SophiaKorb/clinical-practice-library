Child aggression: safety, function and caregiver coaching — Clinician formulation guide

School-age children and caregivers · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Hitting, throwing or threatening behavior needs a clear description, immediate safety response and a broader assessment of communication, health, demands, relationships and learning history. One event does not prove a function, diagnosis or parenting cause.

Understanding the pattern

Separate safe response during an event from prevention, skill teaching and formal assessment. ABC observations generate hypotheses; a qualified team conducts comprehensive FBA where indicated. Pain, sleep, language access, trauma, disability and coercive interactions may matter. Support plans should teach accessible alternatives and change antecedents/consequences appropriately, without provoking aggression, using humiliation or withholding basic needs. Caregiver coaching models vary in age, indication and required training.

Keep competing explanations open

Access/health/demand

The child may be overwhelmed, in pain, unable to understand or unable to communicate a need.

Skills and learning history

Patterns across multiple contexts may suggest skills/support needs and maintaining contingencies, not deliberate badness.

Relationships/safety

Trauma, abuse, bullying or coercion require protection and specialist assessment rather than a compliance target.

Ask the person or caregiver

  • What exactly happened before, during and after, without motive labels?
  • What could the child understand and communicate at that moment?
  • Any pain, sleep, health or relationship changes?
  • What safer way to ask for help/break or manage the task could be taught and supported?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Agree a safe response pathway

Protect people using local trained procedures; reduce escalation and seek urgent help as needed. No restraint instructions here.

Change one plausible barrier

Trial clearer directions, communication access, task adaptation or predictable support, with the child involved.

Teach and review with qualified care

Use trained caregiver/behavioral intervention suited to assessment. Track alternative skills and wellbeing, not only absence of aggression.

Safety and scope

Injury, weapons, abuse, imminent harm or inability to maintain safety requires urgent/local safeguarding response. Do not provoke unsafe behavior to test function, improvise restraint or use food/sleep deprivation or humiliation.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
