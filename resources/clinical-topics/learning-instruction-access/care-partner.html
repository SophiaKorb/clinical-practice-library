Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · School-age children and school teams

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Separate effort from skill

Avoid laziness labels or assuming an accommodation lowers expectations.

Ask for the two tracks

Try: "What is being taught, and what access support is provided while learning?"

Check implementation

Record whether agreed supports occurred before judging their effectiveness.

Use qualified local processes

Coordinate evaluation and school planning; do not infer eligibility from this worksheet.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Define the learning target before selecting a support. If independent decoding is the target, reading the item aloud changes what is measured; if science knowledge is the target, accessible text may help reveal it. Review instruction quality/opportunity, language, hearing/vision, attendance, development and standardized assessment limits. Response to a support informs planning but does not alone diagnose a learning disorder or establish legal eligibility.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

School avoidance with severe distress, bullying, abuse or safety concerns needs timely response. Legal eligibility and test-score interpretation require qualified local procedures; this tool does not determine either.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
