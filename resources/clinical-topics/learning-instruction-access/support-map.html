A student may understand a concept but struggle to read, spell, calculate, organize or express it in the required format. They may also lack an underlying skill that needs explicit instruction. These questions must remain separate rather than choosing either accommodations or teaching.

How the parts relate

  1. Learning targetSkill or knowledge being measured
  2. Barrier evidenceWhat prevents demonstration?
  3. Instruction trackTeach the identified skill
  4. Access trackChange format/demand appropriately
  5. Review bothLearning + participation + delivery

Instruction and access run in parallel. Review skill learning and knowledge demonstration separately.

This map organizes a conversation; it does not establish causation or calculate risk. Follow the sequence for one event, then return to context and competing explanations. The arrows show a proposed relationship to examine, not proof.

Clinician layer: what the map could miss

Skill needing instruction

Direct evidence about the target skill helps specify teaching rather than broadly reducing work.

Output/access barrier

Format, motor demands, language or memory load may hide knowledge and require access changes.

Opportunity/context

Attendance, instruction, multilingual development and health can explain or coexist with a learning difference.

Define the learning target before selecting a support. If independent decoding is the target, reading the item aloud changes what is measured; if science knowledge is the target, accessible text may help reveal it. Review instruction quality/opportunity, language, hearing/vision, attendance, development and standardized assessment limits. Response to a support informs planning but does not alone diagnose a learning disorder or establish legal eligibility.

Worked example

Fictional case: Mina understands a science concept orally but performs poorly on a written quiz with dense text. Separate reading assessment also shows decoding difficulty. The team proposes only shorter science worksheets. Mina wants to learn to read better and show what they know now. Choose a plan that preserves both learning and access.

Best supported: keep the constructs clear and track delivery, learning and participation independently.

Safety and scope

School avoidance with severe distress, bullying, abuse or safety concerns needs timely response. Legal eligibility and test-score interpretation require qualified local procedures; this tool does not determine either.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
