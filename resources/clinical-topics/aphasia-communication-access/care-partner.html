Acquired aphasia: supported communication and participation — Care-partner guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · Adults with acquired aphasia

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Address the adult directly

Use adult tone and respect. Ask how you can help rather than answering automatically.

Allow time

Do not rush, finish every sentence or test the person repeatedly.

Verify respectfully

Try: "I think you chose Tuesday. Is that right, or did I misunderstand?" Use the recommended response mode.

Coordinate access

Bring preferred aids and relay what worked to SLP/team with permission.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Work with speech-language pathology and the person's preferred communication modes. Hearing, vision, fatigue, motor speech, attention, language history and context can affect access. Offer short messages, key words, pictures, gesture or choices as appropriate, and verify meaning rather than assuming yes/no reliability. Adapted standardized testing may no longer support standard score interpretation. This is an access-support aid for acquired aphasia, not a child language-development or dementia protocol.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

New or sudden language difficulty, weakness, facial changes or other acute neurological signs require emergency medical response. Do not use this tool to assess stroke, consent validity or decisional capacity independently.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
