Sibling conflict: power, safety and repair — Visual support map

School-age children and caregivers · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Arguments, competition and hurt between siblings vary in meaning and severity. First distinguish ordinary conflict from intimidation, repeated targeted harm, coercion, sexual harm or abuse. A joint repair exercise is unsuitable when a child cannot safely refuse.

How the parts relate

  1. EventWhat happened; whose account?
  2. Power + choiceCan each child leave or refuse?
  3. Safety decisionProtection/safeguarding or ordinary coaching
  4. Adult responsibilityResources, access, follow-through
  5. Voluntary repair if safeSpecific action; no forced forgiveness

Safety and freedom to decline determine the route. Protective adult action is not contingent on agreeing to repair; voluntary repair is considered only after safety is established.

Safety or free choice is absent

Protect the child, assess immediate risks and follow appropriate safeguarding pathways. Do not require joint mediation, apology or repair.

Both children are safe and may freely decline

Adults can coach concrete skills and offer a voluntary, supported repair option. A child may refuse without penalty.

This map organizes a conversation; it does not establish causation or calculate risk. Follow the sequence for one event, then return to context and competing explanations. The arrows show a proposed relationship to examine, not proof.

Clinician layer: what the map could miss

Ordinary resource/skill conflict

Turn taking, shared space or communication skills may need clearer structure and coaching.

Unequal power or targeted harm

Fear, threats, injury or inability to refuse requires protection and safeguarding evaluation, not mutual blame.

Family/context needs

Stress, disability access, adult responses and inconsistent resources can shape recurring patterns.

Assess age/development, size/power, frequency, fear, injuries, access to adults and whether each child has real choice. Do not assume equal responsibility because two children are involved. Caregivers need to provide protection and fair access to resources; children should not manage an adult safety failure. Once safe and appropriate, separate accounts, concrete skill coaching and voluntary repair can address ordinary conflict.

Worked example

Fictional case: An older sibling repeatedly blocks a younger child from leaving a room and threatens to break their belongings if they tell. An adult proposes a shared apology because "both need to get along." The younger child is frightened and cannot freely decline the exercise. Choose the next adult action.

Best supported: safety and safeguarding precede any voluntary repair; do not presume equal responsibility.

Safety and scope

Injury, threats, coercion, sexual harm, abuse or a child unable to stay safe requires appropriate urgent/safeguarding response. Do not use equal blame, forced forgiveness or mediation to manage abuse.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
