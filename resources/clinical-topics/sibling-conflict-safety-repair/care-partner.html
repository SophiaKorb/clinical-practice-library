Sibling conflict: power, safety and repair — Care-partner guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · School-age children and caregivers

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Check power before mediation

Try: "Can each child safely say no and ask for help?"

Protect without equal blame

Respond to actual harm and responsibility, not a rule that both must be at fault.

Change the environment

Provide supervision, separate safe space or clear resource arrangements as appropriate.

Use voluntary specific repair

Only after safety assessment; do not demand forgiveness or joint disclosure in abusive situations.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Assess age/development, size/power, frequency, fear, injuries, access to adults and whether each child has real choice. Do not assume equal responsibility because two children are involved. Caregivers need to provide protection and fair access to resources; children should not manage an adult safety failure. Once safe and appropriate, separate accounts, concrete skill coaching and voluntary repair can address ordinary conflict.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Injury, threats, coercion, sexual harm, abuse or a child unable to stay safe requires appropriate urgent/safeguarding response. Do not use equal blame, forced forgiveness or mediation to manage abuse.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
