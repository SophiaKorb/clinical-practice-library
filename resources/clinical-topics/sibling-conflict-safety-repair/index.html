Sibling conflict: power, safety and repair

School-age children and caregivers · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Arguments, competition and hurt between siblings vary in meaning and severity. First distinguish ordinary conflict from intimidation, repeated targeted harm, coercion, sexual harm or abuse. A joint repair exercise is unsuitable when a child cannot safely refuse.

Assess age/development, size/power, frequency, fear, injuries, access to adults and whether each child has real choice. Do not assume equal responsibility because two children are involved. Caregivers need to provide protection and fair access to resources; children should not manage an adult safety failure. Once safe and appropriate, separate accounts, concrete skill coaching and voluntary repair can address ordinary conflict.

Practical pathway

Adult responsibility — always

Ensure safety, provide support, set clear expectations, and guide next steps.

Unsafe or coercive

Injury, threats, serious intimidation, sexual harm, coercion, or a child unable to refuse safely. When safety is uncertain, assess and protect first.

  1. Protect. Stop harm and ensure immediate safety.
  2. Assess risk. Consider supervision, safety planning and reporting duties.
  3. Follow safeguarding procedures. Coordinate appropriate services and support all siblings. Do not require joint mediation, apology or repair.

Safe and voluntary

There is no current safety concern or coercion, and each child can freely decline participation or repair.

  1. Help each child understand. Listen to perspectives and emotions without assigning equal blame automatically.
  2. Offer coaching or repair. Support problem-solving, accountability and making amends if freely chosen.
  3. Do not require forgiveness. A child may refuse without penalty; respect and safety do not depend on reconciliation.

Ongoing support for all siblings

Monitor safety and wellbeing, maintain clear expectations, and support healthier ways of interacting.

Adults retain responsibility on both pathways. Safeguarding is not contingent on agreeing to repair; voluntary repair is considered only when safety and genuine choice are established.

Safety and scope

Injury, threats, coercion, sexual harm, abuse or a child unable to stay safe requires appropriate urgent/safeguarding response. Do not use equal blame, forced forgiveness or mediation to manage abuse.

Use these tools for communication, observation and coordination within your role. Follow your training, supervision and agency requirements for diagnosis and treatment. For a crisis, use local emergency services.
