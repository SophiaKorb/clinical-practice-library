Supervised reasoning practice · Adults with intellectual disability

Fictional case: Dev is offered a choice between two activities using a long verbal explanation. They push the paper away. A worker concludes Dev cannot choose. A familiar supporter says Dev points reliably to photographs and often declines crowded rooms. One activity can be offered quietly, but this was not explained. Choose a next step that restores access and a real option to decline.

What is the most useful next step, and why?

Choose an option to review the reasoning. This does not score clinical competence.

Worked formulation and supervision prompts

Use concrete, meaningful options and adequate communication support before interpreting understanding. The UK guideline term learning disability refers here to intellectual disability, not a US specific learning disorder. Consider health, sensory factors, communication, environment and learning history. Decision-making ability is decision-specific; guardianship or diagnosis does not replace applicable consent procedures or the person's participation.

What still needs assessment?

  • How does this person usually show yes, no, preference or discomfort?
  • Are these options concrete, familiar and genuinely available?
  • What happens if the person declines?
  • What health or environmental changes could explain the current difficulty?

Explain which information could change your plan, which tasks your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Ask a supervisor to review the reasoning and communication, rather than treating the preferred option as a universal answer.

Safety and scope

Acute pain/illness, abuse, coercion or inability to remain safe needs prompt appropriate response. Do not infer consent from compliance or override refusal with a behavior-management plan.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
