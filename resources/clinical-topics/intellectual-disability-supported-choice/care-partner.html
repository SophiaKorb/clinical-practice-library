Intellectual disability: supported choice and functional communication — Care-partner guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · Adults with intellectual disability

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Learn the person's communication

Ask how preference and discomfort are usually shown; involve SLP/access expertise as needed.

Offer real choices

Do not present a decision already made as an open choice.

Avoid agreement pressure

Allow time and refusal. Familiarity helps but does not authorize you to substitute your preference.

Review health and rights

Relay new distress/pain concerns and use decision-specific qualified procedures.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Use concrete, meaningful options and adequate communication support before interpreting understanding. The UK guideline term learning disability refers here to intellectual disability, not a US specific learning disorder. Consider health, sensory factors, communication, environment and learning history. Decision-making ability is decision-specific; guardianship or diagnosis does not replace applicable consent procedures or the person's participation.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Acute pain/illness, abuse, coercion or inability to remain safe needs prompt appropriate response. Do not infer consent from compliance or override refusal with a behavior-management plan.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
