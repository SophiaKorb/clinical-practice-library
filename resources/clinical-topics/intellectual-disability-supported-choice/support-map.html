Intellectual disability: supported choice and functional communication — Visual support map

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Adults with intellectual disability · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

A person may communicate preferences through speech, gesture, behavior, aids or familiar routines. Difficulty with an abstract question does not justify removing choice. Distress or behavior change may communicate unmet needs, pain, inaccessible demands or unwanted situations.

How the parts relate

  1. Real optionsAvailable, familiar, decline possible
  2. Communication accessPreferred mode + time
  3. ResponseWords, gesture, aid, behavior
  4. VerificationMeaning, uncertainty, no pressure
  5. Action + reviewChosen support and experience

Real options, communication access and a response mode must be available together before checking meaning. Support choice without assuming agreement, capacity or inability from one answer.

This map organizes a conversation; it does not establish causation or calculate risk. Follow the sequence for one event, then return to context and competing explanations. The arrows show a proposed relationship to examine, not proof.

Clinician layer: what the map could miss

Communication/access

The question may be too abstract, too fast or unavailable in a reliable response mode.

Health/unmet need

Pain, constipation, sleep, sensory changes or medication effects can alter behavior and require medical review.

Choice/power context

A nominal option may not be a real choice if refusal is punished or alternatives are unavailable.

Use concrete, meaningful options and adequate communication support before interpreting understanding. The UK guideline term learning disability refers here to intellectual disability, not a US specific learning disorder. Consider health, sensory factors, communication, environment and learning history. Decision-making ability is decision-specific; guardianship or diagnosis does not replace applicable consent procedures or the person's participation.

Worked example

Fictional case: Dev is offered a choice between two activities using a long verbal explanation. They push the paper away. A worker concludes Dev cannot choose. A familiar supporter says Dev points reliably to photographs and often declines crowded rooms. One activity can be offered quietly, but this was not explained. Choose a next step that restores access and a real option to decline.

Best supported: support participation and preserve uncertainty while following qualified decision procedures.

Safety and scope

Acute pain/illness, abuse, coercion or inability to remain safe needs prompt appropriate response. Do not infer consent from compliance or override refusal with a behavior-management plan.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
