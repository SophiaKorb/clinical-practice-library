Sleep loss: exhaustion or rising activation? — Interactive case practice

Supervised reasoning practice · Adults

Fictional case: Morgan works two jobs and has slept four hours on several nights. They feel exhausted and nearly fell asleep while driving. Their friend says Morgan might be manic because they are irritable. Morgan reports wanting more sleep but having no quiet room before the next shift. There is no report yet of elevated energy, new risky projects or a distinct mood episode. Assessment should still ask about these possibilities and medical sleep conditions. Worked formulation and supervision prompts

Assess sleep opportunity, reported sleep, daytime sleepiness and energy separately. A change toward reduced need for sleep with elevated or irritable mood and increased activity can indicate an episode requiring prompt evaluation. Insomnia treatment is not automatically suitable during unstable mood, acute psychosis or dangerous sleepiness. This tool helps organize a handoff; it must not become a self-triage test or a schedule for restricting sleep.

What still needs assessment?

  • When was there a real opportunity to sleep, and what got in the way?
  • After a short night, did you feel sleepy, exhausted, wired or unusually energetic?
  • What changed in your activity, mood and decisions at the same time?
  • Any driving, work or caregiving situations where sleepiness or activation makes things unsafe?

Explain what evidence would change the plan, what your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Review your reasoning with a supervisor.

Safety and scope

Rapid activation with low sleep, acute confusion, dangerous sleepiness, suicidal thinking or inability to function requires timely clinical assessment. Avoid driving or hazardous tasks when unsafe; use local urgent help for immediate danger.

Use these tools for communication, observation and coordination within your role. Follow your training, supervision and agency requirements for diagnosis and treatment. For a crisis, use local emergency services.
