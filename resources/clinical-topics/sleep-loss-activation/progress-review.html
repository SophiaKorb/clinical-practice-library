Sleep loss: exhaustion or rising activation? — Progress review tool

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Collaborative functional review · Adults

Choose one or two meaningful outcomes with the person. Agree the observation period, context and support before baseline. Compare like situations where possible, and retain uncertainty when conditions change. These indicators are individualized observations, not a validated scale or diagnostic score.

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Interpret change carefully

Ask whether access, health, demands, relationships or support changed. Fewer visible symptoms can reflect avoidance, suppression or fewer opportunities. More participation can matter even while distress remains. Missing data is missing data, not evidence of improvement.

Options to discuss, not automatic rules

Create a paired sleep/energy record

Use approximate sleep times and a next-day description. Record uncertainty, opportunity and disruptions, not a rigid numerical target.

Address immediate safety and access

Discuss transport, hazardous work, care responsibilities and a feasible rest environment. Staff follow local policy and relay changes.

Refer according to the pattern

Qualified evaluation considers mood episodes and sleep/medical conditions. CBT-I is structured treatment for suitable insomnia presentations; no self-directed sleep restriction here.

Urgent deterioration bypasses the routine review date. No percentage change on this worksheet authorizes a diagnosis, medication adjustment or discharge.

Safety and scope

Rapid activation with low sleep, acute confusion, dangerous sleepiness, suicidal thinking or inability to function requires timely clinical assessment. Avoid driving or hazardous tasks when unsafe; use local urgent help for immediate danger.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
