A sudden surge of fear may involve racing heart, breathlessness, dizziness or fears of collapse. Start by considering appropriate medical assessment and context. A worksheet cannot decide that chest pain or breathing difficulty is anxiety.

Understanding the pattern

When panic is an appropriate working formulation, the loop can involve a sensation, catastrophic meaning, threat attention, escalating arousal and escape/checking that brings immediate relief but sustains fear. Not every safety behavior is unnecessary: medically indicated inhalers, mobility supports and safe access arrangements should not be removed as an exposure exercise. Distinguish panic attacks occurring in other conditions from panic disorder; establish recurrence, concern, avoidance and function through qualified assessment.

Keep competing explanations open

Medical or substance contributor

New/changed symptoms, medications, stimulants, respiratory or cardiac problems need appropriate evaluation. Do not use response to reassurance as proof of a psychological cause.

Panic learning loop

Ask what the sensation means to the person, what they do next and the short/long-term effect. Context matters more than labeling the coping as bad.

Other fear/context

Trauma cues, social evaluation, specific situations or broader anxiety can produce panic. Treatment should fit the maintaining process and goals.

Ask the person or caregiver

  • What did you first notice in your body, and what did you think it meant?
  • What happened next, and what helped immediately?
  • What have you stopped doing because another episode might happen?
  • What medical advice or safety plan do we need to respect?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Map one recent episode

Separate sensation, prediction, response and outcome. Include what was actually known medically and what remained uncertain.

Develop a collaborative CBT plan

A trained clinician may use cognitive and exposure approaches appropriate to assessment and consent. Interoceptive exercises require suitability review; this page gives no exercise prescription.

Reconnect with a chosen activity

Define a feasible step and support needs. Evaluate participation and learning, not only whether anxiety disappeared.

Safety and scope

New, severe or changed chest pain, collapse, breathing difficulty or other concerning physical symptoms warrant appropriate urgent medical evaluation. Suicidality or inability to remain safe follows the local crisis pathway; never use this map to rule out medical illness.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
