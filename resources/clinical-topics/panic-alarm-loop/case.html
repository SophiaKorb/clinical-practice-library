Worked formulation and supervision prompts

When panic is an appropriate working formulation, the loop can involve a sensation, catastrophic meaning, threat attention, escalating arousal and escape/checking that brings immediate relief but sustains fear. Not every safety behavior is unnecessary: medically indicated inhalers, mobility supports and safe access arrangements should not be removed as an exposure exercise. Distinguish panic attacks occurring in other conditions from panic disorder; establish recurrence, concern, avoidance and function through qualified assessment.

What still needs assessment?

  • What did you first notice in your body, and what did you think it meant?
  • What happened next, and what helped immediately?
  • What have you stopped doing because another episode might happen?
  • What medical advice or safety plan do we need to respect?

Explain which information could change your plan, which tasks your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Ask a supervisor to review the reasoning and communication, rather than treating the preferred option as a universal answer.

Safety and scope

New, severe or changed chest pain, collapse, breathing difficulty or other concerning physical symptoms warrant appropriate urgent medical evaluation. Suicidality or inability to remain safe follows the local crisis pathway; never use this map to rule out medical illness.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
