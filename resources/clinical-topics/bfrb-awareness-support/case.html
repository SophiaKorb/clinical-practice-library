Worked formulation and supervision prompts

Examine sensory, emotional, cognitive, motor and environmental patterns without imposing a single reason. Habit-reversal and comprehensive behavioral approaches use individualized trained treatment; evidence from a hair-pulling trial cannot automatically establish effects for every picking or biting behavior. A brief awareness record should reduce uncertainty about context, not become punitive surveillance. Medical/dermatological needs and stigma deserve attention.

What still needs assessment?

  • When do you notice it before, during or only afterward?
  • What sensations, feelings, thoughts or surroundings seem connected?
  • What does the behavior do for you right away, and what happens later?
  • Are there injuries or medical concerns, and what support feels acceptable?

Explain which information could change your plan, which tasks your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Ask a supervisor to review the reasoning and communication, rather than treating the preferred option as a universal answer.

Safety and scope

Infection signs, significant wounds, swallowed hair concerns or other medical symptoms require appropriate evaluation. Assess intentional self-harm separately; do not assume intent from visible injury.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
