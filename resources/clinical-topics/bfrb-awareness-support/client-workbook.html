Plain-language participation aid · Adults

You can write, draw, point, dictate or ask someone you trust to help. You can skip a question. This worksheet helps you describe your experience and preferences; it does not diagnose you or decide treatment.

Describe without shame

This is something to understand and get help for, not a moral failure.

Notice gently

What setting or sensation shows up around it? You do not need to count constantly.

Ask for medical help when needed

A sore or injury deserves care; hiding it can make help harder to access.

Choose support boundaries

Tell a supporter what is helpful and what feels like surveillance or punishment.

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Before you finish

Choose one item to discuss with your care team. Ask what choices are available, what the proposed support is meant to help, and when you will review it. Tell the team if a support feels unsafe, inaccessible or unhelpful.

Safety and scope

Infection signs, significant wounds, swallowed hair concerns or other medical symptoms require appropriate evaluation. Assess intentional self-harm separately; do not assume intent from visible injury.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.

Evidence, source access and review limits

Principles below are linked to source families; the maps, case, prompts and monitoring plan are original CPL teaching aids. They have not been validated as measures or professionally approved. No proprietary test items or source worksheets are reproduced. Source review: 2026-10-10.

Self-reported symptoms improved versus minimal-attention control; interviewer-rated symptoms/diagnostic status did not show significant immediate differences. Not generalized to all BFRBs.
