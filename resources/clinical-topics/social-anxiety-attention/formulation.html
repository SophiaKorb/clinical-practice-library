Fear of negative evaluation can lead to self-monitoring, rehearsing, hiding visible anxiety and avoiding interaction. Similar avoidance may reflect language barriers, discrimination, trauma, sensory overload or inaccessible communication. Understand those conditions before calling a strategy unnecessary.

Understanding the pattern

Compare attention directed inward toward performance with attention available for the conversation and environment. A safety strategy may reduce anxiety briefly but prevent learning whether the feared evaluation would occur. Some supports are legitimate access tools: AAC, interpreters, written agendas and sensory accommodations should not be withdrawn as treatment. Ask what the strategy does for this person and distinguish anxiety-driven checking from access needs.

Keep competing explanations open

Evaluation fear

Predictions about humiliation, rejection or visible anxiety link to self-focus, avoidance and post-event review. Ask for a concrete prediction.

Access or actual threat

Communication differences, disability access, bullying, racism or unsafe settings require environmental responses, not exposure to mistreatment.

Overlapping conditions

Depression, autism, trauma and other anxiety can contribute. Establish developmental/contextual patterns and the person's goals.

Ask the person

  • What do you think another person will notice or conclude?
  • Where does your attention go while you talk: your own performance, the other person, or both?
  • Which supports help you understand or communicate, and which mainly try to prevent judgment?
  • Afterward, what do you replay, and does it change what you do next time?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Specify a prediction in a safe context

Replace "it will be awful" with an observable feared outcome, while checking that the situation is reasonably safe and accessible.

Test attention and one optional strategy

With a trained clinician, compare a collaborative conversation experiment with usual self-monitoring. Keep accommodations and change only an agreed anxiety-maintaining strategy.

Review evidence with self-compassion

Include ambiguous outcomes and actual mistreatment. Reduce unproductive replay without demanding positive thinking or social conformity.

Safety and scope

Bullying, coercion, harassment and discrimination need protection and organizational action. Exposure is not a reason to tolerate abuse. Assess severe depression, suicidality or other acute concerns through the appropriate clinical pathway.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
