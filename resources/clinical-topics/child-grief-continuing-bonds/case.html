Child grief: remembrance, routines and traumatic reminders — Interactive case practice

Supervised reasoning practice · School-age children and caregivers

Fictional case: Eli, 9, laughs at recess but cries at bedtime after a caregiver's death. They avoid a photo album because it brings images of the hospital. An adult says Eli must finish the album to heal. Eli prefers keeping a small object and asking the same questions again. School routines have also changed. Choose a useful support plan.

What is the most useful next step, and why?

Choose an option to review the reasoning. This does not score clinical competence.

Worked formulation and supervision prompts

Support developmentally appropriate understanding, safe connection and routines without prescribing a normal timeline. Clarify what the child has been told, family/cultural meanings and whether frightening reminders obstruct grief. Do not require memory sharing, disclosure or a particular ritual. This set addresses supportive care and referral, not adult prolonged-grief criteria or a diagnosis based on time alone.

What still needs assessment?

  • What have you been told about what happened?
  • When do you miss them most, and when is remembering easier or harder?
  • Would you like to talk, draw, play, make something, or not today?
  • Who helps with ordinary days, and what has changed at home or school?

Explain which information could change your plan, which tasks your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Ask a supervisor to review the reasoning and communication, rather than treating the preferred option as a universal answer.

Safety and scope

Assess self-harm statements, abuse, unsafe caregiving or severe functional deterioration using age-appropriate clinical procedures. Do not infer safety from playfulness or require the child to manage an adult's distress.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
