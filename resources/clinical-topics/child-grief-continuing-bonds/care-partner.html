Child grief: remembrance, routines and traumatic reminders — Care-partner guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · School-age children and caregivers

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Answer clearly and calmly

Use truthful developmental language and acknowledge what you do not know.

Make remembrance optional

Try: "Would you like to keep this object, look at a photo, or leave it for now?"

Keep routines flexible

Coordinate school and caregiving supports; do not punish grief-related difficulty.

Seek support for adults too

Children should not carry adult emotional responsibility. Refer for sustained impairment, trauma symptoms or safety concerns.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Support developmentally appropriate understanding, safe connection and routines without prescribing a normal timeline. Clarify what the child has been told, family/cultural meanings and whether frightening reminders obstruct grief. Do not require memory sharing, disclosure or a particular ritual. This set addresses supportive care and referral, not adult prolonged-grief criteria or a diagnosis based on time alone.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Assess self-harm statements, abuse, unsafe caregiving or severe functional deterioration using age-appropriate clinical procedures. Do not infer safety from playfulness or require the child to manage an adult's distress.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
