Child grief: remembrance, routines and traumatic reminders — Clinician formulation guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

School-age children and caregivers · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Grief can come in waves, appear in play or behavior, and change as children understand loss differently over time. A child can enjoy an activity while grieving. Trauma reminders may interfere with remembering the person or engaging in ordinary life.

Understanding the pattern

Support developmentally appropriate understanding, safe connection and routines without prescribing a normal timeline. Clarify what the child has been told, family/cultural meanings and whether frightening reminders obstruct grief. Do not require memory sharing, disclosure or a particular ritual. This set addresses supportive care and referral, not adult prolonged-grief criteria or a diagnosis based on time alone.

Keep competing explanations open

Grief and developmental understanding

Ask what death/loss means to the child and revisit as understanding changes.

Traumatic reminders

Fear, images or avoidance may interfere with positive memories and daily life; qualified trauma/grief assessment may help.

Secondary changes

Housing, caregiving, school, finances and family distress can drive difficulty independently of grief symptoms.

Ask the person or caregiver

  • What have you been told about what happened?
  • When do you miss them most, and when is remembering easier or harder?
  • Would you like to talk, draw, play, make something, or not today?
  • Who helps with ordinary days, and what has changed at home or school?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Use clear, age-appropriate communication

Coordinate truthful language with caregivers and allow repeated questions. Avoid confusing euphemisms or promises no adult can guarantee.

Offer optional remembrance

A memory, object, story or private ritual can support connection if the child chooses. Respect cultural practices and refusal.

Maintain routines and refer when needed

Support school/care stability and evaluate sustained impairment or trauma symptoms with qualified care.

Safety and scope

Assess self-harm statements, abuse, unsafe caregiving or severe functional deterioration using age-appropriate clinical procedures. Do not infer safety from playfulness or require the child to manage an adult's distress.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
