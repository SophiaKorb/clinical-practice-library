Body dysmorphic concerns: checking and participation — Care-partner guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · Adults

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Acknowledge distress

Try: "I can see this is taking a lot from your day. Can I help you access care?"

Avoid rating appearance repeatedly

Follow a collaborative support plan rather than arguing or joining comparison.

Respect privacy

Do not demand photos, expose the concern to others or force participation.

Take safety seriously

Relay hopelessness or unsafe behavior promptly; do not dismiss it as vanity.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

The relevant process is the relationship between attention, feared meaning, checking and life restriction. Avoid turning therapy into proof that the person looks fine. Eating-disorder weight/shape concerns, actual discrimination, medical appearance changes, depression and other conditions may overlap or need different care. Treatment requires qualified assessment and suitable CBT/other care; this aid does not prescribe mirror exposure, cosmetic decisions or medication.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Assess suicidality, severe deterioration, unsafe attempts to alter appearance or other acute risks through qualified procedures. Do not dismiss risk as vanity or use reassurance as the only response.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
