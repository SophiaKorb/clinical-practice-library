Tics: context, impact and CBIT referral

School-age children and caregivers · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Tics can fluctuate, involve movement or sound, and be temporarily suppressed at an effortful cost. Frequency alone does not establish impairment or a need to eliminate them. The child's own distress, pain, participation and preferences matter.

Qualified assessment distinguishes tics from other movements/behaviors and considers comorbid ADHD, OCD, anxiety and health concerns. Some children notice a premonitory urge and others do not. Watchful waiting can be appropriate when there is no functional impairment; CBIT is a trained, structured intervention when suitable. This page supports observation/access and referral, not prescribing a competing response or rewarding suppression.

Practical pathway

  1. Movement / soundDescribe without judgment
  2. ContextTask, stress, social response
  3. Urge / effortKnown or not; suppression cost
  4. Functional impactPain, participation, stigma
  5. Chosen next stepAccess, assessment, referral

Safety and scope

New unusual movements, injury, severe pain or acute neurological change needs medical assessment. Do not punish tics, prescribe unsupervised competing responses or advise medication changes.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
