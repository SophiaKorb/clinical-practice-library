Tics: context, impact and CBIT referral — Clinician formulation guide

School-age children and caregivers · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Tics can fluctuate, involve movement or sound, and be temporarily suppressed at an effortful cost. Frequency alone does not establish impairment or a need to eliminate them. The child's own distress, pain, participation and preferences matter.

Understanding the pattern

Qualified assessment distinguishes tics from other movements/behaviors and considers comorbid ADHD, OCD, anxiety and health concerns. Some children notice a premonitory urge and others do not. Watchful waiting can be appropriate when there is no functional impairment; CBIT is a trained, structured intervention when suitable. This page supports observation/access and referral, not prescribing a competing response or rewarding suppression.

Keep competing explanations open

Tic course and impact

Ask about onset, waxing/waning, pain and interference, not just counts.

Context and suppression cost

School expectations, attention from others and efforts to hold tics may affect the experience.

Co-occurring needs

Attention, obsessions, anxiety, sleep and learning may cause more impairment than the tics.

Ask the person or caregiver

  • What, if anything, bothers you about the movement or sound?
  • Do you notice a feeling before it, or not?
  • What happens when you try to hold it in?
  • What school or home activity would you like easier?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Reduce stigma and unnecessary demands

Allow respectful access and avoid punishment or public reminders about tics.

Assess impact and preferences

Qualified care determines whether observation, CBIT referral or other treatment is appropriate.

Coordinate accommodations and review

Support participation and privacy; record painful or disruptive changes and comorbid needs.

Safety and scope

New unusual movements, injury, severe pain or acute neurological change needs medical assessment. Do not punish tics, prescribe unsupervised competing responses or advise medication changes.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
