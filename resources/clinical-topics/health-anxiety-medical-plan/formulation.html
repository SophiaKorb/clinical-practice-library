Health anxiety: checking and a clear medical plan — Clinician formulation guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Adults · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Repeated body checking, internet searching or reassurance seeking may bring brief relief while increasing attention to threat. The person may also have genuine illness or new symptoms. Psychological formulation must not become a reason to ignore medical concerns.

Understanding the pattern

Coordinate a medical response plan and a psychological formulation rather than asking staff to decide which symptoms are real. Clarify what the agreed medical advice says about new/changed symptoms, routine follow-up and contacts. Examine checking by its function and effect, not by assuming every appointment is unnecessary. Anxiety work targets distress, uncertainty and participation within medically appropriate boundaries.

Keep competing explanations open

Checking/reassurance loop

Threat attention and repeated certainty seeking may sustain concern; ask about relief duration and cost.

Medical need or changed symptom

Existing illness and new symptoms require appropriate qualified evaluation; psychological explanations do not clear risk.

Access/history

Prior dismissal, discrimination, unclear advice or poor access can make repeated seeking understandable. Repair communication and trust.

Ask the person or caregiver

  • What symptom or thought starts the checking?
  • What do you hope checking will settle, and how long does relief last?
  • What medical advice have you received about new or changed symptoms?
  • What activity is interrupted, and what would clearer care coordination help you do?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Clarify the medical plan

Confirm the responsible clinician, follow-up and urgent routes; this page supplies no symptom triage thresholds.

Map one certainty-search pattern

Notice checking and its short/later effects without asking for constant body scanning.

Agree a psychological support plan

A trained clinician can work on uncertainty and participation while respecting medical care, consent and history.

Safety and scope

New, severe, changed or otherwise concerning physical symptoms need appropriate medical assessment. Follow the clinician's advice and urgent local pathways; this worksheet must not classify symptoms as harmless.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
