Difficulty after meetings, errands or social demands may reflect accumulated sensory, communication and executive load. Visible composure does not prove low effort; an adult may need substantial recovery after appearing to cope.

Clinician layer: what the map could miss

Demand/load mismatch

Noise, transitions, social interpretation and multitasking can accumulate; identify which demands are costly.

Access and autonomy

Communication modes, predictability, breaks and control may matter more than a coping exercise.

Health/co-occurring conditions

Pain, sleep change, depression, anxiety and medical illness need evaluation alongside developmental history.

Use the person's own communication and sensory preferences to map demands across the day. Autism is a developmental diagnosis, not inferred from overload alone. Assess health, sleep, anxiety, depression, trauma, workplace demands and actual access. Reducing load can improve participation without requiring more masking, eye contact or tolerance of pain. No single sensory plan works for everyone, and burnout language does not replace differential assessment of deterioration.

Worked example

Fictional case: Alex attends meetings successfully but cannot prepare dinner afterward and needs the weekend to recover. A manager recommends eye-contact practice. Alex says written agendas, fewer overlapping conversations and a quieter return journey would help. They have also developed new headaches and poor sleep. Choose a formulation and support step.

Best supported: address both context and differential needs, then review participation and recovery burden.

Safety and scope

Marked deterioration, inability to meet basic needs, suicidality or new medical symptoms requires appropriate assessment. Do not attribute every difficulty to autism or prescribe endurance through overload.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
