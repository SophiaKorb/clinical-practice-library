Adult autism: load, access and recovery — Care-partner guide

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · Adults

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Ask rather than assume

Try: "What would make this task easier to access?" Do not require eye contact.

Reduce agreed demands

Help with predictable information, quieter settings or transitions the person chooses.

Respect recovery/privacy

Do not interpret a needed break as rejection or automatically schedule more social activity.

Monitor costs with consent

Notice delayed difficulty and new health changes; avoid policing harmless self-regulation.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Use the person's own communication and sensory preferences to map demands across the day. Autism is a developmental diagnosis, not inferred from overload alone. Assess health, sleep, anxiety, depression, trauma, workplace demands and actual access. Reducing load can improve participation without requiring more masking, eye contact or tolerance of pain. No single sensory plan works for everyone, and burnout language does not replace differential assessment of deterioration.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Marked deterioration, inability to meet basic needs, suicidality or new medical symptoms requires appropriate assessment. Do not attribute every difficulty to autism or prescribe endurance through overload.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
