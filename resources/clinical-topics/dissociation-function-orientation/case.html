Dissociation: orientation, time gaps and daily function — Interactive case practice

Supervised reasoning practice · Adults

Fictional case: Ren reports feeling distant during long appointments and not remembering portions afterward. They recently changed medication and sleep poorly. A worker suggests that missing memories must represent a hidden trauma. Ren feels frightened by that explanation but would welcome shorter appointments and written summaries. Choose a response that supports function and keeps the differential open.

What is the most useful next step, and why?

Choose an option to review the reasoning. This does not score clinical competence.

Worked formulation and supervision prompts

Assess phenomenology, onset, memory, triggers, sleep, substances, neurological/medical conditions and current safety. Avoid suggestive questions about hidden identities or assumed memories. Do not encourage memory recovery or confirm unverified traumatic explanations. Reorientation is a practical support, not a diagnosis or causal test. Qualified assessment determines whether dissociation is part of PTSD, another condition or a different process entirely.

What still needs assessment?

  • What does "not here" feel like for you?
  • What do you remember directly, and what did someone else tell you?
  • When did this begin, and what health, sleep or substance changes occurred?
  • Which daily tasks become unsafe or harder when this happens?

Explain which information could change your plan, which tasks your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Ask a supervisor to review the reasoning and communication, rather than treating the preferred option as a universal answer.

Safety and scope

Acute confusion, new neurological symptoms, loss of consciousness, driving danger or inability to remain safe needs prompt medical/clinical response. Do not treat unexplained time gaps as proof of trauma or encourage unverified memory reconstruction.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
