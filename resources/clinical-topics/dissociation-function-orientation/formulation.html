Dissociation: orientation, time gaps and daily function — Clinician formulation guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Adults · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Feeling unreal, detached from the body, far from surroundings or unable to account for time can have different explanations. Describe the experience and its functional impact before labeling it. PTSD depersonalization/derealization is not equivalent to dissociative identity disorder.

Understanding the pattern

Assess phenomenology, onset, memory, triggers, sleep, substances, neurological/medical conditions and current safety. Avoid suggestive questions about hidden identities or assumed memories. Do not encourage memory recovery or confirm unverified traumatic explanations. Reorientation is a practical support, not a diagnosis or causal test. Qualified assessment determines whether dissociation is part of PTSD, another condition or a different process entirely.

Keep competing explanations open

Detachment

Depersonalization and derealization describe different experiences. Use the person's words and assess context.

Memory/time concern

Clarify what is known, inferred or reported by others. Everyday inattention and sleep-related events are among possibilities.

Medical or acute altered state

Sudden confusion, loss of consciousness or neurological changes require medical evaluation; do not rely on grounding response.

Ask the person or caregiver

  • What does "not here" feel like for you?
  • What do you remember directly, and what did someone else tell you?
  • When did this begin, and what health, sleep or substance changes occurred?
  • Which daily tasks become unsafe or harder when this happens?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Separate experience from explanation

Document detachment, uncertainty and function without introducing a story the person has not reported.

Agree safe orientation/access supports

Use a preferred external cue, written next step or trusted contact; review helpfulness and avoid forced sensory exercises.

Coordinate differential assessment

Qualified clinicians consider psychological and medical explanations and a suitable treatment plan; staff relay observations.

Safety and scope

Acute confusion, new neurological symptoms, loss of consciousness, driving danger or inability to remain safe needs prompt medical/clinical response. Do not treat unexplained time gaps as proof of trauma or encourage unverified memory reconstruction.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
