Dissociation: orientation, time gaps and daily function — Care-partner guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · Adults

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Do not suggest explanations

Try: "What do you notice, and what would help now?" Avoid claims about hidden trauma or identity.

Support orientation gently

Offer information about place/time or a preferred external cue, with permission.

Reduce functional demands

Help with an agreed written sequence or shorter visit without assuming incapacity.

Share precise observations

Report timing and observable changes rather than diagnostic conclusions.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Assess phenomenology, onset, memory, triggers, sleep, substances, neurological/medical conditions and current safety. Avoid suggestive questions about hidden identities or assumed memories. Do not encourage memory recovery or confirm unverified traumatic explanations. Reorientation is a practical support, not a diagnosis or causal test. Qualified assessment determines whether dissociation is part of PTSD, another condition or a different process entirely.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Acute confusion, new neurological symptoms, loss of consciousness, driving danger or inability to remain safe needs prompt medical/clinical response. Do not treat unexplained time gaps as proof of trauma or encourage unverified memory reconstruction.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
