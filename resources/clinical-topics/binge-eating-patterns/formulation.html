Binge eating: patterns, shame and treatment engagement — Clinician formulation guide

Clinician formulation guideInteractive case practiceVisual support mapClient workbookCare-partner guideProgress review tool

Adults · Presentation → Understanding → Assessment → Intervention → Tools → Monitoring

Loss-of-control eating, distress and secrecy merit nonjudgmental assessment. Clarify the person's experience rather than estimating a diagnosis from appearance, a single meal or a care partner's judgment. Restriction and compensation can change the formulation.

Understanding the pattern

Map context, prior eating restriction, emotion, loss of control and aftermath without turning notes into calorie surveillance. Treatment targets eating-disorder processes and function, not moral character or weight-loss compliance. Qualified assessment considers binge-eating disorder, bulimia and other presentations, medical concerns and overlapping mood/trauma symptoms. A weight-loss plan is not interchangeable with an eating-disorder treatment plan.

Keep competing explanations open

Restriction/context

Long gaps, inadequate intake, food insecurity or rigid rules may contribute; examine with qualified care.

Emotion and learning

Eating can provide temporary relief, followed by shame and avoidance; the function is individual, not assumed.

Compensation/medical concerns

Vomiting, laxative misuse, extreme exercise or other compensation changes assessment and risks; ask sensitively.

Ask the person or caregiver

  • What does losing control mean in your experience?
  • What was happening earlier that day, including access to food and eating rules?
  • What happens afterward emotionally or in what you do?
  • Do you try to compensate, and are there physical symptoms your team should know about?
Instructions for the interviewer

Ask one question at a time, use the person's preferred language/response mode, and distinguish direct observations, attributed reports and hypotheses. Do not read these instructions as interview questions. Ask permission before collateral contact and explain confidentiality limits. Record contradictions and missing information.

Intervention selection and coordination

Offer trained assessment and treatment options

Facilitate appropriate eating-disorder care, discuss preferences and clarify medical/nutritional roles.

Map one pattern without surveillance

Use broad context and function; avoid detailed calorie/weight tracking when it intensifies symptoms.

Reduce shame and access barriers

Support confidential appointments, adequate food access and a realistic specialist plan.

Safety and scope

Compensatory behaviors, fainting, dehydration, cardiac symptoms, severe deterioration or suicidality require appropriate medical/clinical assessment. Do not delay care while completing a pattern log.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
