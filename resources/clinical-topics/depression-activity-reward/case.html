Supervised reasoning practice · Adults

Fictional case: Dani has stopped attending a community garden after becoming depressed. They say "I should just try harder," but the bus fare is unaffordable and standing worsens pain. A therapist suggested daily gardening, which Dani has not attempted. Dani misses seeing one friend and would welcome a short seated visit if transport were arranged. Worked formulation and supervision prompts

Behavioral activation examines how daily patterns relate to mood, avoidance, opportunity and valued reinforcement. A useful plan changes a manageable action or environmental barrier, rather than demanding that the person feel motivated first. Assess depressive symptoms, duration, functioning and safety, and consider bipolar history, medical contributors, substances and bereavement before selecting treatment. Greater activity is not always better; rest and pacing may be necessary.

What still needs assessment?

  • Which part of the day feels hardest, and what happens just before it?
  • What used to give you connection, meaning or accomplishment?
  • What would make a tiny version possible with your current energy and resources?
  • Have there been periods of unusually high energy or much less need for sleep?

Explain which information could change your plan, which tasks your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Ask a supervisor to review the reasoning and communication, rather than treating the preferred option as a universal answer.

Safety and scope

Suicidality, inability to meet basic needs, severe deterioration or possible activation requires appropriate assessment. A scheduled activity is not a suicide prevention plan or a substitute for clinical care.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
