Caregiver, family or chosen supporter · Adults

The person decides whom they want involved whenever possible. Ask about privacy, preferred communication and practical help before using this guide. Your role is support and a clear handoff, not diagnosis or independent specialist treatment.

Ask permission for specific support

Try: "Would you like me to help note sleep changes or contact the team with you?" Agree what may be shared.

Describe change without arguing about identity

Try: "You usually rest after work; this week you have stayed awake and spent money needed for rent. I am concerned." Avoid threats or diagnostic debates.

Keep help reliable

Follow the agreed plan. Reduce avoidable stimulation and practical pressures where welcomed; do not secretly control money or medication.

Escalate proportionately

Promptly relay rapid deterioration or safety concerns. Emergency situations may require help without ordinary consent under applicable policy/law; do not improvise restraint.

A specific support agreement

Use fictional or appropriately de-identified notes. Entries stay in this open page; they are not saved or sent by this tool. Browser extensions or shared computers may still expose them. Clearing or leaving loses entries. Printing may include your notes.

Why the clinical formulation matters

Build a longitudinal formulation before treating isolated features as a diagnosis. Reduced need for sleep means markedly less sleep with unexpectedly sustained energy; insomnia often leaves the person tired. These can coexist. Ask about depressive episodes, mixed features, psychotic experiences, medications, substances, health changes and the person's own account of what changed. Obtain collateral with permission and preserve disagreements rather than choosing the loudest account.

Discuss the formulation with the treating clinician when invited; do not use it to assign motives, override consent or change medication.

Safety and scope

Rapidly escalating activation, unsafe decisions, inability to care for basic needs, suicidal thinking, psychosis or substantial sleep loss with rising energy needs prompt clinical assessment; imminent danger or medical instability uses the local urgent response pathway.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
