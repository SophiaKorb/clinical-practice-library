Supervised reasoning practice · Adults

Fictional case: Lee becomes frightened by a slammed door during a clinic visit and says the room feels far away. A worker offers a body scan; Lee says it makes the feeling worse. Lee prefers looking at the window and hearing where they are. No one has checked whether Lee feels threatened by a person currently present. Choose a trauma-informed response.

What is the most useful next step, and why?

Choose an option to review the reasoning. This does not score clinical competence.

Worked formulation and supervision prompts

First distinguish a reminder in a reasonably safe setting from actual current threat. Present-orientation support may help some people reconnect with place, time and choice, but it is not trauma processing and can be unhelpful if imposed. Eye closure, touch, body scanning and breathing focus may intensify distress for some. Ask permission, offer alternatives and stop what worsens symptoms. Evidence-based trauma treatment requires trained assessment and collaborative selection; prolonged compulsory stabilization is not automatically required for everyone.

What still needs assessment?

  • Do you feel in danger here now, reminded of something, or unsure?
  • What helps you notice where you are without making things worse?
  • Would words, looking around, movement, quiet or company be preferable?
  • What would you like me to avoid?

Explain which information could change your plan, which tasks your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Ask a supervisor to review the reasoning and communication, rather than treating the preferred option as a universal answer.

Safety and scope

Current violence, abuse, inability to remain safe, acute confusion or medical change needs appropriate urgent response. Do not assume every altered state is a trauma reaction or force trauma disclosure.

Staff and care partners can support communication, observe and coordinate within their authorized role. Diagnosis, treatment selection and specialized interventions require appropriate training and supervision. This page is not a crisis service.
