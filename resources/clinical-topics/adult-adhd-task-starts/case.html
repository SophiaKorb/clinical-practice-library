Worked formulation and supervision prompts

Separate task comprehension, initiation, sequencing and completion. ADHD assessment requires developmental and cross-setting evidence and differential evaluation; a response to a timer or stimulant is not diagnostic proof. A support experiment can externalize the next action and reduce start cost without shaming the person. Look for competing explanations such as depression, sleep loss, pain, literacy demands and uncertainty about quality.

What still needs assessment?

  • At the moment you try to begin, what is the next visible action?
  • What do you need to hold in mind, and what could be written or placed outside your memory?
  • What feels unclear, unpleasant or risky about starting?
  • When is the same task easier, and what is different then?

Explain what evidence would change the plan, what your role permits, and who owns the next handoff. Review your reasoning with a supervisor.

Safety and scope

Major sleep change, mood activation, severe depression, substance concerns or inability to meet basic needs needs clinical assessment. Do not advise medication changes or use task success as diagnostic proof.

Use these tools for communication, observation and coordination within your role. Follow your training, supervision and agency requirements for diagnosis and treatment. For a crisis, use local emergency services.
