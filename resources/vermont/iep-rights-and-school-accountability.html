Administrative complaints, mediation, and due process

Vermont provides administrative complaints, voluntary mediation, and due process procedures for disputes concerning special education evaluation, identification, placement, or services.

Make requests and concerns in writing; preserve dates, evaluations, notices, communications, service logs, and concrete examples. Ask for the current procedural safeguards and identify which agency handles the specific complaint. Some disputes are better addressed through an IEP meeting, state complaint, mediation, due process, or civil rights channels; these options are not interchangeable.

Preparing for an IEP meeting

  1. Gather evaluations, work samples, school communications, and the student's strengths and needs.
  2. Write down clear questions about evaluation, supports, goals, and progress.
  3. Ask the school for its current parent information and procedural safeguards.
  4. Keep copies of the completed IEP, decisions, and agreed next steps.

Start with a practical school request

  1. Write down the student's strengths, specific difficulties, and examples from school.
  2. Ask the receiving school or district about special education referral and evaluation steps.
  3. Keep dated copies of written requests, school notices, evaluation documents, and replies.
  4. Request current procedural safeguards and clarify how the student and family participate in decisions.

Vermont families can obtain state guidance on referrals, eligibility decisions, meetings, and available dispute resolution processes.

What to bring to the IEP meeting

Gather evaluations, attendance and work records, family observations, student priorities, and questions about goals, instruction, accommodations, related services, transportation where relevant, and progress reporting. Ask for a copy of the plan and confirm next steps in writing.

Changing districts or states

Send the new district the most recent IEP and evaluations promptly. Ask the team about comparable services during the transition, the timing of an appropriate review, and local contacts. The new jurisdiction's procedures may differ.
