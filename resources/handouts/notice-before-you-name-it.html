Emotion awareness · Regulation

Notice Before You Name It

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

You do not need the perfect emotion word to understand what your system needs

Start anywhere

Some people notice a body feeling first. Others notice an urge, thought, image, behavior, or change in energy. Any doorway counts.

Body

Fast or slow heart? Tight, heavy, hot, cold, shaky, numb, restless, sore, empty, full, or hard to locate?

Energy

Too much, too little, or about right?

Urge

Move closer, move away, hide, freeze, argue, fix, cry, laugh, sleep, escape, or seek help?

Attention

Narrow and stuck, scattered, watchful, foggy, clear, or pulled toward one thing?

Possible meaning

Is something important, uncertain, unfair, unsafe, disappointing, overwhelming, exciting, or missing?

Choose the level that works

  1. “Something changed.”
  2. “This feels pleasant, unpleasant, mixed, or neutral.”
  3. “My energy is high, low, or steady.”
  4. Try an emotion family such as angry, afraid, sad, glad, ashamed, disgusted, surprised, or connected.
  5. Choose a more precise word only if it helps.

What might help next

Less input, more information, movement, food, water, rest, connection, distance, predictability, expression, problem solving, or protection?

A usable sentence

“I am not sure what I feel yet. I notice __________________, and I may need __________________.”

For supporters

Do not quiz, insist on eye contact, or demand an emotion label. Offer choices without forcing them. Accept pointing, drawing, typing, gestures, examples, or “I don’t know yet.”

When not to use

Do not turn emotion identification into a requirement during acute dysregulation. Do not use a feelings worksheet to argue someone out of pain, danger, discrimination, sensory overload, or a legitimate need.
