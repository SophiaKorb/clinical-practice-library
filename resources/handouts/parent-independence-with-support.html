Building Independence With Support

Created and curated by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Independence is not simply permission or the absence of help. It develops through practice, appropriate choice, trustworthy relationships, and supports that fit the task.

Separate three questions

  1. What does the child want to do? Ask rather than assuming. Include ways to answer without speech if needed.
  2. What skills are already reliable? Look at what happens in ordinary settings, not only on an unusually good day.
  3. What support and supervision make the next step safe? A cue, checklist, rehearsal, transportation plan, or trusted adult may be a bridge rather than a barrier.

A flexible ladder—not an all-or-nothing reward

Do it together

Adult models, child practices with help; start with success.

Child leads, adult nearby

Offer a choice or step back while keeping agreed check-ins.

Child does it, then reviews

Use the minimum useful support and check what worked.

Move in either direction depending on the task, stress, skill, and risk. Needing support today does not erase a child's progress, and having a disability does not cancel the right to supported choice.

Match practice to the setting

Daily tasks: Child chooses clothes and uses a two-step picture list; adult is nearby for hard fasteners.

Schoolwork: Child chooses which problem to try first; adult checks whether instructions and accommodations are accessible.

Community activities: Begin with a rehearsed plan for permission, money, contacts, returning, and what to do if something goes wrong. Increase freedom only as safety and skills become reliable.

Teen privacy: Offer meaningful private space while being transparent about safety-related expectations, check-ins, and why monitoring changes with risk and maturity.

Make the next step observable

Current reliable skill: __________________________
One next step: ________________________________
Useful supports: _____________________________
Who checks in, when, and about what? ______________
How will we know the step is working? ___________
When will we review and adjust? ________________

Important: Supervision for a credible risk is a protective responsibility, not a punishment. If unsafe behavior is recurring, develop a specific safety plan with the treatment/support team before expanding unsupervised access. Never withhold essential disability or communication supports to test readiness.
