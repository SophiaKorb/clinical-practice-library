Perspective-Taking Without Guessing

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Understanding another person without pretending to read minds

The Main Idea

Different people can notice different facts, know different information, want different things, and feel differently. Perspective-taking means making careful possibilities and checking when we can. It does not mean guessing the “correct” hidden feeling.

The Detective Questions

  • What do I know happened?
  • What did this person see or hear?
  • What might they know that I do not?
  • What might they want, expect, or worry about?
  • What are two possible explanations?
  • Can I ask, or do I need to wait for more information?

Possibility Words

Maybe. One possibility is. I wonder whether. I could be wrong. Can you help me understand?

Example

Sam walks past without answering.

Possibility one: Sam is upset.
Possibility two: Sam did not hear.
Possibility three: Sam is focused on getting somewhere.

A useful response: “Hey, did you hear me?”

What Perspective-Taking Is Not

  • It is not agreeing.
  • It is not excusing harm.
  • It is not knowing another person’s mind better than they do.
  • It is not hiding your natural communication style to seem more typical.
  • It is not taking responsibility for every person’s feelings.

For Parents and Therapists

Praise curiosity and checking, not successful guessing. Use real events only after the child is regulated. Include the child’s own perspective with equal seriousness. Ask for two possibilities rather than demanding the adult’s preferred explanation.

When Not to Use

Do not use this tool to override a child’s report of danger, discrimination, bullying, pain, or boundary violations. Safety assessment comes first.
