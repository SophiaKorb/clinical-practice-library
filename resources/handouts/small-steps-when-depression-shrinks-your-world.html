Depression · Behavioral activation

Small Steps When Depression Shrinks Your World

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

A behavioral-activation guide for teens and adults

Depression can reduce energy, interest, concentration, hope, and the sense that effort will matter. Waiting to feel motivated can leave life getting smaller. Behavioral activation reverses the order: choose a small useful action first, then notice what happens to mood, energy, connection, or momentum afterward.

Start smaller than your inner critic wants

A useful step is specific and realistically doable on a low-energy day. “Fix my life” is not a step. “Put one plate in the sink” is.

1. Notice what has dropped out

Something that used to give me connection: __________________
Something that gave me accomplishment or competence: _______
Something that gave me pleasure, interest, or meaning: _______
Something basic my body needs more regularly: ______________

2. Pick ONE small action

My action: ______________________________________________
When: __________________ Where: _________________________
Make it smaller if needed: _________________________________

Examples. Text one safe person. Sit outside for five minutes. Shower or wash face. Put away five items. Attend the first ten minutes. Walk to the mailbox. Make one simple meal. Open the assignment and read the first question.

3. Predict, then test

Before I do it, I predict my mood/energy will be: _____________
Afterward, what actually happened? _________________________
Did it change pleasure, accomplishment, connection, or momentum, even a little? ___________________________________________

4. Use the two-minute doorway

When starting is the barrier, commit only to the first two minutes. After two minutes, choose again. Stopping after the planned small step still counts.

5. Balance the week

Try to include some combination of:

  • Connection, contact with people who are safe enough.
  • Mastery, something that gives a sense of completion or competence.
  • Pleasure or interest, even if enjoyment is muted at first.
  • Body care, food, water, movement, medication routines, daylight, and sleep support as appropriate.
  • Meaning, something connected to a value, responsibility, culture, spirituality, creativity, or community.

When the plan stalls

Ask what the barrier is before deciding you “didn't try.” Is the step too large? Is there a practical obstacle? Pain? Sleep loss? Attention or task-initiation difficulty? Anxiety? Shame? Lack of transportation, privacy, money, or support? Change the plan to match the barrier.

For supporters

Avoid cheerleading that minimizes depression. Concrete company can work better than advice: “Want me to sit with you while you start?” Notice effort without turning activity into a moral test.

Important safety note

Behavioral activation is not a substitute for assessment when depression includes suicidal thoughts, inability to meet basic needs, psychosis, mania, severe substance-related risk, or a major medical change. Seek appropriate clinical or emergency support when safety is in question.

Bottom line

Action does not have to wait for motivation. The next step should be small enough to begin and meaningful enough to be worth testing.
