Keep helpful supports available. Discuss what happened after recovery, when the person can participate.

Plan this together before overload

Ask the person what overload feels like, how they communicate, and which supports they want. A person may need different supports on different days.

For helpers

  • Reduce sound, crowding, demands, and unnecessary talking when those are adding load.
  • Allow communication through speech, writing, pointing, or a device. Avoid demanding eye contact, explanations, or an apology during overload.
  • Offer supports the person has chosen. Do not force touch, pressure, weighted items, or other sensory input.

Look beyond sensory input. Pain, illness, hunger, sleep, medication effects, trauma cues, and immediate danger may also matter. New or concerning symptoms need appropriate assessment. This sheet does not determine the cause of distress.

Review when ready

Show a short example

Signal: point to “quiet.” Reduce: move away from the crowded room. Recover: headphones and space, if wanted. Return: one familiar task when ready.
