Perspective-Taking · Practice Cards

Perspective Possibility Cards

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Practice making possibilities without pretending to read minds

Read one card. Name the facts everyone could observe. Generate at least two possible explanations. Then choose a useful question or action. There may be many reasonable answers.

Card 1

A friend walks past you in the hallway without saying hello.

Card 2

A classmate laughs after you answer a question.

Card 3

Someone sends “fine.” and does not reply again.

Card 4

A sibling takes their plate to another room during dinner.

Card 5

A teacher says, “See me after class.”

Card 6

A friend cancels plans one hour before you were supposed to meet.

Card 7

A student covers their ears while you are talking.

Card 8

A parent says no to a request but does not explain why.

Card 9

Two people stop talking when you enter the room.

Card 10

Someone does not answer a question you asked.

Card 11

A teammate changes the plan without checking with you.

Card 12

A person says they are not upset, but their voice sounds different.

Questions for Every Card

  • What are the facts?
  • What did the person see or know?
  • What information is missing?
  • What are two or more possible explanations?
  • Which explanation are you treating as true?
  • Could you check? If so, what could you say?
  • If you cannot check, what response protects both you and the relationship?

Useful Checking Phrases

“Did you hear me?”
“I may be reading this wrong. Are we okay?”
“Can you explain what changed?”
“Do you want space, help, or something else?”
“I noticed __________. What does it mean for you?”

Important Limits

Perspective-taking is not agreement, forgiveness, mind-reading, or taking responsibility for everyone’s feelings. Do not use these cards to reinterpret reports of danger, bullying, discrimination, pain, or boundary violations. Start with low-stakes or fictional examples, not the learner’s most painful conflict.
