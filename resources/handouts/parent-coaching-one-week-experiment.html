One-Week Parent Coaching Experiment

CPL education draft · For review by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Choose one recurring situation and test one practical change. This is a learning exercise, not a scorecard for whether a parent or child is ‘good.’

1. Name one situation

When does it happen? (time, place, activity)

What does the child actually do or say? (observable words/actions)

When does this go a little better? What is different?

2. Consider what may be making it hard

  • Instructions or expectations unclear
  • Skill still developing
  • Language or communication barrier
  • Fatigue, hunger, pain, health issue
  • Transition or unexpected change
  • Sensory load
  • Anxiety or emotional overload
  • Too many steps or too much help
  • Relationship conflict
  • Something else / not sure

These are possible explanations, not established motives. Ask the child what helps, in a communication mode they can use.

3. Change just one thing

Before the moment, the adult will:

In the moment, the adult will say or do:

The child will be helped to practice this replacement skill/request:

We will notice and acknowledge success by:

Safety plan or stop condition, if relevant:

4. Notice what happened

For roughly a week, note a few brief examples. Do not collect information during an emergency or pressure the child to perform for a chart.

Situation / date: ____________________
What we tried: __________________________
What happened: ________________________
Child's perspective: ______________________
What I noticed about myself: ______________

5. Review together

What became easier, safer, or more connected?

What didn't work? Which assumption should we reconsider?

Next week: keep / adapt / stop / seek help because…

Clinical boundary: For credible danger, coercion, abuse, or significant deterioration, use individualized professional and safety support rather than experimenting with ordinary rewards or consequences.
