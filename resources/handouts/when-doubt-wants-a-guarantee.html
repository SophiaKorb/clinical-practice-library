When Doubt Wants a Guarantee

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

A guide to reassurance, compulsions, and practicing uncertainty

OCD often turns ordinary uncertainty into an emergency. The mind asks for one more check, one more review, one more confession, one more search, or one more guarantee. The relief can be real, but brief. Then doubt returns and asks for another round.

The cycle

  • Trigger or intrusive thought.
  • Doubt, disgust, fear, guilt, or a sense that something is not right.
  • Compulsion, avoidance, mental review, reassurance, checking, researching, confessing, or repeating.
  • Short-term relief.
  • More importance given to the next doubt.

Reassurance can become a compulsion

A genuine question usually stays answered. OCD reassurance often asks the same question in new forms because the goal is not information. The goal is certainty. Certainty is the part nobody can provide.

ERP changes the response to doubt

Exposure and response prevention means deliberately approaching a planned trigger or uncertainty while reducing the compulsive response that normally follows. Practice should be collaborative and gradual. It is not surprise exposure, humiliation, or being forced into a feared situation.

A useful response sounds different from reassurance

  • “I can hear how strong the doubt is.”
  • “Maybe yes, maybe no. We do not have to solve it right now.”
  • “What would OCD like you to do next?”
  • “What response are you practicing instead?”
  • “I will stay with you, but I am not going to answer the same certainty question again.”

Progress is not measured by calm

A practice can count even when anxiety or doubt stays present. The learning target is being able to continue without completing the usual compulsion.

When this guide does not fit

Do not label reasonable safety behavior, medical care, trauma-related protection, culturally meaningful practice, or a valid boundary as a compulsion without assessment. Severe OCD, unclear diagnosis, significant depression or suicidality, psychosis, eating-disorder symptoms, or high-risk exposures need individualized clinical planning.

Bottom line

The goal is not to prove the feared thought false. The goal is to make more room for uncertainty while choosing what matters instead of what the compulsion demands.
