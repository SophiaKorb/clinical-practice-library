The ARD committee process

Texas calls its IEP decision-making team the Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee. The Texas Education Agency and SPEDTex provide a Parent’s Guide to the ARD Process, procedural safeguards, and multilingual materials.

Prepare for a meeting

  1. Write down concrete examples of barriers to participation and learning, alongside the student’s strengths.
  2. Collect relevant school records, evaluations, work samples, and questions.
  3. Ask the school how to request an evaluation and obtain the applicable procedural safeguards.
  4. At the meeting, discuss individual needs, goals, supports, services, and how progress will be measured.
  5. Request copies of the written plan or decisions and follow up on unresolved questions.

How to request help

  1. Write down what the student can do and where the barrier appears: lessons, reading, communication, behavior, attendance, or participation.
  2. Send a dated evaluation or support request to the district's special education contact and retain a copy.
  3. Ask which process applies, what information the team needs, and how to receive current parent-rights information.

Texas uses an Admission, Review, and Dismissal (ARD) committee. Parents can review the TEA/SPEDTex ARD process guide before meeting and ask for the committee's written decisions.

Prepare for an IEP meeting

  • Bring evaluations, current schoolwork, teacher communications, and the child's strengths and interests.
  • Prepare questions about measurable goals, accommodations, related services, progress reporting, and who implements each support.
  • Ask how caregivers and the student can contribute, and request a copy of the written plan and follow-up steps.

If the family moves between states

Keep the IEP, eligibility and evaluation documents, related-service records, and progress reports. Contact the receiving district promptly and request a transition meeting if needed. IDEA addresses comparable services for students transferring with an IEP, while the new state may have distinct procedures.

For ongoing concerns, document specific events and consult the district and state education department's published parent-resource and dispute-resolution information.
