Eating concerns: medical safety and practical treatment access

Use practical supports to improve access, choice and participation. Case management does not diagnose a condition or replace specialized clinical care.

Case management boundariesSupport resource navigation, skill rehearsal, authorized care coordination and advocacy according to organizational scope and consent. Verify actual referral eligibility, safety and privacy rules. Do not mistake a checklist for clinical treatment.

Six things to do together

  1. Clarify whether the person needs transportation, referrals, insurance, accessible appointments or food-resource support.
  2. Facilitate urgent medical assessment when restrictive intake, purging or physical instability is suspected; do not manage food plans independently.
  3. Coordinate multidisciplinary care with appropriate consent and confidentiality, including qualified eating-disorder clinicians.
  4. Avoid weigh-ins, food rules, or comments on appearance as case-management 'interventions'.
  5. Review access to appropriate medical care, client autonomy, logistical burden and safety follow-through.

When to coordinate qualified care

Fainting, significant medical instability, rapid deterioration or acute safety concerns require urgent medical evaluation.
