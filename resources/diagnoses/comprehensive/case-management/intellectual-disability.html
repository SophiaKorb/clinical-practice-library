Intellectual disability: supported choice and communication

Use practical supports to improve access, choice and participation. Case management does not diagnose a condition or replace specialized clinical care.

Case management boundariesSupport resource navigation, skill rehearsal, authorized care coordination and advocacy according to organizational scope and consent. Verify actual referral eligibility, safety and privacy rules. Do not mistake a checklist for clinical treatment.

Six things to do together

  1. Discover the person's communication method and supported decision-making preferences; do not substitute caregiver preferences for the person's voice.
  2. Offer real choices in understandable formats, with adequate response time and independent advocacy as indicated.
  3. Break service tasks into visible steps and rehearse with accessible materials rather than treating the person as incapable.
  4. Coordinate adaptive, educational, vocational, social and health services in accordance with authorization and rights.
  5. Review actual participation, understanding, safety and self-determination—not simple staff task completion.

When to coordinate qualified care

Abrupt changes, pain, exploitation, communication breakdown or loss of essential support require escalation.

Review the clinical-depth guide, differential and linked evidence before selecting clinical treatment.
