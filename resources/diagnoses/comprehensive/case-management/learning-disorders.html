Learning differences: instructions, literacy and access

Use practical supports to improve access, choice and participation. Case management does not diagnose a condition or replace specialized clinical care.

Case management boundariesSupport resource navigation, skill rehearsal, authorized care coordination and advocacy according to organizational scope and consent. Verify actual referral eligibility, safety and privacy rules. Do not mistake a checklist for clinical treatment.

Six things to do together

  1. Identify the exact learning-related barrier: reading, written output, number sense, instruction comprehension or memory.
  2. Offer accessible alternatives such as audio, demonstration, plain-language written steps or technology as chosen.
  3. Help the student/family organize specific work samples and questions for an educational evaluation without asserting eligibility.
  4. Coordinate with school or adult learning services only with permission and appropriate policy.
  5. Review whether the person can demonstrate knowledge or complete desired tasks with less unnecessary load.

When to coordinate qualified care

Significant academic regression, language/hearing concerns or medical/vision issues require appropriate evaluation.
