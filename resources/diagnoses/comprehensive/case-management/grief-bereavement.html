Grief: practical support and connection without pathologizing loss

Use practical supports to improve access, choice and participation. Case management does not diagnose a condition or replace specialized clinical care.

Case management boundariesSupport resource navigation, skill rehearsal, authorized care coordination and advocacy according to organizational scope and consent. Verify actual referral eligibility, safety and privacy rules. Do not mistake a checklist for clinical treatment.

Six things to do together

  1. Ask what responsibilities or contact the bereaved person wants help navigating and respect cultural, spiritual and relational meanings.
  2. Support chosen logistics such as appointments, benefits forms, transport, funeral-related referrals or school communication.
  3. Offer communication assistance or an accessible support plan without imposing a grief timetable or exposure exercises.
  4. Coordinate specialized bereavement or trauma care when desired or indicated, and help people maintain valued connections.
  5. Review relief from administrative burden, meaningful participation, autonomy and social support.

When to coordinate qualified care

Suicidality, prolonged severe impairment or other acute risk requires qualified clinical assessment.
