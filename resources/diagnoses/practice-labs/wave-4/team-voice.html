When several teams are involved

Help a young person shape decisions involving caregivers, school and behavioral-health supports—without making every disagreement a diagnosis or treating one team’s decision as another team’s consent.

Safety is not a bargaining point. If threats, abuse, exploitation, severe aggression or inability to remain safe are suspected, qualified professionals follow appropriate safeguarding and emergency procedures first. Do not use joint mediation or a collaborative worksheet to resolve coercion or unsafe power differences.

Four perspectives, one accountable follow-up

Each has a different purpose. They are not equivalent votes and must not be blended into a single adult-authored “team goal.”

01

What the young person wants understood

Their description, strengths, unwanted pressure, preferred communication and who they want to participate. Do not turn silence or nonattendance into agreement.

02

What caregivers can support

Safety, ordinary routines, accessible expectations, positive relationships and caregiver needs. Do not assign blame or assume adults speak for the child.

03

What the school can change

Learning and communication access, respectful response to incidents, appropriate qualified functional assessment and learning supports. School and CFT procedures are separate.

04

What the team can coordinate

Case-manager follow-through, lawful information sharing, specialist or medical review when indicated, and a named owner for each agreed action.

Choose a question the group is ready to discuss

This optional selector only shows a different explanation. It does not record an answer, give permission to share information or indicate that a goal was agreed.

Youth contribution

Invite youth-friendly participation or preferences collected accessibly and lawfully. Record exactly what the youth says and what remains unknown; a missed meeting is not consent.

Safeguarding first

Separate possible harm, threats, coercion or intimidation from ordinary disagreement. Qualified staff follow urgent safeguarding procedures; no forced joint mediation or compelled apology.

Different roles, different authority

A CFT, school IEP team, informal family meeting and clinical encounter are not interchangeable. Identify which entity may make the actual decision before asking anyone to agree.

Follow-through without blame

Distinguish proposed, agreed, assigned, attempted and completed actions. Give the youth a chance to correct the summary; do not record an unverified school or provider contact as complete.

Fictional example: a team meeting without a shared story

A middle-school student has repeatedly left class during writing tasks. School staff describe refusal; a caregiver worries about escalating arguments at home. The student reports that the writing demand and an earlier peer interaction feel overwhelming and wants adults to stop discussing them in front of classmates. In a prior planning conversation, adults agreed on a goal before asking the student.

What changes: The facilitator asks how the student would like to participate and separately documents the student’s account, family priorities, possible access needs and any independent safety concerns. The school reviews educational supports through its authorized process. The behavioral-health team considers clinical needs through its own process. Authorized coordinators identify who owns each follow-up. Nobody infers that the student agreed simply because adults attended.

Use these six moves

  1. Ask the young person how they want their perspective included: present, accompanied, written, drawn, AAC, interpreter, separate meeting, or through another lawful accessible route.
  2. Identify the young person’s stated goal, what matters to family or caregivers, and areas where views genuinely differ. Keep their words separate.
  3. Assess safety and coercion independently, including who can leave or disagree without consequences; route risk to qualified safeguarding and clinical staff.
  4. Describe observable situations and multiple explanations (task access, communication, pain, sleep, peer conflict, neurodevelopment, trauma or adult responses) without diagnosing from a single incident.
  5. Identify the relevant decision process: school support/FBA/IEP, clinical treatment and CFT service planning each has different roles, records and legal requirements.
  6. Agree on no more than a few authorized actions, ownership and confirmation; then invite youth and caregivers to correct the summary and review burden and participation.

Four distinctions worth keeping in view

Who decides?

The youth’s preference and developmentally appropriate participation matter. Legal guardian, agency and school authority still require separate verification.

What is safe?

If imminent violence, abuse, severe threats or exploitation are possible, use qualified protection/clinical pathways; do not use this tool to negotiate safety.

What can be shared?

Confirm the applicable HIPAA, FERPA, Part 2, guardian and agency rules before involving external professionals or exchanging records.

What was actually done?

Name one authorized person, the next concrete action, and a safe way to confirm it without implying that a referral or school change already occurred.

Two optional printable pages

My part of the conversation

Different roles, one honest follow-up

Fill out only through authorized, confidential channels. A proposed action is not a completed action; do not include identifying information on an unprotected public webpage.

1. Safety questions sent to qualified review, if indicated
2. Young person’s view and any disagreement not resolved
3. School action and who is authorized to consider it
4. Care coordinator, agreed next action and permitted information sharing
5. Who checks what happened, when and whether the youth agrees the summary is fair
