Depression: capacity, meaning and activity

Plan meaningful action without mistaking depleted energy, disability or social constraints for insufficient motivation.

Before useScreening and this worksheet do not determine depression severity or safety. Clinicians must assess suicidality directly and evaluate episodes of activation or decreased need for sleep for a bipolar differential. Pain, medications, medical conditions, trauma and economic constraints can limit activity. Behavioral activation is an evidence-based treatment when delivered with suitable training; the page is not a standalone protocol.

Distinctions that change the next step

Small is not trivial

A five-minute action can be a meaningful trial when chosen and feasible. More tasks are not automatically better treatment.

External barriers count

A ride, caregiver support, flexible timing or service access may matter more than another reminder.

Safety is not a worksheet field

Marked deterioration, suicidality, mania-like activation or inability to meet needs requires trained evaluation.

Use this collaboratively in a session

  1. Begin with the person's preferred outcome rather than a deficit list.
  2. Check current safety, bipolar history and relevant health concerns through appropriate clinical assessment.
  3. Separate capacity from meaning: something can be important while currently inaccessible.
  4. Identify one previously rewarding, connecting or mastery-related activity—and whether it still suits the person's values.
  5. Map one obstacle and one adaptation, not an obligation to push through it.
  6. Invite the person to choose a trial, a rest plan, practical support or a decision to defer.
  7. Review observed experience, pleasure/meaning, feasibility and downstream burden without treating noncompletion as noncompliance.
Illustrative fictional example. Fictional example: A caregiver who has stopped seeing friends wants connection but has limited transportation and pain. A supported short phone call may be more feasible than insisting on an outing. Progress is the person's reported connection and ability to repeat an acceptable option—not the number of tasks checked off.

This is an original educational visual facilitation aid. It is not a standardized measure, a diagnostic algorithm or a substitute for condition-specific treatment training.

Two printable working sheets

Print blank pages and complete them together on paper. Do not enter client information into site search or shareable URLs. No local storage, form submission or analytics are used by these pages.

Working sheet 01 · Name and date optional on paper

Capacity and value map

  1. What matters to me right now?

  2. My current energy, pain, safety and access limits

  3. One activity that might offer connection, pleasure or a sense of agency

  4. Why the activity has become difficult

  5. A support or smaller version that preserves my goal

  6. My preferred next step—including rest or postponement

Working sheet 02 · Review with the person's own words

Observe and adjust, rather than grade performance

  1. The action or support we selected together

  2. What I expected might happen

  3. What actually felt different, if anything

  4. Was the cost reasonable and were my limits respected?

  5. What should stay, change or be dropped?

  6. Any new safety, medical or specialist care concern to bring to the team?
