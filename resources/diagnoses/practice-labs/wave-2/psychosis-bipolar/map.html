What question should we explore first?

Only a topic selection is made; no personal text is entered, stored or sent.

Episode chronology

What might be happening?

Baseline, onset, duration, change, recovery

What needs distinguishing?

When did sleep need, mood, activity, unusual experiences, medications or substances change? What is observed versus inferred?

A possible next connection

Seek specialist/psychiatric assessment for a diagnosis; new severe activation, confusion or danger needs urgent professional evaluation.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

Experience and distress

What might be happening?

Perceptions, voices, beliefs, meaning and burden

What needs distinguishing?

What is the experience like in the person's own words? Does it feel distressing, useful, neutral or frightening?

A possible next connection

Respond respectfully, explore negotiated coping and support, without affirming or debating contested beliefs.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

Recovery goals

What might be happening?

Relationships, routine, work, rest, autonomy

What needs distinguishing?

What matters to this person beyond symptom elimination? Which access, side-effect or social barriers limit participation?

A possible next connection

Co-design supports and coordinate indicated treatments through an authorized care team.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

Safety and urgent needs

What might be happening?

Risk, acute confusion, inability to meet needs

What needs distinguishing?

Is immediate medical/specialist help indicated? Are delirium, substances, neurological or other medical causes possible?

A possible next connection

Follow clinical emergency/safeguarding procedures; this visual aid is not a risk score or crisis plan.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

Follow through

Choose a blank two-page working sheet if helpful; never place identifying clinical details in a public site's search or URL.
