Adults and qualified clinicians; chosen supporters with consent

Episodes and recovery: two kinds of evidence

Keep the clinical chronology of episodes distinct from the person's present-day experiences, needs and recovery goals.

Collaborative educational aid, not a test. Acute confusion, serious risk, inability to meet basic needs or severe activation require qualified assessment. This worksheet does not determine diagnosis, crisis status or medication decisions. The page does not store or transmit responses.

Four distinct lenses

Lens 1

Episode chronology

Baseline, start, duration, mood and sleep changes, between-episode function

Lens 2

Differential context

Medical, substance, medication, trauma and cultural context

Lens 3

Recovery priorities

Distress, meaning, rest, work, relationships and personal choice

Lens 4

Shared response

Urgent assessment when needed, specialty treatment and daily access support

A shared-thinking structure, not a stage ladder, diagnostic instrument or numerical score.

Fictional case for discussion

A fictional adult describes hearing a voice while sleeping poorly and struggling at work. Others offer competing labels. The clinician needs a dated history of mood, sleep, functioning, medical conditions, substances and medications; the person wants meaningful support now.

Invented example, not an observation or treatment outcome.

Six concrete conversation moves

  1. Ask which problem the person wants help with, without requiring agreement with a diagnosis.
  2. Develop a timeline separating direct observation from self-report.
  3. Differentiate insomnia with exhaustion from reduced need for sleep with activation.
  4. Seek indicated medical or psychiatric assessment; obtain collateral under lawful consent.
  5. Choose practical supports while respecting the person's boundaries and preferred language.
  6. Name a review contact, triggers for clinical reassessment and the person's right to revisit.

Distinctions that change the next action

An unusual experience occurs

It alone cannot establish a psychotic or mood disorder.

Activation and sleep change

Marked change can require urgent clinical review, not just a worksheet.

A relative wants information

Use consent and applicable confidentiality rules; do not presume sharing rights.
