Use this material collaboratively

A clinical chronology of mood, sleep, unusual experiences and functioning is different from a person-led map of distress, daily participation and chosen supports. Use both without turning the diagram into a diagnosis.

Keep professional roles clear. Medical or diagnostic decisions, specialist therapies, safeguarding, consent and eligibility rules are handled through the appropriate qualified pathway. These exercises are optional visual supports.

Seven moves for a session

  1. Ask what the person wants from the meeting, including communication and support-person preferences.
  2. Assess acute safety and medical or substance causes through suitable professional protocols before using the map.
  3. Construct a neutral timeline separating baseline, changes, timing, recollections and observed facts.
  4. Keep unusual experiences in the person's own words rather than using leading or confrontational questions.
  5. On a separate track, identify meaningful recovery outcomes, environmental supports and strengths.
  6. Coordinate medication questions, specialist treatment and care-team communication within professional and consent boundaries.
  7. Review what the person found helpful, burdensome or inaccurate; modify the plan without coercion.

Distinctions that change the plan

Psychosis, bipolar disorder, substance effects, medical illness and mood symptoms require differential assessment.

Reduced need for sleep during activation differs from insomnia and fragmented sleep.

Respectful validation of distress does not require affirming contested interpretations.

No choice on this page excludes urgent assessment or authorizes medication changes.

Illustrative fictional example

A fictional adult reports markedly reduced sleep and disturbing messages in ordinary sounds. A clinician assesses urgency and medical/substance factors, documents a non-leading chronology and offers the person's chosen quiet space and prescriber contact. The map does not prove mania or psychosis.

The scenario is invented and not a claim of effectiveness. Ask which assumptions the person might challenge.
