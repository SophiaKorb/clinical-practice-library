Overview / Case-to-action workshop

Adults, qualified SUD clinicians and authorized coordinators

Substance use: safety, choice and privacy

Do not collapse medical safety, person-chosen change goals, care access and lawful disclosure into one readiness label.

Collaborative educational aid, not a test. Potential dangerous withdrawal, severe intoxication or overdose warrants medical/emergency care. This page cannot determine detoxification, level of care, prescriptions or legal consent. The page does not store or transmit responses.

Four distinct lenses

Lens 1

Medical safety

Overdose, intoxication or withdrawal concerns call for appropriate professional care

Lens 2

Person-defined goal

Explore values, uncertainty, reduction or abstinence without imposed stages

Lens 3

Treatment access

Medical and psychological choices, transport, cost and follow-up

Lens 4

Information rights

Which records, recipients, purposes and consents govern actual disclosure?

A shared-thinking structure, not a stage ladder, diagnostic instrument or numerical score.

Fictional case for discussion

A fictional adult wants fewer alcohol-related work problems but worries about confidentiality. An immediate residential-care suggestion bypasses medical evaluation, the person's goals, available options and the rules for sharing protected records.

Invented example, not an observation or treatment outcome.

Six concrete conversation moves

  1. Ask permission to discuss the person's goal and reflect without coercion.
  2. Clarify whether medical symptoms require emergency or supervised assessment.
  3. Distinguish the person's preferred changes from a helper's proposed outcome.
  4. Offer care options and practical access coordination, including co-occurring needs.
  5. Ask authorized staff to confirm applicable Part 2/HIPAA consent and disclosure requirements.
  6. Name a handoff owner, confirm completion appropriately and schedule a choice-based review.

Distinctions that change the next action

Possible overdose or withdrawal

Qualified medical evaluation takes priority; no home-detox instructions here.

Mixed motivation

Do not assign a readiness score or force abstinence.

Records may be shared

Generic consent assumptions do not establish 42 CFR Part 2 permissions.
