Bereaved people, supporters and clinicians adapting to age and culture

Choose a path to discuss

Grief has no required sequence. A person may need ritual, connection, quiet, help with responsibilities, or specialist support. Remembrance and daily living can coexist without forcing acceptance or a timetable.

Different paths may coexist. Select any path and change your mind at any time. Nothing here establishes severity or diagnosis.

What question should we explore first?

Only a topic selection is made; no personal text is entered, stored or sent.

Remembrance

What might be happening?

Connection, memories, rituals and emotion

What needs distinguishing?

Does the person want to remember, talk, engage with a tradition, sit quietly or avoid an exercise today?

A possible next connection

Offer the chosen private or shared remembrance opportunity without obligatory disclosure.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

Everyday life

What might be happening?

Practical roles, work, rest and daily support

What needs distinguishing?

What responsibilities changed? What feels inaccessible because of fatigue, paperwork, financial pressure or isolation?

A possible next connection

Identify one practical accommodation or helpful person without framing it as 'moving on'.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

Culture and belonging

What might be happening?

Family, religious or community expectations

What needs distinguishing?

Which cultural or spiritual practices fit? Which expectations feel unwelcome?

A possible next connection

Respect chosen practices, language, timing and privacy, including opting out.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

When further help is wanted

What might be happening?

Persistent impairment, trauma, depression or risk

What needs distinguishing?

What is the context, severity, duration and cultural meaning? Could a different condition be contributing?

A possible next connection

Offer qualified differential assessment or indicated grief-focused treatment, not a label from this sheet.

Discuss with the person. The choices are examples, not individual clinical recommendations.

Follow through

Choose a blank two-page working sheet if helpful; never place identifying clinical details in a public site's search or URL.
