Practical shared-thinking collections

Learning, psychosis/bipolar, substance use and grief call for different decisions and different support paths. Each collection includes an accessible visual choice map, facilitator guide and two blank worksheets.

Learning disorders

Determine whether reading words, number and mathematics, written expression, or inaccessible task demands are blocking participation. This map is neither a quick screen nor a diagnosis.

Psychosis / bipolar

A clinical chronology of mood, sleep, unusual experiences and functioning is different from a person-led map of distress, daily participation and chosen supports. Use both without turning the diagram into a diagnosis.

Substance use

A medical safety issue, a person-led goal, a treatment-access question and a privacy concern are different starting points. Support reflection without assigning an abstinence goal or a readiness stage.

Grief / bereavement

Grief has no required sequence. A person may need ritual, connection, quiet, help with responsibilities, or specialist support. Remembrance and daily living can coexist without forcing acceptance or a timetable.

Choose appropriately
