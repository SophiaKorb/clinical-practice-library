Keep professional roles clear. Medical or diagnostic decisions, specialist therapies, safeguarding, consent and eligibility rules are handled through the appropriate qualified pathway. These exercises are optional visual supports.

Seven moves for a session

  1. Ask which activity the learner wants to accomplish and which part is difficult; offer multiple communication modes.
  2. Distinguish reading, math, writing and access demands instead of inferring a global skill deficit.
  3. Check instruction, language exposure, sensory/medical needs, fatigue, culture and opportunity to learn.
  4. Compare what the person demonstrates with read-aloud, dictation, explicit modeling or manipulatives.
  5. Agree on a feasible accommodation now and a distinct teaching/evaluation question.
  6. Assign instructional and coordination tasks only to professionals within their roles.
  7. Review what the learner can now do, burden and whether the selected supports remain acceptable.

Distinctions that change the plan

Reading, mathematics and writing have different evidence-based instructional recommendations.

A school eligibility decision, formal psychological diagnosis and classroom accommodation are not interchangeable.

An informal visual game cannot diagnose dyslexia, written-language disorder or a vision problem.

Adequate instruction and linguistic context must be examined without delaying access support.

Illustrative fictional example

A fictional student explains science well aloud but writes little. The team distinguishes spelling, transcription, idea organization, hand fatigue and instruction. Dictation provides access while an appropriate writing evaluation and teaching plan are pursued.

The scenario is invented and not a claim of effectiveness. Ask which assumptions the person might challenge.
