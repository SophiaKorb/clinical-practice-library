Four distinct lenses

Lens 1

Task demand

Which part involves reading, language, number, spelling, ideas or motor output?

Lens 2

Access

Lens 3

Teaching

Which specific skill would benefit from explicit instruction by an educator?

Lens 4

Evaluation

What developmental, sensory, linguistic and instructional evidence remains missing?

A shared-thinking structure, not a stage ladder, diagnostic instrument or numerical score.

Fictional case for discussion

A fictional student explains science accurately aloud but writes very little. Dictation helps the student communicate an idea; it does not establish whether handwriting, spelling, language, reading, fatigue or instruction is the main barrier.

Invented example, not an observation or treatment outcome.

Six concrete conversation moves

  1. Invite the learner to choose one meaningful task and describe the hard step.
  2. Observe reading, math, writing and access separately; avoid global ability labels.
  3. Offer two accessible ways to show the same knowledge and record exactly what changed.
  4. Separate immediate accommodation from a teaching intervention and a formal diagnosis.
  5. Name who will provide classroom access and who will coordinate evaluation or teaching.
  6. Review participation and the learner's preferences, not only task speed.

Distinctions that change the next action

Dictation helps

It reduces transcription demands but does not prove a diagnosis.

Reading aloud helps

It may reveal content knowledge while word-reading still requires separate evidence.

A number line helps

Access may improve while mathematics instruction remains necessary.
