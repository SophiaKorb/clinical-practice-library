Adult ADHD: assessment and formulation

A clinical interview should establish history, pattern, impairment and competing explanations—not simply count endorsed symptoms.

A usable assessment sequence

Source basis: AADPA assessment and diagnostic guidance and co-occurring conditions and differential diagnosis. Interview, rating scales and collateral information contribute different kinds of evidence; none replaces clinical synthesis.

  1. Referral question and preferences: what prompted evaluation now, what outcomes matter and how should feedback be delivered?
  2. Developmental course: examine childhood onset, changing demands, school/occupation, compensations and histories of support. Invite collateral with consent, never treat missing school records as proof of absence.
  3. Current symptom picture: attention, hyperactivity/internal restlessness, impulsivity, working memory, sequencing and task-switching. Record specific examples, frequency and contexts.
  4. Functional consequence: education/work, home, finances, relationships, driving and health management. Ask what skills and support make a difference.
  5. Differential/coexistence: anxiety, depression, episodic mood change, trauma/dissociation, autism/learning needs, sleep, pain, medication or substance effects, medical causes.
  6. Evidence integration: review contradictions, cultural and contextual factors, risk, current impairment and follow-up questions; do not turn one scale into a diagnosis.
  7. Collaborative feedback: explain certainty and limits, available care, reasonable accommodations and what findings would change the formulation.

Diagnostic and tool boundaries

DSM-5-TR adult symptom thresholds and age-of-onset requirements are interpreted by qualified professionals using an appropriate clinical assessment. Brief screeners (including the ASRS) can inform triage or discussion, but do not diagnose. A structured diagnostic interview can support comprehensive evaluation; cognitive or neuropsychological testing is not routinely required for straightforward ADHD diagnosis. Only use measures as licensed and trained; do not reproduce proprietary items.

Example formulation structure

Predisposing/developmental: longstanding attention/activation pattern and learning environment. Current triggers: change in workload, support loss, stress or sleep. Maintaining loop: unclear task → avoidance/overwhelm → lost time → shame → higher future start cost. Protective: strengths, preferred supports, relationships, values and access. Uncertainties: what evidence is missing and what would discriminate explanations?

Translate this formulation into measurable, person-chosen treatment targets.

Clinical sources and evidence limits

NICE NG87: ADHD diagnosis and management (UK, applicable guideline version and updates); AADPA: Cognitive-behavioural interventions; AADPA: Multimodal treatment and support; NIMH: ADHD in adults; JAN: Workplace accommodations.
