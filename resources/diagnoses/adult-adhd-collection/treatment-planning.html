Clinician guide · Function and quality of life

Adult ADHD: collaborative treatment planning

Build a plan around what the adult wants to change, what might block access, and how both benefit and burden will be reviewed.

Plan anatomy

  1. Target: describe a person-chosen daily-life outcome, not “improve compliance.”
  2. Baseline: record current functioning and the cost of getting there (time, stress, help, money).
  3. Hypothesis: name plausible executive, emotional, environmental and medical contributors; document uncertainty.
  4. Intervention: identify who does what in or between sessions and why it follows from the formulation.
  5. Measurement: record outcome, quality of life, effort, accessibility and adverse effects.
  6. Review: keep, modify, stop or refer based on evidence and preference.

Worked examples (fictional, adapt clinically)

Appointment access

Goal: attend chosen health appointments with manageable distress and less dependence on others. Intervention: collaborative workflow map, one capture system, departure rehearsal, accessible reminders and coordination with consent. Measure: attended/rescheduled by choice, prompt burden, stress and autonomy; review after 2–4 weeks.

Emotional recovery

Goal: reduce prolonged conflict after stressful transitions. Intervention: functional chain, somatic cue awareness, agreed pause-and-return script, cognitive restructuring or skills rehearsal as indicated. Measure: participant-described intensity, time to repair, safety and relationship experience.

Finances / administrative tasks

Goal: complete one chosen essential administrative process without avoidable fees. Intervention: break down actual form, accessibility review, plan first action, test one weekly processing cue. Measure: completed necessary action, total time, distress, cost and sustainability.

Do not interpret these as ADHD outcome measures

Timeliness, perfect task completion or another person's satisfaction alone. Consider standardized symptom tracking in context when appropriate, but functional and quality-of-life outcomes need separate attention. Complex comorbidities and safety changes may require changing the clinical plan rather than escalating reminder intensity.

Clinical sources and evidence limits

NICE NG87: ADHD diagnosis and management (UK, applicable guideline version and updates); AADPA: Cognitive-behavioural interventions; AADPA: Multimodal treatment and support; NIMH: ADHD in adults; JAN: Workplace accommodations.
