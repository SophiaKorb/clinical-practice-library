Shared responsibilities and support agreements

I want help without feeling controlled, criticized or treated like a child.

Start with the client's choice. This activity supports practical planning and coordination. It is not a diagnostic assessment, medication protocol or psychotherapy. Ask permission before contacting another person or service.

What may be making this difficult?

  • Missed commitments are interpreted as not caring, even when the barrier is memory or planning.
  • One partner may become the manager of all reminders and tasks.
  • Constant checking can erode autonomy or worsen conflict.

These are hypotheses to explore, not explanations to impose. Ask what is already working.

Use this in a case-management visit

  1. Ask each involved adult, separately or collaboratively as appropriate, what problem they want solved and what support is welcome.
  2. Map a shared task into ownership, information needed, cues and fallback; avoid assuming one person is responsible for the other's executive functioning.
  3. Practice a request and repair script: what happened, impact, needed support and next agreement.
  4. Choose a two-week experiment with explicit stop conditions, boundaries and privacy controls.
  5. Review both client benefit and support-person burden; refer to couples/family therapy if relational treatment is necessary.

Follow-through and documentation

Coordination boundary: Case management can support scheduling and resource coordination but should not mediate coercive relationships or provide therapy outside scope.

What to measure: Equitable responsibility, perceived control, reduced conflict and sustainability.

Ask the client: Did this make the situation easier? What did it cost you? Do you want to keep, change or discard this support?

Practice basis and limitations

The practical workflows here are original educational case-management aids, not separately validated ADHD treatments. They emphasize person-centered coordination and environmental adaptations informed by NICE NG87, AHRQ shared care planning, and AHRQ care coordination. For workplace examples see the Job Accommodation Network. Verify all program criteria and laws before making referrals.

Use within your professional role and supervision requirements. Check consent, privacy, accessibility, rights, local agency policy, and current eligibility. Do not enter identifying client information into public tools.
