Facilitator guide · Case-management role

How to make a session useful

A case-management visit should reduce a concrete barrier, not merely send someone home with another list of things to remember.

A workable 25-minute structure

  1. 3 minutes — Permission and direction: “What matters most to work on today?” Confirm preferred communication mode, sensory conditions and the client's right to choose.
  2. 5 minutes — Locate the problem: Describe one example in ordinary terms. Distinguish task start, remembering, sequence, time, environment, paperwork, finances and emotional/medical concerns.
  3. 10 minutes — Do together: Make the call, rehearse the script, organize the blank checklist, find verified agency information or practice the first step. Avoid doing for the client what they want to do themselves.
  4. 4 minutes — Transfer: Leave one accessible artifact or agreed cue (paper note, appointment card, shared plan). Ask the client to explain the next step in their own preferred way to evaluate whether the staff explanation made sense.
  5. 3 minutes — Handoff and follow-up: Agree on who will do what and by when. Document client voice, consent, observed participation and unresolved barriers in the approved system.

Support versus treatment: role boundaries

Usually appropriate within approved case-management scopeRefer or coordinate for qualified services
Resource navigation, appointments, practical organization, rehearsal, advocacy, environmental adjustments, follow-through and authorized care coordination.Diagnosing ADHD, interpreting diagnostic tests, prescribing or changing medication, psychotherapy, specialized trauma work, legal representation and crisis assessment.
Help clarify the client's question to a clinician; support obtaining instructions and safe pharmacy logistics.Medication adverse effects, acute activation, suicidality, intoxication/withdrawal, psychiatric emergencies and medical deterioration.
Offer accommodations and referral pathways; verify current program contact and client preference.Eligibility determinations, disability certification, benefits appeals requiring specialized representation and school law conclusions.

Agency policy, training and supervision determine actual scope and billable activities. A worksheet is not evidence that a covered service occurred.

Interaction principles

  • Do not call a client “noncompliant” because an inaccessible or unwanted system failed.
  • Offer real choices and check whether the client wants another person involved. Revisit consent as circumstances change.
  • Prefer one trusted support to four competing reminder apps; check language, literacy, sensory and financial access.
  • For young people, protect meaningful youth involvement and follow applicable guardian/consent requirements.
  • Keep confidential documents and client answers in approved systems, not on public worksheets.
  • Measure participation, agency, burden and client-defined outcomes; not only employee or caregiver convenience.

Example progress documentation (not a clinical note template)

Client-defined purpose: wants a more reliable way to reach a chosen appointment. Observed collaboration: client selected paper cue, practiced reverse-planning travel and identified transportation constraint. Case-manager action: reviewed options and identified a consented referral for transportation assistance. Plan: client to try chosen cue; worker to check verified transportation eligibility and report back. Outcome status: strategy not yet tested; no claim of successful attendance.
