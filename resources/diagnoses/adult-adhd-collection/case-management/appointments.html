Appointments and transportation

I want to make and get to the appointments that matter to me.

Start with the client's choice. This activity supports practical planning and coordination. It is not a diagnostic assessment, medication protocol or psychotherapy. Ask permission before contacting another person or service.
UnderstandDo togetherPrint worksheetReview

What may be making this difficult?

  • The appointment exists in several calendars, or nowhere accessible.
  • Travel time is mistaken for departure time; documents and transit are missing.
  • Phone calls, portals, copays, transportation and unfamiliar offices create separate barriers.

These are hypotheses to explore, not explanations to impose. Ask what is already working.

Use this in a case-management visit

  1. Ask the client to choose an actual upcoming appointment or a fictional example. Never enter identifying details into the public tool.
  2. Draw a journey with six stations: book → confirm → prepare → leave → attend → follow up. Mark the first station that tends to fail.
  3. Rehearse one real action: locating the office phone, practicing a call, setting a departure reminder, or writing down the follow-up question.
  4. Offer a reminder mode the client controls (paper, phone, calendar, trusted person). Check what privacy or cost it adds.
  5. Make a fallback plan for missed transport or rescheduling without blame.

Follow-through and documentation

Coordination boundary: Ask permission before contacting providers or transportation services; document whether the client initiated the request, what coordination was agreed, and unresolved barriers.

What to measure: Appointments attended or purposefully rescheduled; missed-connection burden; client's independence and distress.

Ask the client: Did this make the situation easier? What did it cost you? Do you want to keep, change or discard this support?

Practice basis and limitations

The practical workflows here are original educational case-management aids, not separately validated ADHD treatments. They emphasize person-centered coordination and environmental adaptations informed by NICE NG87, AHRQ shared care planning, and AHRQ care coordination. For workplace examples see the Job Accommodation Network. Verify all program criteria and laws before making referrals.

Use within your professional role and supervision requirements. Check consent, privacy, accessibility, rights, local agency policy, and current eligibility. Do not enter identifying client information into public tools.
