Adult ADHD: practical case-management toolkit

Choose a real-life barrier, work through it together, and leave with one useful next step. Twelve concrete activities and printable client worksheets for organizing services, improving access, and building independence without removing choice.

How to use the toolkit

Begin with the client's goal, not their diagnosis. A person may benefit from these supports regardless of whether they have ADHD. Choose one module, practice a step during the visit, give the client an accessible copy if wanted, and review usefulness rather than rewarding perfect task completion.

For teens and families

Open the youth ADHD case-management, school and CFT guide. Includes participation options, school barrier questions, youth-authored meeting prompts, caregiver coordination, transition-age planning and a printable youth-voice page.

The modules are primarily written for adults. For adolescents, include the young person's own participation and permission/consent requirements, collaborate appropriately with caregivers, and adapt reading level and school contexts. Do not use the adult pages as a substitute for legally required child and family team participation or developmentally appropriate planning.

Evidence and boundaries

These are original functional support and case-management materials, not independent clinical trials or a manualized ADHD psychotherapy protocol. NICE NG87 addresses ADHD care planning, environmental modifications and support; AHRQ supports patient-centered shared care plans; JAN offers workplace accommodation examples. This toolkit does not determine diagnosis, legal entitlements, medication, crisis response or billable service scope.
