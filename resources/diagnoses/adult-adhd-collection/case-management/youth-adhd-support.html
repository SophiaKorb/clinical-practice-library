Children and teens · Case-management and school coordination

ADHD support that includes the young person

A youth's care plan should not be a private agreement among adults about how to manage the youth. Find out what the child or teen wants, what school and home demand, and what access supports would be acceptable.

Role boundaries. Use age-appropriate communication, required guardian/consent rules and agency policies. Support school and service navigation; do not diagnose ADHD, certify an IEP or 504 plan, provide untrained psychotherapy, or presume youth assent to adult-selected interventions.

1 · Prepare for a CFT or school meeting

  1. Ask the youth how they would prefer to participate: speak, write, select pictures, use a support person, send questions ahead, or contribute another accessible way.
  2. Ask what is going well and what adults misunderstand. Present two or three possible goals and let the youth add or reject options.
  3. With appropriate authorization, coordinate the school, caregiver, clinician and other invited supports. Confirm who is responsible for the actual team process and meeting notice.
  4. Practice a sentence or visual card: “The hardest part is ____; I want adults to try ____; please stop ____.”
  5. After the meeting, check what the youth heard, whether they believe their concerns were represented, and which commitments actually belong to whom.

AHCCCS youth involvement practice tool · AHCCCS CFT practice tool. Check current policies and requirements rather than treating the original tools as an exhaustive current legal determination.

2 · Translate school problems into access questions

What school task is hard?
What support might change access?
What can student and school verify?
  • Missing work: distinguish whether the difficulty is understanding instructions, bringing materials home, starting, writing, sustained attention or turning work in.
  • Transitions: explore advance warning, stable visual schedule, hall congestion, sensory conditions and predictable handoff.
  • Frequent correction: request observable triggers, instruction format and supports; do not presume defiance or require a punishment plan.
  • Social conflict: establish safety, bullying context, peer relationships and disability access rather than treating every event as an ADHD behavior.
  • School eligibility: case manager can help the family request evaluation and collect concerns; the school applies IDEA/Section 504 procedures and eligibility standards.

CPL school clinical ADHD support guide · U.S. Department of Education ADHD and Section 504 resources · CDC ADHD in the classroom.

3 · A family-home experiment without coercion

  1. Let the child select one wanted routine: getting ready, a preferred responsibility, preparing for an activity, organizing a backpack or sharing a leisure plan.
  2. Find the step that is hard; ask caregivers what time and materials are actually available.
  3. Offer a visible sequence, fewer instructions at once, point-of-use supplies, movement or sensory breaks, or a model-and-practice approach.
  4. Agree on what kind of reminder is welcome; do not use shaming, withholding basic needs or humiliating public charts.
  5. Review the child's experience as well as caregiver convenience, safety, effort and relationship cost.

4 · Transition-age youth: support self-direction

For older adolescents, rehearse skills in contexts they choose: requesting refills from a qualified team, preparing questions for a visit, keeping access to documents, transportation, education/employment navigation and knowing who to contact when something changes. Build supported decision making rather than abruptly replacing caregiver management with zero support.

AHCCCS transition-age youth identifies early transition planning as part of its behavioral health process. Check actual coverage and eligibility before referral.

Clinical and ethical note

Co-occurring anxiety, autism, trauma, sleep, learning differences and family stress may require additional evaluation. When there are threats, self-harm concerns, abuse or acute safety issues, follow qualified safety and safeguarding procedures rather than using an organization worksheet as an intervention.
