Starting, sequencing and finishing tasks

I want to start or finish something important without a lecture about discipline.

Start with the client's choice. This activity supports practical planning and coordination. It is not a diagnostic assessment, medication protocol or psychotherapy. Ask permission before contacting another person or service.
UnderstandDo togetherPrint worksheetReview

What may be making this difficult?

  • The first physical action is invisible or the task requires tools not yet available.
  • Perfectionism, uncertainty or distress can maintain avoidance alongside executive difficulty.
  • Finishing includes cleanup, submission or transition that may never get planned.

These are hypotheses to explore, not explanations to impose. Ask what is already working.

Use this in a case-management visit

  1. Ask the client to show or describe a current wanted task; define success in their words.
  2. Use a three-column map: before I can begin / first action / how I know it's finished.
  3. Identify the first accessible action that takes roughly two minutes or less; adjust if it still feels impossible.
  4. Offer body doubling, visual steps, supplies at point of use, or a single follow-up cue, but let the client choose.
  5. Rehearse the first step together and review its cost; do not equate task completion with consent to ongoing monitoring.

Follow-through and documentation

Coordination boundary: Document observable participation, agreed support and barriers—not 'noncompliance' or diagnostic inference.

What to measure: Task started/completed by choice, effort, avoidance burden and confidence.

Ask the client: Did this make the situation easier? What did it cost you? Do you want to keep, change or discard this support?

Practice basis and limitations

The practical workflows here are original educational case-management aids, not separately validated ADHD treatments. They emphasize person-centered coordination and environmental adaptations informed by NICE NG87, AHRQ shared care planning, and AHRQ care coordination. For workplace examples see the Job Accommodation Network. Verify all program criteria and laws before making referrals.
