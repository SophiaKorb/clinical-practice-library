Time estimation and leaving on time

I want to arrive when I choose without relying on constant last-minute pressure.

Start with the client's choice. This activity supports practical planning and coordination. It is not a diagnostic assessment, medication protocol or psychotherapy. Ask permission before contacting another person or service.
UnderstandDo togetherPrint worksheetReview

What may be making this difficult?

  • Events start at one time but require travel, preparation and transition before that.
  • Task duration is estimated optimistically; interruptions and fatigue are ignored.
  • Reminders may fire at the wrong place or become background noise.

These are hypotheses to explore, not explanations to impose. Ask what is already working.

Use this in a case-management visit

  1. Start with a real nonidentifying timeline: arrival time at right, then travel, buffer, dressing, searching for supplies, and ending the previous activity.
  2. Use three previous examples to estimate duration; separate fixed steps from variable steps.
  3. Place the departure trigger at the physical transition: 'put shoes on' rather than 'be ready'.
  4. Rehearse the transition, including what to stop doing and what to do when running late.
  5. Review whether the chosen cues lower burden; do not add timers indefinitely.

Follow-through and documentation

Coordination boundary: If lateness threatens employment, benefits, or treatment access, coordinate reasonable supports with client permission.

What to measure: Desired arrival/replan outcomes, stress, number of prompts and feasibility.

Ask the client: Did this make the situation easier? What did it cost you? Do you want to keep, change or discard this support?

Practice basis and limitations

The practical workflows here are original educational case-management aids, not separately validated ADHD treatments. They emphasize person-centered coordination and environmental adaptations informed by NICE NG87, AHRQ shared care planning, and AHRQ care coordination. For workplace examples see the Job Accommodation Network. Verify all program criteria and laws before making referrals.
