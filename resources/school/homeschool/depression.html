Homeschool Barrier → Support

Depression

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

A problem-first guide for learning at home. Start with the task that is hard, not the diagnosis. Pick one or two barriers, change a small number of things, and watch what happens over time.

Barrier 1

Low energy and slowed thinking make learning harder

What this may look like at home: The child works much more slowly, tires quickly, seems mentally foggy, or cannot sustain the previous workload.

Try first

  • Reduce unnecessary volume while preserving key learning goals.
  • Use shorter work periods and visible stopping points.
  • Schedule harder work during the child’s best energy window.

Track: Note sleep, energy, pace, appetite, physical complaints, and time of day.

Consider referral when

Medical and mental-health evaluation is warranted when low energy/mood persists, represents a clear change, or affects daily functioning. Rule out medical and sleep contributors.

Barrier 2

Starting and finishing work is hard

What this may look like at home: The child knows what to do but cannot initiate, remains in bed/room, or leaves many tasks unfinished.

Try first

  • Set one very small first action.
  • Use body-doubling or brief shared starts without taking over the work.
  • Prioritize a few essential tasks instead of building a backlog.

Track: Record initiation time, prompting, and whether starting improves after activity or connection.

Consider referral when

Refer when loss of initiation is broad, persistent, or accompanies sustained low mood, hopelessness, or marked functional decline.

Barrier 3

Focus and memory are reduced

What this may look like at home: The child forgets recently learned material, loses place, or cannot concentrate as before.

Try first

  • Keep directions visible and reduce multistep memory load.
  • Use retrieval practice in small doses rather than long review sessions.
  • Separate concentration problems from mastery of the concept.

Track: Compare current concentration with the child’s own baseline and note medication/sleep changes.

Consider referral when

Mental-health and medical evaluation are appropriate when cognitive slowing is new or worsening; assess for ADHD/learning issues if the pattern predates mood symptoms.

Barrier 4

Daily rhythm and participation have collapsed

What this may look like at home: Sleep/wake timing, meals, movement, self-care, social contact, and learning all become irregular or absent.

Try first

  • Rebuild a simple daily scaffold with wake time, meals, light/movement, one learning block, and one meaningful activity.
  • Use behavioral activation principles: schedule activity before waiting for motivation.
  • Protect sleep rather than shifting all learning late into the night.

Track: Track sleep, activity, social contact, self-care, and amount of time out of bed/room.

Consider referral when

Refer when basic routines deteriorate substantially, the child is isolated, or the family cannot restore daily functioning with support.

Barrier 5

Withdrawal makes asking for help harder

What this may look like at home: The child stops sharing work, avoids family learning, disengages from peers, or says nothing matters.

Try first

  • Use brief, low-pressure check-ins.
  • Offer choices about how to show learning.
  • Maintain connection without making every interaction about productivity.

Track: Note social withdrawal, pleasure, interests, and communication changes.

Consider referral when

Mental-health evaluation is warranted for persistent withdrawal, loss of pleasure, hopelessness, or major decline.

Barrier 6

Safety concerns require a different plan

What this may look like at home: The child talks about death, self-harm, suicide, feeling like a burden, or shows self-injury or dangerous behavior.

Try first

  • Treat safety as the priority, not a homeschool behavior problem.
  • Increase supervision and reduce access to lethal means when risk is present.
  • Use crisis/emergency resources appropriate to the level of danger.

Track: Do not use a worksheet as a substitute for a real safety assessment.

Consider referral when

Immediate professional assessment is warranted for suicidal thoughts with intent/plan, escalating self-harm, psychosis, severe agitation, or inability to maintain safety. Call emergency/crisis services when danger is immediate.

Barrier 7

Grades or assessments do not reflect current access

What this may look like at home: Performance drops because mood, energy, sleep, concentration, or treatment side effects interfere with demonstration.

Try first

  • Document the conditions under which the child can currently perform.
  • Use shorter assessments and flexible response modes when the learning target permits.
  • Reassess after stabilization rather than treating temporary impairment as a fixed academic ceiling.

Track: Compare current performance to prior baseline and treatment/recovery periods.

Consider referral when

Seek broader learning evaluation if academic difficulty remains after mood and functioning improve.

Who to call first

Start with the pediatrician/primary-care clinician and a qualified child mental-health professional. Child psychology/therapy is appropriate for depressive symptoms; psychiatry may be needed for medication or severe/complex presentations. OT can support functional routines, self-care, activity participation, and regulation when those are major barriers, but OT is not a substitute for depression treatment.

How to make the referral

Use the shared guide 00 Getting Outside Help When You Homeschool – Referral Guide. Bring a short concern summary, representative work samples, what you have already tried, and specific questions you want the evaluator to answer. If insurance is involved, ask whether a physician referral or prior authorization is required.

Public evaluation while homeschooling

A private clinical evaluation is not the only route. Arizona explicitly includes homeschool students in Child Find. California recognizes home schools as private schools for IDEA equitable-services purposes. Use the Arizona or California homeschool guide in this library for the current state-specific pathway and service rules.

Important boundary

Do not force a child through pain, panic, unsafe behavior, or severe dysregulation in order to “collect more data.” Urgent medical or safety concerns come before educational troubleshooting.

Scope

This guide is educational information and a planning aid, not a diagnosis or individualized treatment plan.
