Homeschool Barrier → Support

Persistent Conflict & ODD

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

A problem-first guide for learning at home. Start with the task that is hard, not the diagnosis. Pick one or two barriers, change a small number of things, and watch what happens over time.

Barrier 1

Escalation stops learning

What this may look like at home: A correction or demand quickly becomes yelling, threats, leaving, shutdown, or a long argument that replaces the lesson.

Try first

  • Stop teaching during full escalation and restore safety/regulation first.
  • Use brief directions and fewer words.
  • Return later to one small doable step rather than restarting the entire conflict.

Track: Record triggers, escalation length, recovery, and what adult responses shorten versus intensify the pattern.

Consider referral when

Seek mental-health evaluation when escalation is frequent, severe, aggressive, or disrupting family functioning.

Barrier 2

Need for control turns into power struggles

What this may look like at home: Almost any instruction becomes a debate, counter-demand, delay, or contest over who decides.

Try first

  • Offer bounded choices where either option is acceptable.
  • Separate nonnegotiable safety limits from flexible preferences.
  • Avoid adding extra consequences in the middle of a power struggle.

Track: Identify which demands truly require adult control and which can be redesigned.

Consider referral when

Parent-management training, family therapy, or child therapy may help when the family is trapped in coercive cycles.

Barrier 3

Planning and task problems look like defiance

What this may look like at home: The child says no, wanders off, argues, or avoids when the real barrier may be initiation, language, attention, memory, or task difficulty.

Try first

  • Make the first step concrete.
  • Reduce working-memory and organization load.
  • Test whether performance changes when the task is easier to understand and start.

Track: Compare behavior across tasks with different cognitive demands.

Consider referral when

Consider ADHD, learning, language, or developmental evaluation when “defiance” clusters around specific task demands.

Barrier 4

Changes or mistakes trigger getting stuck

What this may look like at home: Unexpected changes, losing, correction, or small mistakes cause disproportionate rigidity or anger.

Try first

  • Preview changes and practice flexible alternatives.
  • Use neutral correction focused on the task.
  • Build tolerance with small, supported mistakes rather than surprise demands.

Track: Record the specific triggers and recovery supports.

Consider referral when

Consider autism, anxiety, ADHD, trauma, or other developmental/mental-health evaluation when rigidity is broad or severe.

Barrier 5

Communication problems fuel conflict

What this may look like at home: The child misinterprets tone, cannot explain what is wrong, uses extreme language, or gets more dysregulated as adults talk more.

Try first

  • Use direct, literal language.
  • Ask the child to show or write rather than explain under pressure.
  • Teach a few functional messages: help, break, not sure, too hard, need space.

Track: Note whether conflict changes when language load is reduced.

Consider referral when

SLP evaluation is appropriate when receptive/expressive/pragmatic language problems appear to be part of the conflict pattern.

Barrier 6

Threat or trauma responses affect participation

What this may look like at home: Demands trigger fear, freezing, fight/flight behavior, extreme vigilance, or reactions linked to prior adverse experiences.

Try first

  • Prioritize safety and predictability.
  • Avoid coercive escalation and humiliating consequences.
  • Use trauma-informed pacing and clear boundaries.

Track: Track triggers and safety patterns without forcing disclosure.

Consider referral when

Refer to a trauma-informed mental-health clinician when trauma symptoms are suspected or known and impair daily functioning.

Who to call first

A child psychologist, therapist, family therapist, or clinician trained in parent-management interventions is usually the best first referral. The pediatrician can screen for ADHD, sleep, medication, developmental, or medical contributors and coordinate psychiatry if needed. SLP or OT may be appropriate when communication, sensory-motor, or functional-regulation barriers are contributing. Severe aggression, threats, cruelty, weapon access, or inability to keep family members safe requires prompt individualized safety assessment rather than a generic behavior plan.

How to make the referral

Use the shared guide 00 Getting Outside Help When You Homeschool – Referral Guide. Bring a short concern summary, representative work samples, what you have already tried, and specific questions you want the evaluator to answer. If insurance is involved, ask whether a physician referral or prior authorization is required.

Public evaluation while homeschooling

A private clinical evaluation is not the only route. Arizona explicitly includes homeschool students in Child Find. California recognizes home schools as private schools for IDEA equitable-services purposes. Use the Arizona or California homeschool guide in this library for the current state-specific pathway and service rules.

Important boundary

Do not force a child through pain, panic, unsafe behavior, or severe dysregulation in order to “collect more data.” Urgent medical or safety concerns come before educational troubleshooting.

Scope

This guide is educational information and a planning aid, not a diagnosis or individualized treatment plan.
