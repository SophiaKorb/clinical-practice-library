Homeschool Barrier → Support

Dyslexia

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

A problem-first guide for learning at home. Start with the task that is hard, not the diagnosis. Pick one or two barriers, change a small number of things, and watch what happens over time.

Barrier 1

Sounds in words are hard to hear or manipulate

What this may look like at home: Rhyming, blending, segmenting, or identifying sounds in words is unexpectedly hard.

Try first

  • Teach phonological awareness explicitly and systematically.
  • Use brief daily practice with immediate feedback.
  • Do not substitute guessing from pictures or context for sound work.

Track: Record which phonological tasks are mastered and which remain unstable.

Consider referral when

Seek dyslexia/language evaluation when phonological weaknesses persist despite explicit instruction or there is a strong family/history risk pattern.

Barrier 2

Connecting letters and sounds and reading new words is hard

What this may look like at home: The child memorizes familiar words but struggles to decode unfamiliar ones accurately.

Try first

  • Use structured, explicit phonics with cumulative review.
  • Practice decodable text matched to taught patterns.
  • Correct errors by returning to the sound-spelling pattern, not by encouraging guessing.

Track: Track accuracy on taught and untaught words separately.

Consider referral when

A dyslexia-experienced evaluator and structured-literacy specialist are appropriate when progress is slow despite high-quality explicit instruction.

Barrier 3

Long words and word parts are hard to read

What this may look like at home: Multisyllabic words collapse even when simpler words are readable.

Try first

  • Teach syllable division and meaningful word parts explicitly.
  • Mark prefixes, roots, suffixes, and syllables visually.
  • Practice reading and spelling the same word patterns.

Track: Track accuracy across increasing word length and complexity.

Consider referral when

Refer when decoding remains far below expectation or oral-language weaknesses suggest an SLP evaluation too.

Barrier 4

Reading is accurate but slow or exhausting

What this may look like at home: The child can decode but reads so slowly that comprehension, stamina, or access to grade-level material suffers.

Try first

  • Build automaticity with appropriately leveled repeated reading.
  • Use audiobooks/text-to-speech for content learning while continuing reading instruction.
  • Separate reading practice from access to science, history, and literature.

Track: Track accuracy, rate, fatigue, and comprehension.

Consider referral when

Seek evaluation when fluency remains markedly weak or the child requires disproportionate effort despite accurate instruction.

Barrier 5

Spelling and written word production are hard

What this may look like at home: Spelling is highly inconsistent, phonetic patterns are weak, or writing is avoided because word production is so effortful.

Try first

  • Teach spelling patterns and morphology explicitly.
  • Use typing/spell-check for content work while teaching spelling separately.
  • Analyze errors to see whether the issue is phonological, orthographic, or language-based.

Track: Keep periodic spelling samples rather than relying on single tests.

Consider referral when

Consider dyslexia evaluation and SLP assessment when oral language/phonological weaknesses are also present.

Barrier 6

Heavy reading demands block grade-level learning

What this may look like at home: The child understands concepts when heard or discussed but cannot access the amount of print needed independently.

Try first

  • Use audiobooks, text-to-speech, or read-aloud for content subjects.
  • Preteach vocabulary and provide accessible notes.
  • Protect daily remedial reading time without making every subject a reading test.

Track: Compare content comprehension with and without print-access support.

Consider referral when

Professional evaluation can clarify the reading disability and document needed access tools.

Barrier 7

Tests hide what the child knows

What this may look like at home: Reading load, spelling, or slow decoding makes tests look like content failure.

Try first

  • Read directions/items aloud when decoding is not the target.
  • Allow oral/typed responses when appropriate.
  • Use untimed or extended-time conditions when speed is not the skill being measured.

Track: Compare content performance with reading demand reduced.

Consider referral when

Seek comprehensive evaluation when the family needs diagnostic clarity, instructional planning, or formal documentation.

Who to call first

A psychologist, neuropsychologist, educational evaluator, SLP, or other professional with dyslexia expertise may conduct diagnostic evaluation depending on jurisdiction and scope. For treatment, look for a structured-literacy specialist or dyslexia therapist. An SLP is especially relevant when phonological awareness or broader oral-language problems are present. OT is not the primary treatment for dyslexia unless separate motor/handwriting/sensory issues also interfere.

How to make the referral

Use the shared guide 00 Getting Outside Help When You Homeschool – Referral Guide. Bring a short concern summary, representative work samples, what you have already tried, and specific questions you want the evaluator to answer. If insurance is involved, ask whether a physician referral or prior authorization is required.

Public evaluation while homeschooling

A private clinical evaluation is not the only route. Arizona explicitly includes homeschool students in Child Find. California recognizes home schools as private schools for IDEA equitable-services purposes. Use the Arizona or California homeschool guide in this library for the current state-specific pathway and service rules.

Important boundary

Do not force a child through pain, panic, unsafe behavior, or severe dysregulation in order to “collect more data.” Urgent medical or safety concerns come before educational troubleshooting.

Scope

This guide is educational information and a planning aid, not a diagnosis or individualized treatment plan.
