Compare a support by its purpose

Clinical route: what am I seeing → explanation → assessment → action → tool → review
  1. Seeing: preserve the learner’s original response and record help provided.
  2. Explaining: compare a skill gap, an access barrier, insufficient instruction, language, distress, and overlapping contributors.
  3. Assessing: analyze errors, instructional history, work samples, and response to explicit teaching; seek qualified educational/language assessment as indicated.
  4. Acting: teach the missing skill and support access concurrently.
  5. Tool: use the two-track plan and original diagnosis guides.
  6. Reviewing: separate independent learning, supported performance, generalization, burden, and student perspective.

A support trial does not establish a diagnosis, school eligibility, or placement recommendation. On standardized assessments, verify the permitted accommodations and implications for score interpretation rather than assuming the classroom support transfers unchanged.

Worked case: explaining a math strategy

A fictional learner can show equal groups with counters but writes little when asked for a paragraph explanation. The target may be mathematical reasoning; paragraph production is an additional demand. Allowing a diagram or spoken explanation may reveal reasoning while writing instruction addresses a separate need. If the target is written mathematical argument, that output requirement is part of the skill and needs explicit teaching.

Record what the learner showed, the exact help, and what remains uncertain. Do not silently replace the learner’s answer with an adult’s correction.
