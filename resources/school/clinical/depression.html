School Access Toolkit · Clinical

Depression
Barrier → Support

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Clinical version for functional formulation, differential questions, implementable plan wording, and implementation review. Barrier numbers and support codes match the Plain Language version.

Clinical and educational formulation

  • Define the barrier in a specific task, setting, and demand.
  • Generate competing hypotheses instead of relying on diagnosis alone.
  • Compare conditions, supports, settings, and response formats.
  • Separate access supports from instruction for missing skills.
  • Operationalize who, where, when, how often, and how success will be reviewed.

Barrier 1

Low energy and slowed thinking reduce access

Observable presentation: The student works slowly, appears tired, or cannot sustain the full task.

1-APrioritize essential work. Identify the learning target and reduce repetitive items.
1-BChunk work. Use brief sections with visible completion points.
1-CProvide additional processing time. Define amount and task types.
1-DSchedule demanding work thoughtfully. Use known higher-energy periods when scheduling allows.
1-EProvide notes and recorded directions. Reduce the need to hold or copy information.
Consider

Consider psychomotor slowing, sleep disruption, medication effects, pain/medical illness, and reduced reinforcement.

Data to review

Compare morning vs later-day functioning and high-interest vs routine tasks.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Avoid moralizing fatigue or slow pace as laziness.

Barrier 2

Initiation and completion break down

Observable presentation: The student cannot start, falls behind, or becomes overwhelmed by accumulated work.

2-AGive a concrete first step. Staff identify the first small action.
2-BUse scheduled check-ins. Name the adult and times for start and progress checks.
2-CCreate a catch-up plan. Prioritize essential assignments and set realistic dates.
2-DUse one task system. Post complete work and deadlines in one location.
2-EReview workload weekly. A named staff member checks for unmanageable accumulation.
Consider

Separate low activation, executive dysfunction, hopelessness, task difficulty, and perfectionism.

Data to review

Measure time to start, response to first-step prompts, and whether smaller task units change completion.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Supports should reduce activation barriers while preserving opportunities for mastery.

Barrier 3

Concentration and memory are reduced

Observable presentation: The student misses directions, loses place, or cannot retain new information.

3-AKeep directions visible. Use short written steps and examples.
3-BProvide guided notes. Give key information without excessive copying.
3-CReduce distraction. Specify a work location for identified tasks.
3-DRepeat and retrieve. Check the first step and use brief cumulative review.
3-ESegment tests and assignments. Present manageable sections with clear return points.
Consider

Depression can impair attention, working memory, processing speed, and retrieval.

Data to review

Review whether cognitive performance varies with symptom severity, sleep, or task demand.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Do not assume every concentration problem represents a separate attention disorder.

Barrier 4

Attendance or re-entry disrupts learning

Observable presentation: Absences, late arrival, or hospitalization creates gaps and shame-driven avoidance.

4-ACreate a re-entry plan. Name the contact, schedule, workload priorities, and review date.
4-BProvide one point of contact. Use one staff member to coordinate, not multiple competing messages.
4-CPrioritize essential standards. Do not require every missed worksheet when fewer tasks show mastery.
4-DProvide instruction for missed content. A make-up packet alone is not instruction.
4-EPlan gradual return if needed. Use a team-approved schedule with goals and frequent review, not an indefinite shortened day.
Consider

Consider symptom severity, treatment schedules, transportation, sleep, family stress, and school avoidance.

Data to review

Use coordinated re-entry plans after prolonged absence or intensive treatment.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Avoid requiring a student to 'catch up first' before receiving access supports.

Barrier 5

Withdrawal limits participation and help seeking

Observable presentation: The student stops speaking, avoids peers, or cannot ask for support.

5-AUse private check-ins. Schedule brief contact with a named adult.
5-BOffer low-demand response options. Allow written or digital check-in before verbal discussion.
5-CProvide structured peer roles. Use clear, bounded roles rather than unstructured group demands.
5-DPrompt supports proactively. Do not require the student to initiate every accommodation.
5-EProtect confidentiality. Share disability information only with staff who need it to implement the plan.
Consider

Consider anhedonia, social withdrawal, shame, irritability, and reduced help seeking.

Data to review

Identify safe adults and low-burden ways to request help.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Do not make participation supports depend on public disclosure of depression.

Barrier 6

Safety concerns require coordinated response

Observable presentation: Statements, behavior, or marked change suggests self-harm or suicide risk.

6-AFollow the school safety protocol. Use trained staff for immediate risk assessment and caregiver notification.
6-BCreate an individualized safety plan. Name warning signs, contacts, supervision, environmental steps, and emergency actions.
6-CPlan return after crisis care. Coordinate re-entry, workload, supports, and monitoring with appropriate consent.
6-DDo not use the accommodation guide as crisis care. Call emergency services or 988 when urgent safety help is needed.
6-EReview access after incidents. Assess whether disability-related barriers and the school plan need revision.
Consider

School planning must be coordinated with clinical risk assessment, family/support systems, and emergency procedures when relevant.

Data to review

Clarify what school staff should notice, whom they contact, and what is outside the educational plan.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

An IEP/504 support plan does not replace suicide-risk assessment, treatment, or emergency care.

Barrier 7

Testing and grading do not reflect current access

Observable presentation: Speed, stamina, absences, or concentration distort performance.

7-AUse flexible completion windows. Define the window and tasks; avoid open-ended “whenever.”
7-BAllow planned breaks. Define length, frequency, and return.
7-CSegment assessments. Give sections separately.
7-DUse alternate response methods. Use when valid for the target.
7-EGrade current learning fairly. Separate missing access from mastery and follow the team’s documented plan.
Consider

Current symptoms can distort pace, attendance, output, and test performance.

Data to review

Review whether grading practices are measuring mastery or primarily missed access during an episode.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Temporary access supports and make-up plans should be specific and periodically reviewed.

Clinical documentation

Diagnosis can be relevant evidence, but it does not by itself determine IEP or Section 504 eligibility. Describe functional impairment, likely school impact, and why school-based evaluation or review is recommended.
