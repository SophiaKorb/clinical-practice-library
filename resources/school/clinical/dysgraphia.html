School Access Toolkit · Clinical

Dysgraphia
Barrier → Support

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Clinical version for functional formulation, differential questions, implementable plan wording, and implementation review. Barrier numbers and support codes match the Plain Language version.

Clinical and educational formulation

  • Define the barrier in a specific task, setting, and demand.
  • Generate competing hypotheses instead of relying on diagnosis alone.
  • Compare conditions, supports, settings, and response formats.
  • Separate access supports from instruction for missing skills.
  • Operationalize who, where, when, how often, and how success will be reviewed.

Barrier 1

Handwriting is slow, effortful, or painful

Observable presentation: Writing speed, grip, fatigue, or legibility prevents completion.

1-AReduce copying. Provide notes, printed directions, or digital text.
1-BAllow keyboarding. Make a device available in the settings where written output is required.
1-CAllow speech-to-text or dictation. Use when handwriting is not the target.
1-DProvide adapted paper or tools. Use individually selected spacing, line, grip, slant, or positioning supports.
1-EConsider OT evaluation. Evaluate functional motor, access, and tool needs; do not assume one cause.
Consider

Consider graphomotor speed, pain, fatigue, endurance, fine-motor control, posture, and assistive-technology access.

Data to review

Compare handwriting across short vs sustained tasks and note pain/fatigue, not just legibility.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

OT/motor evaluation may be relevant when motor demands are a major bottleneck.

Barrier 2

Letter formation and spacing reduce legibility

Observable presentation: Letters, size, alignment, spacing, or direction are inconsistent.

2-AProvide explicit handwriting instruction. Teach formation, start points, spacing, and efficient patterns if this is an instructional need.
2-BUse a visual model. Keep the alphabet and formation model visible at the point of writing.
2-CUse adapted paper. Select line or spacing supports based on trial data.
2-DShorten handwriting practice. Use brief accurate practice rather than long fatigue-based copying.
2-EMeasure legibility and independence. Track agreed features in a real writing sample.
Consider

Letter formation, size, spacing, alignment, and automaticity can be distinct from idea generation.

Data to review

Review consistency across copying, dictation, and spontaneous writing.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Legibility goals should be functional and individualized rather than aesthetic.

Barrier 3

Transcription load hides ideas

Observable presentation: Spelling, punctuation, handwriting, and holding a sentence in mind consume working memory.

3-ASeparate drafting from editing. Allow ideas to be recorded before mechanics are corrected.
3-BProvide sentence or paragraph frames. Use a structure matched to the task, then fade by data.
3-CAllow word prediction and spell check. Use when mechanics are not the construct.
3-DProvide additional writing time. Define the amount and types of assignments.
3-EScore content separately. Report ideas separately from transcription when appropriate.
Consider

Transcription requires coordinating spelling, handwriting/typing, working memory, and language formulation.

Data to review

Compare oral expression with written output and handwritten vs typed output.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Use access technology while separately evaluating whether spelling or written-language instruction is needed.

Barrier 4

Copying and note taking cause lost information

Observable presentation: The student cannot copy accurately while listening or keeping place.

4-AProvide essential notes. Give teacher notes, guided notes, or a peer-note copy.
4-BPermit photos or digital copies. Use an approved method for board or slide content.
4-CPlace source and response together. Avoid repeated shifting between board, book, and paper.
4-DAllow audio support. Permit recording under the school’s defined privacy rules.
4-ECheck note access. Name who posts or provides notes and by when.
Consider

Copying and note taking add visual scanning, working memory, motor output, and divided attention.

Data to review

Check what information is lost when copying from board/text vs when notes are provided.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Providing notes can preserve access without removing opportunities for active engagement in the content.

Barrier 5

Written organization is weak

Observable presentation: Ideas are present but paragraphs, sequence, and structure break down.

5-AEvaluate written expression separately. Do not assume all composition difficulty comes from handwriting.
5-BUse graphic organizers. Match the organizer to narrative, explanation, or argument.
5-CProvide models and rubrics. Show the expected structure and success criteria.
5-DTeach planning and revision. Provide explicit instruction if this is a skill deficit.
5-EAllow oral planning. Let the student talk, record, or map ideas before drafting.
Consider

Written organization can reflect language formulation, executive function, knowledge organization, or transcription overload.

Data to review

Compare planning with graphic organizers, oral planning, and reduced transcription demand.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

Do not assume disorganized writing is purely a handwriting problem.

Barrier 6

Written tests understate knowledge

Observable presentation: Handwriting speed or transcription, not the subject, limits answers.

6-AAllow typing or speech-to-text. Use when handwriting is not being assessed.
6-BAllow oral or selected response. Use when valid for the intended construct.
6-CDefine extended time. State amount, setting, and tests.
6-DProvide adequate response space. Use separate paper, larger boxes, or digital response.
6-EDo not penalize unrelated mechanics. Score the skill the assessment is designed to measure.
Consider

Written tests can confound knowledge with handwriting speed, spelling, organization, and motor output.

Data to review

Compare oral/typed/selected-response performance when the target is content knowledge.

Avoid assuming / implementation note

If written expression itself is the target, preserve the construct while reducing unrelated access barriers.

Clinical documentation

Diagnosis can be relevant evidence, but it does not by itself determine IEP or Section 504 eligibility. Describe functional impairment, likely school impact, and why school-based evaluation or review is recommended.
