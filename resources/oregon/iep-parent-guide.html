Oregon's Standard IEP and quality IEP toolkit

Oregon's Department of Education publishes the Oregon Standard IEP and a quality-IEP toolkit covering present levels, measurable goals, services, transition, and procedural safeguards. Families can use these documents to understand how an IEP is organized.

How to start

  1. List the student's strengths, barriers to participation, and examples of what happens at school.
  2. Contact the school district's special education office about a written referral or evaluation request and obtain current parent-rights guidance.
  3. Gather schoolwork, attendance information, past evaluations, and questions from the student and family.
  4. Ask how the team will address all suspected areas of educational need, and request copies of written decisions and any resulting plan.

IEP meeting checklist

  • Review present academic and functional performance; ask how information from home and school is incorporated.
  • Discuss measurable goals, specially designed instruction, accommodations, related services, and ways to measure progress.
  • Confirm who implements each support, when progress is reported, and how the team will revisit a plan that is not working.
  • Record unanswered questions and follow up in writing.

Moving into Oregon from another state

Bring the most recent IEP, eligibility and evaluation records, service schedules, and progress reports. Contact the receiving district promptly. IDEA addresses comparable services for students transferring with an IEP, while the new district follows applicable state procedures when reviewing or developing a plan. Ask about immediate services and who will coordinate the transition.
