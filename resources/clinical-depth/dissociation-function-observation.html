Practical clinical tool · Function and access

Memory, continuity and daily life: what actually changes?

Use collaboratively with a clinician to describe one concrete functional difficulty at a time. This sheet is not a memory test, disability determination, DID screen or a claim that a changed sense of identity establishes functioning.

Use fictional or properly de-identified examples on shared devices. Text entered here stays in the open page and is not saved or submitted by CPL; printing can include entries. You can skip any section, communicate by pointing, drawing, dictating or using supported communication, and stop if the exercise becomes distressing.

What I experience

Time loss · Detachment · Feeling more/less connected · Self-knowledge · Uncertainty

The person is the expert on describing their experience.

What happens in daily tasks

Remembering · Initiating · Completing · Staying safe · Needed support · Real-world outcomes

Functional performance needs its own observations.

Choose a real task or context

Talk about one task, not “memory in general.” For example: a planned appointment, paying a bill, a familiar route, following medical instructions, preparing food or retrieving a conversation.

Prospective tasks & appointmentsPersonal care & medication coordinationWork/school participationFinances & home managementTravel & safetyConversations & relationships

Observe the task through four distinct lenses

LensRecord separatelyKeep in mind
Directly reportedThe person's memory and experience in their own wordsDon't convert an interpretation into a witnessed event.
Observed / corroboratedRecords, completed steps, direct observation, consent-based collateralAbsence of a record isn't proof something did not happen.
Conditions & accessSleep, health, language, interruption, privacy, aids, opportunitiesPerformance without access is not a fair test of capacity.
Outcome & costCompletion, accuracy, safety, time, assistance, burden, choice“Completed” can hide extraordinary support effort.

Review two trajectories, not one

ExperienceMore continuity? Less distress? Different sense of agency or self-description?
FunctionCan the person complete this task more reliably and safely, with acceptable support?
These can improve, worsen or stay unchanged independently.

Clinical boundaries and related materials

Acute confusion, new neurological changes, unaccounted unsafe travel, loss of consciousness, serious medication-management concerns, or immediate danger bypass routine worksheet use. Evaluate appropriately. An informal functional sample cannot replace licensed neuropsychological testing, decisional-capacity evaluation, disability adjudication or a specialist DID assessment.
