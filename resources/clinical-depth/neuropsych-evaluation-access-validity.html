Clinical depth · Assessment · Neuropsychology

What can this evaluation responsibly conclude?

Design psychological and neuropsychological assessments around a real referral decision. Plan evidence and access conditions together; a test score is useful only when its interpretation is justified. This is a clinician decision aid, not a test battery, diagnostic algorithm or permission to use restricted measures.

Urgent medical change comes first. New aphasia, focal weakness, loss of consciousness, sudden confusion or other acute neurological deterioration requires appropriate medical assessment. Speech difficulty alone does not establish low cognition or inability to decide.

Two tracks must run together

Clarify whether the referral asks about diagnosis, acquired change, school access, capacity for a specific decision, adaptive disability, or treatment planning. These do not require identical evidence. Specify the authorized evaluator, intended audience, permissions and what the findings may not establish.

  1. Identify the decision and the smallest evidence package that could change it.
  2. Separate the person's report, records, collateral, observation and tested performance.
  3. Choose measures with valid norms, training and publisher-authorized procedures; document when none fits.
  4. Adapt the communication environment appropriately; consult SLP and other specialties for inaccessible performance.
  5. When modified administration compromises norms, report descriptive findings and the inference limits rather than manufacturing a corrected score.
  6. Integrate discrepancies and explicitly state what further information could revise the report.
  7. Give actionable feedback in the person's communication mode; track implementation and real-world effects.

Access adjustments and score validity are not interchangeable

BarrierAssess and supportInterpretation boundary
Expressive/receptive languageDistinguish understanding from spoken output; consider AAC, gesture, supported pointing, written options, preferred language and SLP input.Changing a standardized task or response rule may invalidate norms; document exactly what was changed.

Supported communication enhances fairness in conversation and functional demonstration. It does not license changing protected items or asserting original test norms remain valid. Poor/inconsistent performance is not evidence of deception by itself. Assessment of specific decisional capacity requires the appropriate qualified, decision-specific evaluation.

Two fictional cases with different unresolved questions

Case A: expressive aphasia after stroke

Jules, 48, has new word-finding and speech-output limitations following a stroke, right-hand weakness, fatigue and a visual-field deficit. A prior verbal-heavy screen was low; it did not document comprehension or alternate response access. A referral asks if Jules lacks cognitive capacity to return to community activities.

What should the evaluator do next?

Clarify the actual decision; coordinate neurological and SLP assessment, understand expressive versus receptive limits, plan safe communication, review functional performance and determine which standardized interpretations remain valid. Low spoken output alone cannot establish broad impairment or legal incapacity.

What feedback is defensible?

Describe observed communication barriers, tested conditions, supports enabling choices, uncertainties, medical/rehab referrals and specific community participation goals. Distinguish disability and access recommendations from unsupported global cognitive conclusions.

Case B: autism, language, attention and recent bullying

Casey, 14, has longstanding sensory/social-communication differences, reading difficulty, inconsistent attention and new avoidance after bullying. The school proposes a single rating scale to decide whether this is autism, ADHD, a learning disorder or trauma; dense written directions undermine demonstration of knowledge.

Which questions must remain separate?

Developmental pattern, specific academic skills, recent threat/anxiety, sleep, family/school context and access to demonstrating knowledge. Support against bullying and legitimate access needs need not wait for a single definitive label.

What would distinguish instruction from accommodation?

If reading skill is the target, evaluate decoding directly with appropriate procedures; if science knowledge is the target, accessible text or alternative response modes may be appropriate. Record both learning and participation, not just one test result.

Make the final feedback actionable

Clinician interpretation

Referral decision → competing explanations → evidence by source → testing conditions and validity → supported conclusions → unresolved questions → next specialist/assessment action.

Person and family feedback

What we found, what we do not know, strengths, access barriers, one or two meaningful recommendations, who owns implementation, and when to verify benefit or harm.
