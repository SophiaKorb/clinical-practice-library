Define the assessment

The supervised-treatment exception is considered for prescribed opioids, stimulants, and sedatives/anxiolytics. The dropdown is disabled for other classes; clinical exceptions still require individual verification.

A criterion should be marked “Supported” only when the full relevant requirement is established, not merely suggested. “Unclear” keeps diagnostic uncertainty visible.

Clinical reasoning before a conclusion

Check clinically significant impairment or distress; substance type and diagnostic exceptions; whether evidence genuinely occurred in one 12-month interval; medical and psychiatric explanations; intoxication/withdrawal syndromes; co-occurrence; and any acute safety concerns.

Important: Tolerance and withdrawal from appropriate supervised treatment are not counted as SUD criteria. Withdrawal criteria are not defined for some substance classes (including hallucinogens, inhalants, and PCP); verify the substance-specific DSM-5-TR criteria before using a tally. Medical withdrawal risk needs separate assessment; alcohol or sedative withdrawal can be dangerous.

Severity by supported criteria, when all diagnostic conditions are independently verified: 2–3 mild, 4–5 moderate, 6+ severe. A 0–1 count is below the usual SUD threshold, but does not establish safety or rule out other problems.
