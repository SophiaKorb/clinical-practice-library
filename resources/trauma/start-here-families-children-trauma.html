A curated clinical and family-resource section for work with traumatized children, adolescents, caregivers, and family systems.

How to Use This Section

Start with the concern you need help with: a recent traumatic event or disclosure, continuing trauma-related difficulties, caregiver–child relationships, unsafe family interactions, traumatic separation, or challenges involving child welfare. Choose a section below to open its resources.

Before using these resources

  • Behavior and emotional distress can have many explanations. Do not assume trauma is the cause.
  • Consider development, health, communication, learning, sensory needs, relationships, safety, and environment.
  • Trauma-informed support is different from specialized trauma treatment. Follow the training and supervision required for each approach.
  • Consider safety, the child’s perspective, and each person’s needs when involving caregivers. Open the detailed guide for treatment-model considerations.

State-System Note

Clinical trauma principles are not state-specific, but child welfare, foster/kinship supports, school procedures, developmental-disability systems, homeschooling rules, and Medicaid behavioral-health pathways differ. Use the Arizona and California shelves for system navigation rather than carrying one state's procedures into another state's work.
