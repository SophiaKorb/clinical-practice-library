Families, Children & Trauma

Curated by Sophia Cohon, PhD

A curated clinical and family-resource section for work with traumatized children, adolescents, caregivers, and family systems.

How to Use This Section

Start with the problem you are trying to solve rather than with a broad label of “trauma.” Separate acute response, established posttraumatic symptoms, complex developmental trauma, caregiver-child relationship work, coercive or unsafe family interaction, traumatic separation, and child-welfare/system stress.

01 Treatment Models & Clinical Guidance

The Curated Clinical Guide is the main clinician map. It covers TF-CBT, CPP, CFTSI, AF-CBT, ARC, SFCR, PFA, SPR, PCIT, CARE, and related authoritative treatment and training sources.

02 Caregiver, Family & Youth Resources

Use this shelf for psychoeducation and conversations with families and youth: complex trauma, age-related reactions, traumatic grief and separation, caregiver roles in recovery, and high-quality NCTSN family resources.

03 Foster, Kinship, Adoption & Child Welfare

Use this shelf for resource parents, kinship caregivers, adoptive families, birth parents involved with child welfare, traumatic separation, placement transitions, and trauma-informed child-welfare practice.

04 Trauma, School & Neurodevelopment

Use this shelf when trauma intersects with school functioning, autism, intellectual/developmental disability, communication differences, or behavior that may be multiply determined.

Clinical Guardrails

  • Do not treat all dysregulation, avoidance, aggression, shutdown, school refusal, or relationship difficulty as evidence of trauma.
  • Assess developmental, medical, neurodevelopmental, family-system, learning, sensory, safety, and environmental explanations as appropriate.
  • Trauma-informed care is not the same as trauma treatment. Use model-specific training, consultation, and manuals when a treatment requires them.
  • Caregiver involvement should be clinically meaningful when the model and safety context support it. Do not use conjoint work when coercion, credible fear, abuse, or another safety condition makes joint work inappropriate.

State-System Note

Clinical trauma principles are not state-specific, but child welfare, foster/kinship supports, school procedures, developmental-disability systems, homeschooling rules, and Medicaid behavioral-health pathways differ. Use the Arizona and California shelves for system navigation rather than carrying one state's procedures into another state's work.
