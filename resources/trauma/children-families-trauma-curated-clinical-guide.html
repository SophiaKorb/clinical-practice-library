Trauma · Curated Clinical Guide

Children, Families & Trauma

Curated by Sophia Cohon, PhD

A focused navigation guide to high-value resources for clinicians working with children, adolescents, caregivers, and families affected by trauma.

This collection prioritizes authoritative treatment-model sources and practical caregiver/family resources. It is a guide to resources, not a substitute for formal training, supervision, certification, or model-specific manuals where those are required.

Quick Match

Recent trauma or recent disclosure: Child and Family Traumatic Stress Intervention (CFTSI)

Child/adolescent trauma with active caregiver participation: Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT)

Infants and young children, attachment and caregiver-child relationship: Child-Parent Psychotherapy (CPP)

Physical abuse, coercive family conflict, harsh discipline, or child behavior problems: Alternatives for Families: A Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (AF-CBT)

Complex developmental trauma across child, caregiver, and service systems: Attachment, Regulation, and Competency (ARC)

Whole-family coping in chronic or ongoing traumatic contexts: Strengthening Family Coping Resources (SFCR)

Immediate and intermediate recovery after disaster, violence, or terrorism: Psychological First Aid (PFA) and Skills for Psychological Recovery (SPR)

1. Trauma-Focused Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (TF-CBT)

Best fit: children and adolescents with trauma-related symptoms when caregiver involvement can be incorporated into treatment.

Official TF-CBT program · Implementation resources

2. Child-Parent Psychotherapy (CPP)

Best fit: children from birth through age 5 and their caregivers, especially when trauma, attachment disruption, caregiver-child relationship strain, or developmental concerns are central.

NCTSN CPP model page

3. Child and Family Traumatic Stress Intervention (CFTSI)

Best fit: children and adolescents in the acute phase after a recent traumatic event or recent disclosure of abuse, together with a caregiver.

NCTSN CFTSI model page

4. Alternatives for Families: A Cognitive Behavioral Therapy (AF-CBT)

Best fit: school-age children and caregivers in families affected by frequent conflict, coercive interaction, harsh or physical discipline, child physical abuse, or significant behavior problems.

NCTSN AF-CBT model page

5. Attachment, Regulation, and Competency (ARC)

Best fit: children, adolescents, caregivers, and systems affected by complex developmental trauma.

NCTSN ARC model page

6. Strengthening Family Coping Resources (SFCR)

Best fit: families living with chronic, ongoing, or complex traumatic stress, particularly when treatment needs to build coping and regulation at the level of the whole family.

NCTSN SFCR model page

7. Psychological First Aid (PFA)

Best fit: immediate and early support after disasters, mass violence, terrorism, and other large-scale traumatic events.

PFA Field Operations Guide

8. Skills for Psychological Recovery (SPR)

Best fit: children, adolescents, adults, and families who continue to experience distress or functional difficulty after the immediate post-event period.

SPR Field Operations Guide

Curation Principles

  • Prefer model developers, NCTSN, federal agencies, and other authoritative primary sources.
  • Link to current official materials rather than redistributing restricted or commercial manuals.
  • Keep treatment selection developmentally appropriate and driven by the child's presentation, trauma context, caregiver availability, safety, and treatment setting.
  • Treat caregiver participation as clinically substantive, not decorative.
  • Distinguish acute response, trauma-focused treatment, complex-trauma work, and family-system intervention rather than treating all “trauma therapy” as interchangeable.
  • Recheck training requirements, manuals, and implementation guidance before using a model clinically.
