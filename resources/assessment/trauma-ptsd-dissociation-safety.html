TRAUMA, PTSD, DISSOCIATION & SAFETY

Clinician Start-Here Guide

USE THIS SHELF FOR

Child/adolescent trauma screening, adult PTSD screening and monitoring, dissociation questions, and suicide-risk screening/triage.

CHILD / ADOLESCENT TRAUMA

CATS — Child and Adolescent Trauma Screen

Best use: no-cost trauma exposure plus PTSD symptom screening when more than an ultra-brief screen is useful.

Respondents: caregiver ages 3–6; self-report ages 7–17; caregiver ages 7–17.

Interpretation: a positive result identifies clinically relevant posttraumatic stress symptoms needing review and possible PTSD diagnostic follow-up.

Do not interpret as: a stand-alone PTSD diagnosis.

Spanish: a Spanish version is available through the CATS developers.

Rights: ISTSS describes CATS as freely accessible with no copyright or licensing fee.

Official source: ISTSS Child and Adolescent Trauma Screen

ADULT PTSD: ULTRA-BRIEF SCREEN

PC-PTSD-5

Best use: brief identification of probable PTSD in primary-care or other settings where a short screen is appropriate.

Do not interpret as: a PTSD diagnosis.

Positive screens require further assessment, preferably a structured clinical interview when feasible.

Spanish: VA provides a confirmed Spanish translation but notes that the translation has not been validated in published research.

Official source: VA PC-PTSD-5

ADULT PTSD: SYMPTOM SEVERITY / MONITORING

PCL-5

Best use: PTSD symptom severity, screening, and monitoring change during and after treatment.

Format: 20-item self-report corresponding to DSM-5 PTSD symptoms.

Do not interpret as: a stand-alone diagnosis. VA identifies a structured interview such as CAPS-5 as the diagnostic gold standard.

Rights: the VA National Center for PTSD states the PCL-5 is public domain and not copyrighted, intended for qualified health professionals and researchers.

Spanish: VA provides a confirmed Spanish translation with the limitation that it has not been validated in published research.

Official source: VA PCL-5

DISSOCIATION / COMPLEX TRAUMA

Use a clinical dissociation interview and legitimate dissociation measures where available.

DES-II can support screening for dissociative symptom burden but should not establish a dissociative diagnosis by score alone.

MID should be used only when legitimate complete materials and appropriate specialty competence are available. In the current internal library, MID analysis resources exist but the official blank full MID remains unresolved.

Do not use dissociation scores as a malingering determination or forensic conclusion.

Always consider neurologic, sleep, substance, psychosis and trauma-related differentials when clinically relevant.

SUICIDE-RISK SCREENING

Columbia Protocol / C-SSRS

Best use: structured suicide-risk screening and triage in healthcare/community settings.

The Columbia Lighthouse Project states that the protocol and training are free for healthcare/community use and that healthcare providers do not need a license to embed the protocol in EHR/EMR systems.

Do not interpret as: prediction of future suicide or a substitute for clinical risk formulation.

A positive screen must connect to an action pathway appropriate to acuity, setting and local policy.

Official source: Columbia Protocol for Healthcare

CLINICAL SAFETY RULE

Acute risk, dangerous commands, severe disorganization, intoxication/withdrawal, mania, severe medical change or inability to maintain outpatient safety overrides routine measurement. Screening never delays an urgent clinical response.

SPANISH

Use official or developer-provided Spanish versions when available. Preserve the source's stated limitations. Do not create unofficial translated test items and apply English-language norms to them.

STOP RULE

Stop adding measures when the assessment has answered the immediate question: whether trauma/PTSD or dissociation needs further clarification, whether treatment monitoring is adequate, or what safety action is required.
