FUNCTIONING, DISABILITY & FORMAL TESTING BOUNDARIES

Clinician Start-Here Guide

USE THIS SHELF FOR

Adult functional disability, developmental/adaptive-function questions, and deciding when a no-cost screen is no longer an adequate substitute for formal normed testing.

GENERIC ADULT FUNCTIONING

WHODAS 2.0

Best use: cross-diagnostic adult disability and functioning when functioning itself is the clinical question.

Domains: cognition/communication, mobility, self-care, getting along, life activities and participation.

Versions: 12-item and 36-item; self, interviewer and proxy formats are available.

Do not interpret as: developmental adaptive behavior, capacity/forensic conclusions, or diagnosis-specific symptom severity.

Important boundary: WHODAS is not a substitute for Vineland or ABAS when the question requires normed adaptive behavior across conceptual, social and practical domains.

Rights rule: WHO provides the instrument and manual, but its current page directs users to licensing for reproduction, electronic records/data-capture embedding, or other reproduction. Link to the official source unless the intended use has been cleared.

Official source: WHO WHODAS 2.0

ADAPTIVE BEHAVIOR / DEVELOPMENTAL FUNCTIONING

No-cost coverage: developmental history, concrete functional interview, records and observed functioning.

What this can answer: how the person manages real-world tasks and where support is needed.

What this cannot answer: a norm-referenced adaptive composite or formal ID/DD eligibility criterion.

Formal benchmark when required: an appropriate licensed adaptive-behavior instrument such as Vineland or ABAS.

Rule: if a normed adaptive score is the decision variable, purchase, use licensed organizational access, or refer. Do not approximate it with WHODAS or a home-built checklist.

COGNITIVE ABILITY

There is no adequate free substitute for formal normed IQ testing.

History and selected tasks can identify concern and guide referral.

They cannot produce defensible FSIQ/index scores or satisfy formal intellectual-disability cognitive criteria.

Use an appropriately normed licensed cognitive battery when the referral question requires formal cognitive scores.

ACADEMIC ACHIEVEMENT / SPECIFIC LEARNING DISORDER

Records, curriculum data, work samples, focused interview and informal process probes can support formulation.

They do not replace normed reading, writing or math achievement testing when formal SLD evaluation or standardized achievement data are required.

The internal library's Math Learning Interview is a formulation tool, not an achievement test.

LANGUAGE / COMMUNICATION

Behavioral-health history, observation and social-cognition prompts can identify functional communication concerns and referral needs.

They do not replace normed receptive, expressive or pragmatic-language testing.

Refer to SLP or use appropriate specialty testing when normed language assessment is the question.

VISUAL-MOTOR / VISUAL-PERCEPTUAL CONCERNS

Structured observation and informal probes can identify a pattern worth evaluating.

They do not replace formal visual-motor/perceptual or occupational-therapy assessment when normed data are required.

BROADBAND BEHAVIORAL PROFILES

Free broad screens such as PSC-17 or SDQ can be excellent front-end tools.

They do not provide the same normed multi-informant broadband profile, validity indicators, composites and adaptive/syndrome scales as proprietary broadband systems.

Use the proprietary instrument only when those additional properties answer a question that still matters.

EXECUTIVE FUNCTION

CHEXI/TEXI/ADEXI can provide structured descriptive collateral.

They are not substitutes for a normed BRIEF profile or performance-based neuropsychological executive-function testing.

FORMAL-TESTING DECISION RULE

Before ordering a proprietary or lengthy assessment, name the missing decision variable.

If the answer will change diagnosis, eligibility, treatment, level of care or referral, formal testing may be justified.

If the information is already adequate for the clinical decision, more testing is not automatically better.

REPORT-WRITING RULE

State clearly whether information comes from screening, clinical interview, collateral, observation, informal probes or formal standardized testing. Do not use the language of normed testing for an informal procedure.
