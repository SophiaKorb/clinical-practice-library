CLINICAL ASSESSMENT, SCREENING & SAFETY

Free / Official Resource Guide

Purpose

A practical index of assessment and screening resources that clinicians can obtain from the measure developer, a federal source, or another authoritative host. A screening instrument is not a diagnosis. Check population, setting, permissions, scoring guidance, and current validation literature before clinical use.

1. SUICIDE RISK SCREENING & SAFETY

NIMH Ask Suicide-Screening Questions (ASQ) Toolkit

Brief suicide screening plus setting-specific pathways, brief suicide safety assessment materials, patient resources, translations, and training.

https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-conducted-at-nimh/asq-toolkit-materials

NIMH Youth ASQ Toolkit

Youth-specific outpatient, inpatient, and emergency-department pathways and worksheets.

https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-conducted-at-nimh/asq-toolkit-materials/youth-asq-toolkit

NIMH Adult ASQ Toolkit

Adult outpatient, inpatient, and emergency-department pathways and worksheets.

https://www.nimh.nih.gov/research/research-conducted-at-nimh/asq-toolkit-materials/adults-asq-toolkit

Columbia Suicide Severity Rating Scale / Columbia Protocol

Official overview of the Columbia Protocol and available versions.

https://cssrs.columbia.edu/the-columbia-scale-c-ssrs/about-the-scale/

Columbia Lighthouse Project – Free Training

Free individual and system training options for the Columbia Protocol.

https://cssrs.columbia.edu/training/training-options/

2. TRAUMA & PTSD ASSESSMENT

VA National Center for PTSD – Assessment Overview

A high-value hub for validated trauma and PTSD assessment instruments, administration guidance, and measure access.

https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/assessment/overview/

PTSD Checklist for DSM-5 (PCL-5)

Official VA page with scoring guidance and downloadable public-domain forms.

https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/assessment/adult-sr/ptsd-checklist.asp

Primary Care PTSD Screen for DSM-5 (PC-PTSD-5)

Official brief PTSD screen with administration and interpretation guidance.

https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/assessment/screens/pc-ptsd.asp

Life Events Checklist for DSM-5 (LEC-5)

Official trauma-exposure checklist information and forms.

https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/assessment/te-measures/life_events_checklist.asp

VA National Center for PTSD – List of All Measures

Index of adult, child/adolescent, trauma-exposure, interview, and self-report measures.

https://www.ptsd.va.gov/professional/assessment/list_measures.asp

NCTSN Screening & Assessment

Trauma-informed screening and assessment guidance plus access to the NCTSN Measures Review Database.

https://www.nctsn.org/node/739

NCTSN Measures Review Database – Usage & Glossary

Useful guidance for comparing measures by purpose, age, population, trauma type, and psychometric characteristics.

https://nctsn.org/treatments-and-practices/screening-and-assessments/measure-reviews/usage-and-glossary

3. CHILD & ADOLESCENT BROAD SCREENING

Pediatric Symptom Checklist (PSC / PSC-17)

Massachusetts General Hospital hosts forms, translations, scoring instructions, and age-specific guidance.

https://www.massgeneral.org/psychiatry/treatments-and-services/pediatric-symptom-checklist/

CRAFFT

Official adolescent substance-use screening site. The CRAFFT is free to use and includes self-administered and clinician-interview formats, translations, and provider guidance.

https://crafft.org/

NICHQ Vanderbilt Assessment Scales

NICHQ hosts the original Vanderbilt ADHD scales and links to current editions and guidance. Note that the first edition is available free; newer editions have separate access terms.

https://nichq.org/downloadable/nichq-vanderbilt-assessment-scales/

Official M-CHAT-R/F

Official autism screening site with current M-CHAT-R/F materials and permissions. The instrument is copyrighted, so follow the site’s use and reproduction terms.

https://www.mchatscreen.com/

4. PRACTICE NOTES

  • Prefer the developer or authoritative host over third-party copies.
  • Screening tools identify possible concerns; diagnostic conclusions require appropriate clinical evaluation.
  • Do not assume that “free to download” means unrestricted redistribution. Link to the official source when permissions are limited or unclear.
  • For repeated measurement, verify that the instrument is appropriate for monitoring change, not merely initial screening.
  • When interpreting scores, consider setting, base rates, developmental level, language, disability, culture, and the consequences of false positives and false negatives.
  • For high-stakes decisions, use the measure as one source of information rather than allowing a single score to become the formulation.
