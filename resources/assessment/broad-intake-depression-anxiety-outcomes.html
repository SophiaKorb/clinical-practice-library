BROAD INTAKE, DEPRESSION, ANXIETY & OUTCOMES

Clinician Start-Here Guide

USE THIS SHELF FOR

Diffuse presenting problems, broad symptom mapping, depression/anxiety follow-up, and low-burden psychotherapy outcome monitoring.

BROAD INTAKE

DSM-5-TR Level 1 Cross-Cutting Symptom Measures

Best use: brief multi-domain symptom map when the presentation is broad or several domains may matter.

Interpretation: a flagged domain identifies an area for additional inquiry or a relevant Level 2/focused assessment.

Do not interpret as: a diagnosis, a complete diagnostic battery, or a meaningful global score.

Rights rule: use current APA forms and version-specific rights statements.

Official source: APA DSM-5-TR Assessment Measures

DEPRESSION

MFQ / SMFQ

Best use: focused depressive-symptom measurement in children, adolescents, parents-on-child, and adults when a broader battery is unnecessary.

Recall: past two weeks.

Scoring: Not True = 0, Sometimes = 1, True = 2; sum the items for the version used.

Important interpretation rule: Duke explicitly does not prescribe a universal cutoff.

Do not interpret as: a diagnosis based on a locally invented threshold.

Rights rule: Duke permits clinicians and researchers to download any MFQ version free for use with clients/research; authors should be cited in publications.

Official source: Duke Mood and Feelings Questionnaire

PSYCHOTHERAPY OUTCOMES

CORE-10 / YP-CORE

Best use: very brief transdiagnostic distress/outcome monitoring when diagnosis-specific measures would be too narrow.

CORE-10: adult short-form outcome measure.

YP-CORE: designed for ages 13–17, with use roughly 11–18; interpretation varies with age and gender.

Do not interpret as: a specific DSM diagnosis or proof that treatment caused score change.

Rights rule: CORE measures are CC BY-NC-ND 4.0. They may be reproduced unchanged for noncommercial use with attribution; do not create derivative forms or unofficial translations.

Official source: CORE System Trust Instruments

ANXIETY

Use the narrowest age-appropriate anxiety measure already supported in the clinical setting when anxiety is the identified question. In the internal assessment library, SCAS/RCADS pathways provide good routine symptom coverage.

Clinical rule: symptom scale elevation should be integrated with impairment, avoidance, developmental context, medical/sleep contributors and differential assessment.

Do not buy or administer a proprietary measure merely to duplicate a routine symptom question already answered well.

HOW TO CHOOSE

If the presenting problem is diffuse, begin broad and then narrow.

If the presenting problem is already specific, skip the broad screener and assess the specific question.

If repeated measurement is needed, choose one measure that matches the treatment target and keep the version/respondent consistent.

If score change will not alter treatment, the measure may not be necessary.

SPANISH

Use official Spanish forms where available. Do not translate standardized items locally and then apply published norms/cutoffs as though nothing changed.

STOP RULE

Stop broad screening once a specific clinical question and next action are clear.
