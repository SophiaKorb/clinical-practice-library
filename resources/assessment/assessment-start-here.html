Clinical Resource Library

PURPOSE

Use this section to answer a clinical question with the smallest assessment package that can responsibly answer it. Screening is used to guide inquiry, treatment, monitoring, referral, and safety decisions. It is not a shortcut around clinical interview, collateral, impairment, developmental history, or differential diagnosis.

CORE RULE

Start with the decision that actually needs to be made. Add a measure only when its result could change that decision.

WHAT TO USE WHEN

Broad pediatric intake

Start with safety, developmental/functional interview, and a low-burden broad screen such as the PSC-17 or an appropriate DSM-5-TR Level 1 measure. Follow only the domain that remains clinically important.

Child trauma / PTSD

Use trauma interview plus safety and functioning. CATS is the preferred no-cost structured screen in this library when more than an ultra-brief screen is useful. A positive CATS result leads to PTSD-focused clinical clarification, not automatic diagnosis.

Pediatric ADHD

Establish developmental course, DSM symptoms, impairment, and cross-setting evidence. Parent/teacher disagreement is data, not an error to average away. Add formal normed ADHD or EF ratings only when the remaining question actually requires them.

ADHD vs autism vs trauma

Use developmental timeline, concrete examples across settings, and the ADHD–Autism–Trauma formulation matrix. Add the smallest targeted assessment for the domain that remains uncertain. Comorbidity is allowed.

Under age 5

Use developmental surveillance and caregiver interview. SWYC is a broad early-childhood screen; M-CHAT-R/F is appropriate only when age and use conditions fit. Positive screening does not replace developmental or autism evaluation.

Autism diagnostic clarification

Use developmental/social-communication history, restricted/repetitive/sensory history, functional examples, and differential assessment. Screening measures are adjuncts. Formal autism diagnosis remains dependent on competent comprehensive evaluation and access to appropriate diagnostic tools.

Adult broad diagnostic intake

Begin with diagnostic/functional interview and safety. Use broad or targeted measures only to identify domains needing focused follow-up. Stop broad screening once the clinical question is specific.

OCD

Use OCD-focused interview, impairment, avoidance, insight, reassurance and ritual assessment. FOCI/C-FOCI can support quick severity screening/monitoring. Intrusive thoughts alone do not establish OCD.

Bipolar / episodic activation

Build an episode timeline. Assess sleep, psychosis, safety, substances, medications, depression history and collateral. ASRM can quantify current manic symptoms but cannot establish episodicity or bipolar disorder.

Suicide risk

Use direct suicide-risk interview and clinical formulation. The Columbia Protocol can structure screening and triage. A risk screen supports action; it does not predict the future or replace formulation.

Substance use

Use direct substance history and age-appropriate screening such as AUDIT/AUDIT-C for alcohol and CRAFFT for adolescents. Stop screening once severity, immediate risks and treatment/referral needs are clear.

Dissociation / complex trauma

Use clinical dissociation interview and legitimate dissociation measures where available. DES-II or MID scores do not by themselves establish a dissociative disorder.

Sleep / insomnia

Begin with sleep history, opportunity, schedule, apnea/parasomnia/circadian issues, substances and medication. Do not let an insomnia score terminate evaluation for another sleep disorder.

Adaptive / developmental disability

Use developmental history, concrete functional interview and records. If a normed adaptive score is the decision variable, use or refer for an appropriate licensed adaptive measure. WHODAS is not a substitute for Vineland or ABAS.

FORMAL-TESTING BOUNDARY

No free screening battery adequately substitutes for formal normed cognitive, adaptive, academic-achievement, language, or visual-motor testing when those scores are actually required. Do not build homebrew substitutes.

SPANISH AND TRANSLATION RULE

Use official or validated Spanish versions of standardized measures when available. Do not translate test items locally and then interpret them as though standardization were preserved. Original clinic instructions, preparation guides, and score explanations can be bilingual.

ASSESSMENT TYPE BOUNDARIES

Screening — answers whether a domain deserves further inquiry. A positive screen is not a diagnosis.

Diagnostic assessment — integrates interview, history, impairment, collateral, differential diagnosis and, when needed, formal testing.

Severity monitoring — tracks symptom burden or change over time after the target condition has been clinically established or is being actively evaluated.

Risk assessment — supports immediate safety decisions. Risk tools do not predict the future and do not replace clinical formulation.

Formal standardized testing — answers questions that require normed scores, standardized administration and qualified interpretation. Do not substitute a homemade battery when the score itself is the decision variable.
