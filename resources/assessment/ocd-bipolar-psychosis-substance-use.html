OCD, BIPOLAR, PSYCHOSIS & SUBSTANCE USE

Clinician Start-Here Guide

USE THIS SHELF FOR

Focused screening and severity assessment when obsessive-compulsive symptoms, episodic activation, emerging psychosis risk, or substance use are part of the clinical question.

OCD

FOCI / C-FOCI

Best use: quick obsessive-compulsive symptom severity screening and repeated monitoring when a full clinician-rated OCD scale is unnecessary.

Recall: past 7 days.

Interpretation: elevated severity supports more detailed OCD assessment.

Do not interpret as: an OCD diagnosis or equivalence to Y-BOCS-II / CY-BOCS-II.

Next step: assess obsessions, compulsions, time consumed, avoidance, reassurance, mental rituals, impairment, insight and differential diagnosis.

Rights rule: use current official APA forms and the exact use terms that accompany the version. Clinician patient-use reproduction is permitted for the APA Level 2 forms; electronic or other use can have additional conditions.

Official source: APA DSM-5-TR Assessment Measures

BIPOLAR / EPISODIC ACTIVATION

ASRM / DSM-5-TR Level 2 Mania

Best use: very brief current mania/hypomania symptom severity screening when episodic activation, decreased need for sleep or bipolar spectrum is in the differential.

Adult ASRM: five items, past 7 days.

Interpretation: an elevated score warrants episode-level diagnostic clarification.

Do not interpret as: bipolar disorder.

Next step: build an episode timeline; review sleep, impairment, psychosis, safety, substances, medications, depression history and collateral.

Use current APA forms, including current response-scale labeling.

Official source: APA DSM-5-TR Assessment Measures

PSYCHOSIS-RISK CONCERN

PQ-B

Best use: specialty psychosis-risk screening when attenuated unusual perceptions or thoughts raise a genuine emerging-psychosis-risk question.

Do not interpret as: a general psychosis test, a diagnosis, or a substitute for a structured specialty interview.

UCSF's Loewy Lab states that PQ versions including PQ-B are free for research, clinical purposes and EHR platforms as long as users are not charged per use.

Escalate when dangerous commands, severe disorganization, acute psychosis, inability to care for self or major functional decline requires urgent or specialty evaluation.

Official source: UCSF Loewy Lab PQ

ALCOHOL

AUDIT / AUDIT-C

Best use: identification of hazardous or harmful alcohol use and brief assessment.

WHO developed AUDIT as a simple screening method for excessive drinking and as a framework for brief intervention.

Do not interpret as: a complete substance-use diagnosis or withdrawal-risk assessment.

Next step: assess quantity/frequency, consequences, withdrawal, medical risk, co-use, medications and treatment needs.

Official source: WHO AUDIT

ADOLESCENT SUBSTANCE USE

CRAFFT 2.1 / 2.1+N

Best use: adolescent substance-use screening. The developer describes CRAFFT as validated for ages 12–21.

CRAFFT 2.1 incorporates vaping and edibles; 2.1+N adds nicotine/tobacco screening.

Rights rule: the official site describes CRAFFT as free to use and provides instructions for paper or EMR reproduction.

Do not interpret as: a substance-use diagnosis without clinical evaluation.

Official source: CRAFFT

GENERAL SUBSTANCE RULE

Screening is only the front end. Always assess immediate medical risk, withdrawal, overdose, route, polysubstance use, prescribed/nonmedical use, nicotine when relevant, motivation, prior treatment and the level of care required.

DIFFERENTIAL RULE

Sleep deprivation, prescribed medications, intoxication/withdrawal, trauma, neurodevelopmental differences and medical conditions can alter attention, mood, perception and behavior. Use a screener to sharpen the question, not to force symptoms into the first category that produces a high score.

STOP RULE

Stop screening once severity, immediate risks and the next treatment, referral or diagnostic step are clear.
