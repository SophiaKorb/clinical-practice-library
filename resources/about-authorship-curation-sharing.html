About the Library

Authorship, Curation & Sharing

Sophia Cohon, PhD

Navigation principle

This library is organized by topic, not by author. Start with the clinical or practical question you are trying to solve. Original materials, official resources, open-access tools, and curated guides sit together in the relevant topic area.

Authorship is preserved for attribution and transparency, but it is not a browsing category.

Original materials

Many of the handouts, teaching tools, DBT-informed materials, school-access guides, and practical clinical toolkits were created by Sophia Cohon, PhD.

Those materials are integrated into the same topic shelves as related authoritative and external resources, including CBT and cognitive-flexibility handouts, anxiety and avoidance materials, parenting resources, sensory and transition supports, neurodevelopmental adaptations, and school-access tools.

Curated and outside-source material

Other resources in the library come from authoritative or external sources such as the U.S. Department of Veterans Affairs, World Health Organization, SAMHSA, NCTSN, state education and disability agencies, and other professional or public organizations.

When possible, outside manuals and official materials are linked to their primary source instead of copied into the library.

Sharing original materials

Original materials in this library may be shared for clinical care, teaching, supervision, training, and other noncommercial professional or educational use.

  • Keep authorship or source notes with the material.
  • Preserve citations, safety cautions, and scope limitations.
  • Do not present original library materials as official manuals for branded treatment models.
  • Do not redistribute commercial, licensed, or source-uncertain material unless the source explicitly permits it.
  • Examples and formatting may be adapted for accessibility or developmental level without removing substantive cautions.

Spanish materials

Patient- and family-facing materials are paired with Spanish versions when appropriate. Spanish-first navigation is centralized in the Español shelf so users do not have to move through English resources first.

Standardized measures are different. Do not independently translate test items or scoring forms. Use an official validated Spanish version when one exists.

Clinical boundary

These resources support clinical thinking and patient/family education. They do not replace individualized assessment, emergency evaluation, treatment-model training, supervision, or applicable law and policy.

Comments and improvements

Corrections, clinical disagreements, accessibility suggestions, better sources, and feedback about what did or did not work in practice are welcome.
