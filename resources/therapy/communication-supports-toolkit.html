Clinical Practice · Communication Access

Communication Supports Toolkit

Practical supports for neuropsychological assessment and clinical work when language, overload, memory demands, transitions, sensory access, or social ambiguity may interfere with accurate participation.

Start with the barrier, not the diagnosis. Choose the support that helps the person understand, respond, or participate more accurately while preserving autonomy and keeping the clinical construct as clean as possible.

Folder 01

Communication & Expression

Use when the person may know or understand more than they can easily express, or when open-ended verbal responding is becoming the barrier.

Response Menu

When to use

When open-ended speech may mask what the person knows or make answering unnecessarily hard.

Say it · Point to it · Choose · Show me · Write/draw · Not sure

Clinical point

Offer more than one response route, then confirm what the person meant.

When Words Get Stuck

When to use

When the person appears to know more than they can express, especially as frustration rises.

Pause · Point · Show · Choose · Try another way

Clinical point

“Can’t say it” is not the same as “doesn’t understand.”

Folder 02

Choices, Consent & Assent

Use when the person needs help understanding a choice, comparing options, communicating a preference, or showing willingness to continue.

Supported Consent

When to use

When language demands may distort apparent understanding of consent information.

What is happening? · Why? · Choices · Risks/benefits · Questions

Clinical point

Support comprehension without steering the decision.

Decision Support Menu

When to use

When options or consequences need to be made concrete enough to compare.

Option A ↔ Option B · What changes? · What matters to me?

Clinical point

Avoid false choices and loaded wording.

Concrete Choice Board

When to use

When generating an answer is harder than recognizing one, or too many words and choices are overwhelming.

This · That · Neither · Other/different

Clinical point

Always leave room for an answer you did not anticipate.

Supported Assent

When to use

When the person needs a clear, usable way to show whether they want to continue.

Yes · No · Not sure · Break · Stop

Clinical point

Assent supports participation and autonomy. It does not replace legal consent.

Folder 03

Safety, Regulation & Access

Use when distress, pain, sensory load, fatigue, or the environment may be changing communication or performance.

Emotion / Overload Thermometer

When to use

When emotion language is hard or regulation is visibly shifting.

0 Calm · 1–2 Building · 3 Hard · 4–5 Too much

Clinical point

A shared intensity scale can support communication, but it is not a full risk assessment.

Body + Safety Check

When to use

When behavior may be signaling pain, fear, fatigue, hunger, illness, or another basic need.

Pain? · Tired? · Hungry/thirsty? · Scared? · Need a break?

Clinical point

Treat endorsed pain or danger as information, not as noncompliance.

Sensory + Visual Load Check

When to use

When noise, light, movement, people, clutter, or visual complexity may be making access harder.

Noise · Light · Movement · People · Clutter · Visual load

Clinical point

More visuals are not always more accessible. For some people, including people with CVI, extra visual material can worsen access.

Folder 04

Transitions & Flexibility

Use when the next step, an upcoming change, or a disrupted plan is creating uncertainty, overload, or loss of access.

First / Then Board

When to use

When initiation, transitions, or working-memory demands make “what happens next” hard to hold onto.

FIRST: what is happening now → THEN: what comes next

Clinical point

Use “then” to clarify sequence, not as a bribe for basic needs.

Transition Countdown

When to use

When a transition is easier if the change is predictable before it arrives.

5 minutes · 2 minutes · 1 minute · Change

Clinical point

Countdowns predict change. They should not function as threats.

Plan B Flexibility Map

When to use

When Plan A becomes unavailable and the change itself is creating overload.

Plan A changed → What stayed the same? → Plan B → What helps?

Clinical point

Plan B is a map for change, not forced compliance.

Folder 05

Learning, Memory & Generalization

Use when you need to separate memory from response demands, organize an event, or help a learned skill transfer across settings.

Free Recall vs Recognition

When to use

When free recall is weaker than expected and you need to know whether the problem is storage, retrieval, or response demand.

Tell me what you remember → Now choose what you recognize

Clinical point

Interpret the pattern, not a single score in isolation.

What Happened?

When to use

When the person cannot easily generate a coherent event narrative without structure.

Before → What happened → After → What do we know?

Clinical point

Organize sequence without leading the person or assuming causation.

Generalization Bridge

When to use

When a skill works in one setting but seems to disappear somewhere else.

Works here → What is different there? → What support travels with it?

Clinical point

One successful performance does not establish mastery across contexts.

Folder 06

Social Meaning & Repair

Use when fluent language is hiding difficulty with implied meaning, ambiguity, misunderstanding, or conversational repair.

Social Meaning Decoder

When to use

When literal words are understood but the implied social meaning remains unclear.

What was said? · What might it mean? · What else could it mean? · What can I ask?

Clinical point

Reduce ambiguity rather than turning every interaction into a social quiz.

Conversation Repair Card

When to use

When misunderstanding is building frustration and the person needs ready-to-use clarification language.

Say that another way · Show me · Give me choices · I meant something different

Clinical point

Clarification is a communication skill, not a communication failure.
