Clinical Stance

The goal is not to make an autistic young person look more typical. Useful social work improves access to relationships, communication, consent, boundaries, self-advocacy, conflict navigation, bullying recognition, and safety while respecting autistic communication and regulation.

Do not make eye contact, suppression of harmless stimming, small talk, stillness, or masking into treatment goals unless the young person identifies a specific functional reason.

Evidence-Based-Practice Implementation

AFIRM Modules

What to Teach Directly

  • what makes a relationship reciprocal rather than one-sided
  • signs that someone is joking versus repeatedly humiliating
  • direct and indirect bullying, exclusion, rumor-spreading, coercion, and cyberbullying
  • how to say no, leave, block, report, or ask an adult for help
  • boundaries around touch, possessions, privacy, money, images, passwords, and personal information
  • how to recognize “friendship” that mainly involves favors, pressure, ridicule, or exploitation
  • repair after misunderstandings
  • settings where shared interests make genuine friendship more likely

For Homeschool Families

Homeschooling can remove a harmful school environment without automatically solving loneliness, social anxiety, friendship difficulty, or vulnerability to exploitation. Build repeated, interest-based contact rather than random “socialization.” Prefer settings where the same people meet repeatedly and there is a shared activity.

Track whether homeschooling is making the young person's world safer and richer, or simply smaller.

School Planning

Bullying that affects educational access may need to be addressed through the school team. Use the School – IEP & 504 shelf for barrier-to-support planning. Do not place the burden of preventing bullying solely on the autistic student by assigning “better social skills.”

Clinical Cautions

Bullying is not ordinary mutual conflict when there is a power imbalance, targeted humiliation, coercion, threats, repeated exclusion, exploitation, or fear. Do not teach indiscriminate compliance as a social skill. Do not interpret every peer problem as an autism deficit. Group norms, prejudice, ableism, school climate, adult response, and the behavior of peers matter.
