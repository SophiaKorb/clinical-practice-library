Clinical Use

  • Begin with safety, medical/substance contributors, sleep, medication effects, and level-of-care needs before assuming a purely psychotherapeutic problem.
  • Do not directly argue a patient out of a delusion. Work with distress, meaning, coping, uncertainty, functioning, and alternative explanations while preserving rapport.
  • Distinguish unusual experiences from dangerous commands, severe disorganization, catatonia, mania, delirium, or intoxication/withdrawal.
  • Include family/support systems when appropriate and with consent.
  • Rehabilitation is treatment: social skills, supported education/employment, cognitive/compensatory strategies, and daily-life recovery goals matter alongside symptom reduction.

Training Boundary

A brief CBTp overview is not equivalent to competence in full CBT for psychosis. Use specialty training or consultation when CBTp is the primary treatment.
