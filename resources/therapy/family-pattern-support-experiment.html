Family pattern → safer response → shared review

A clinician guide and printable family planning sheet for recurring conflict. Start with safety, then observe the interaction pattern and test one manageable change.

Joint work requires a safety decision first.

Assess coercion, intimidation, violence, abuse, and fear of retaliation. Routine shared communication exercises may be unsafe in those circumstances. Use private assessment, safeguarding, and specialist pathways as indicated; do not frame abuse as a mutual communication problem.

Clinician layer: preserve asymmetry and context

Observe demand → interpretation → action → response → aftermath. Separate intention from impact; map who holds authority and who can safely disagree. A child's dysregulation does not remove caregiver responsibility for safety. Avoid requiring disclosure or apologies as the price of a break.

If substance use is part of the pattern, coordinate appropriate treatment and clarify consent, confidentiality, and family participation. Family involvement can help care, but its form depends on risk and the person's preferences. The SAMHSA TIP 39 advisory is a source orientation, not a protocol for every family.

A fictional worked sequence

DemandA caregiver gives three instructions as a tired child arrives home.
ResponseThe child argues; the caregiver repeats more loudly.
AftermathBoth withdraw; the task remains unclear.

Competing explanations include overload, ambiguous first step, conflict expectations, and escape from an aversive task. Test a short landing period plus one visible first step. Preserve the expectation and permit a supported pause. Review starts, prompts, escalation, recovery time, and both perspectives; this does not prove a diagnosis.

Our one-change plan

Client / caregiver sheet. Use blank or fictional information on shared devices. These fields are not saved by this tool.

When the experiment is not helping

Check whether the expectation fits age and capacity, whether the support was actually available, and whether the pause increases risk or avoidance. Revisit pain, sleep, trauma, substance use, language, and environmental demands. Escalating punishment is not a substitute for a clearer formulation.

Support staff can help observe routines and implement an agreed plan; assessment of abuse, diagnostic formulation, and specialized family therapy require appropriate clinical roles and supervision.
