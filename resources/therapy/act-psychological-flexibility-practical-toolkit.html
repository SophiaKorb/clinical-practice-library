ACT & Psychological Flexibility

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Original shareable tools for psychological flexibility work. These materials are not an official ACT protocol.

Clinician Tool: The Stuck Loop

Describe the difficult internal experience: thoughts, feelings, memories, sensations, urges, or self-stories. Then identify what the patient does to get rid of, control, avoid, or obey that experience.

Ask what the strategy accomplishes in the short term and what it costs over time. Finish with a workability question: If this pattern continues, does it move the patient toward or away from the life they want?

Clinician Tool: Values to Action

Direction: What kind of person does the patient want to be in this area of life? Values are directions, not achievements.

Barrier: What thought, feeling, memory, or urge tends to show up when the patient moves in that direction?

Willingness: What discomfort is the patient willing to make room for in service of the chosen direction?

Small action: What can the patient do in the next 24 to 72 hours that is observable and small enough to happen?

Clinician Tool: Defusion Without Arguing

When a thought is sticky, shift from “Is this thought true?” to “What happens when the patient treats this thought as a command or literal fact?”

Try language such as “I am noticing the thought that…” or “My mind is giving me the story that…” only if it helps create useful distance. The goal is not to make the thought disappear. It is to loosen its control over behavior.

Patient Handout: You Do Not Have to Win an Argument With Your Mind

Your mind can produce convincing warnings, criticism, memories, and predictions. Trying to defeat every thought can become another full-time job.

You can practice noticing a thought without automatically following it. Name what your mind is doing. Notice what feeling comes with it. Then ask what action would move you toward the person you want to be, even if the thought stays.

The goal is not “never feel anxious” or “stop having negative thoughts.” The goal is more freedom to choose what you do next.

Clinical Boundary

ACT processes can be integrated into many treatments, but a few metaphors or defusion exercises do not constitute competent delivery of a full protocol. Keep the treatment target, evidence base, and patient preference visible.
