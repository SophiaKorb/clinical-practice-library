Visual Access Tools — Replacement Review

Seventeen age-respectful, black-line visual supports designed to replace inconsistent pictograms across the CPL communication toolkit. Each is a standalone, US Letter printable page with readable text and reusable artwork. No original page is changed by this review draft.

Clinical safety: These are communication and participation supports, not diagnostic measures, capacity determinations, or substitutes for individualized safety plans. Confirm meanings with the person, respect refusal, and use established AAC systems when present.

Notice Early Signs

Observe individual patterns without guessing what a person feels.

Usual state

What does the person typically do?

Their own cues: __________________
Something is changing

Less speech, more movement, withdrawal, repeated question—or something else.

Their own cues: __________________
Hard right now

Reduce demand and follow the person’s communication.

What helped: __________________
Settling afterward

Give time; check whether reconnecting is wanted.

What they preferred: __________________
Avoid assigning feelings from appearance

Someone may appear calm while distressed, or look upset without feeling unsafe. Ask and verify when possible.

This is a descriptive aid, not a medical or suicide-risk assessment.CPL review draft · not yet validated

Words That Help

Brief, respectful phrasing when communication is difficult.

Instead of…
Try…
“Calm down.”
“Would you like space or company?”
“Why are you doing this?”
“Something seems hard. What would help?”
“I already told you.”
“I can show you the next step.”
“Use your words.”
“You can point, draw, type, or show me.”
Before / during / after

Before: show a plan. During: fewer words and safety. After: ask what worked and adapt together.

What sounds natural and respectful to this person?
