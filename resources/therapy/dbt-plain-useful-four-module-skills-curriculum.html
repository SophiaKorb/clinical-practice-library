DBT-Informed & Emotion Regulation – Practical Toolkit

Original shareable tools for skills-informed psychotherapy. These are not official Linehan DBT materials and do not substitute for comprehensive DBT training.

Clinician Tool: Behavior Sequence Map

Start with the behavior you are trying to understand rather than the diagnosis.

Prompting event

What happened immediately before the sequence began?

Vulnerabilities

What made the patient more sensitive that day? Consider sleep, hunger, pain, conflict, substance use, shame, isolation, sensory load, illness, or previous stress.

Links

What thoughts, body sensations, emotions, urges, interpretations, and small actions happened between the prompting event and the target behavior?

Target behavior

Describe exactly what the patient did.

Immediate consequences

What changed right away? What relief, attention, escape, numbness, control, or other short-term effect followed?

Longer-term consequences

What happened to relationships, safety, trust, work, health, or self-respect?

Skill point

Where in the chain is there a realistic place to insert a different action next time?

Clinician Tool: Skill Coaching That Actually Transfers

Choose one skill, not six. Explain what problem it is supposed to solve. Practice it in session. Identify the real-world cue that should trigger its use. Make the first version easy enough to remember under stress. Review what happened next session without shaming failure.

When a skill “did not work,” ask whether it was used too late, misunderstood, inaccessible in the setting, mismatched to the function of the behavior, or expected to eliminate emotion rather than help the patient act effectively.

Clinician Tool: Crisis Survival vs Life Building

Crisis-survival work asks: How do we get through the next minutes or hours without making the situation worse?

Life-building work asks: What changes reduce the number of crises over time?

Do not let short-term survival strategies become the whole treatment. Once immediate safety is addressed, return to relationships, routines, meaning, work, health, sleep, boundaries, and problem solving.

Patient Handout: When Emotion Is at 90 Out of 100

When emotion is extremely high, the goal is usually not to solve your whole life. The first job is to avoid adding a second problem.

Slow the sequence down. Notice the urge. Put space between the urge and the action. Reduce immediate danger. Use a skill that helps your body come down enough to choose rather than react.

Later, when the intensity is lower, ask what set the situation up, what you needed, what made things worse, and what you want to do differently next time.

Strong emotion is real. It does not have to make every decision for you.

Hoja para pacientes: Cuando la emoción está en 90 de 100

Cuando una emoción está extremadamente intensa, la meta normalmente no es resolver toda su vida. El primer trabajo es no crear un segundo problema.

Haga más lenta la secuencia. Observe la urgencia. Ponga espacio entre la urgencia y la acción. Reduzca el peligro inmediato. Use una habilidad que ayude a que su cuerpo baje lo suficiente para poder elegir en vez de reaccionar.

Después, cuando la intensidad sea menor, pregúntese qué preparó la situación, qué necesitaba, qué empeoró las cosas y qué quiere hacer diferente la próxima vez.

La emoción intensa es real. No tiene que tomar todas las decisiones por usted.

Clinical Boundary

Suicidal behavior, self-harm, severe substance use, eating-disorder behaviors, violence risk, or other high-risk patterns require appropriate risk assessment and level-of-care decisions. A coping skill sheet is not a safety plan.
