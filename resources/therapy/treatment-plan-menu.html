This first pass emphasizes breadth and qualitative signs of change; exact thresholds, baselines and deadlines belong to a later collaborative refinement. A self-help practice, even when a clinician coaches it, is not the same as a therapist-delivered intervention. Clinician interventions require appropriate assessment, training, consent and scope of practice. All examples are starting points, not universal clinical targets. Sources, clinical scope, and previous plan builder

These are original, editable ideas, not a licensed treatment planner, diagnosis algorithm, or required sequence. The current breadth pass is not a numerical target-setting tool. Treatment appropriateness depends on formulation, cultural and individual context, access, consent, and clinician qualifications. Exposure practices and eating-disorder treatment require appropriately qualified guidance; medical concerns require suitable medical assessment and coordination.

Standardized measures are identified separately with external source links; confirm original administration, interpretation and permissions. An individually devised frequency count or rating is not a validated symptom measure. Repeated measurements must inform collaborative decisions rather than compliance judgments.
