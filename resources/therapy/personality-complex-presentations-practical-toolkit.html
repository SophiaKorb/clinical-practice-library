Clinical Practice · Practical Toolkit

Personality & Complex Presentations

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Original shareable tools for structured generalist work with chronic interpersonal instability, recurrent crises, self-damaging patterns, and complex personality presentations.

Clinician Tool: Treatment Frame

Write down the treatment target in plain language. Define session frequency, between-session contact, crisis pathways, attendance expectations, medication roles, consultation needs, and what the clinician can and cannot provide.

A frame should be predictable enough that the patient does not have to rediscover the rules during every crisis.

Clinician Tool: Interpersonal Episode Map

Trigger: What happened?

Meaning: What did the patient believe the other person's action meant?

Emotion and urge: What feeling and action urge followed?

Strategy: What did the patient do to protect themselves, obtain reassurance, retaliate, escape, regain control, or test the relationship?

Other person's response: What happened next?

Long-term effect: Did the strategy move the relationship toward or away from what the patient wanted?

Alternative: What could preserve both the patient's need and the relationship more effectively next time?

Clinician Tool: Crisis Without Reinforcing Crisis

Assess actual risk carefully and respond proportionately. Avoid making every intense state into an emergency that receives more structure than ordinary treatment, while also avoiding dismissiveness.

Clinician Tool: Team Consistency

For patients seen by multiple providers, clarify who manages psychotherapy, medication, crisis response, case management, and care coordination. Inconsistent provider responses can unintentionally intensify splitting, confusion, or repeated crisis escalation.

Patient Handout: Therapy When Relationships Feel Intense

When relationships feel very important and very uncertain, your mind may move quickly from “we are okay” to “everything is ruined.” Therapy can help slow down that sequence.

A useful question is: What do I want from this relationship, and will the action I am about to take move me closer to that?

Clinical Boundary

Recurrent self-harm, suicidality, violence risk, severe eating-disorder behavior, substance use, mania, psychosis, dissociation, or medical instability require condition-specific assessment and level-of-care decisions.
