Start With the Target Hierarchy

When several problems are present, do not let the most emotionally vivid topic automatically become the session target. Address life-threatening behavior and acute safety first, then behavior that interferes with treatment, then severe quality-of-life problems, while continuing to build skills and a life the patient experiences as worth participating in.

Practice Orientation

  • Begin with the function of the behavior and the immediate treatment target.
  • Validate the understandable part without validating an ineffective behavior as the only option.
  • Move from global labels to observable sequences: prompting event, vulnerabilities, links, behavior, consequences.
  • Teach one skill in enough detail that the patient can actually use it.
  • Practice in session, troubleshoot barriers, and define the next real-world cue for use.
  • Distinguish crisis-survival strategies from longer-term life-building strategies.
  • When suicidal or self-harm behaviors are present, use appropriate risk assessment, safety planning, level-of-care decisions, and consultation. A skills worksheet is not a risk-management plan.

When a Skill “Didn’t Work”

Ask what the patient noticed, when they noticed it, which skill they selected, whether they could access it at that arousal level, what they actually did, and what consequence followed. A skill may have been poorly matched, introduced too late, insufficiently practiced, inaccessible under the circumstances, or used correctly without producing immediate relief. The goal is effective behavior, not proof that distress disappeared.
