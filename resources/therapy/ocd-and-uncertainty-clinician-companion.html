OCD and Uncertainty

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Use alongside the matching client/family resources.

Best Fit

Use when OCD has been assessed or is a strong working formulation and the treatment target involves reassurance, checking, avoidance, mental rituals, or other compulsions.

Clinical Targets

  • Identify the obsession-compulsion-reassurance cycle by function, not content.
  • Develop collaborative, graded ERP targets.
  • Differentiate support from accommodation and reassurance.
  • Measure progress by willingness and reduced ritualizing, not calmness.

Cautions and Differential Considerations

  • Do not conduct exposure to actual danger, non-consensual situations, medically necessary precautions, trauma boundaries, or culturally/religiously normative practices without careful assessment.
  • Avoid turning cognitive discussion into reassurance or certainty-seeking.
  • Severe depression, suicidality, psychosis, eating-disorder risk, or complex medical themes may require coordinated or specialty treatment.

Implementation

  • Name the compulsion precisely, including covert review, self-reassurance, confession, comparison, and online research.
  • Select one manageable exposure and one response-prevention target.
  • Plan supporter language in advance so validation does not become repeated certainty-giving.
  • Review what the patient did while doubt was present rather than whether anxiety decreased.

Source guidance

The International OCD Foundation describes planned, collaborative exposure with response prevention. Practice is developed with a clinician trained in ERP.
