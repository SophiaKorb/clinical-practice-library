5. Clinical Use

  • Start with the child's functional needs, developmental profile, context, strengths, and goals rather than treating a diagnostic label as an intervention plan.
  • Separate assessment from advocacy.
  • For behavior, ask what reliably precedes it, what follows it, what skill or access problem may be involved, and what environmental changes alter it.
  • Parent training, classroom intervention, direct child treatment, sensory/developmental supports, medication, and school accommodations solve different problems.
  • For autism resources, combine intervention evidence with the autistic person's own goals, assent, communication needs, sensory needs, and quality of life.
