05 / Two Perspectives

Compare experiences without deciding what another person must have felt.

Two people can experience the same event differently.

We can ask rather than assume what someone else thought or felt.

MY PERSPECTIVE

What I experienced or noticed

I noticed...

I felt...

I needed...

YOUR PERSPECTIVE

What you tell me you experienced

You noticed...

You felt...

You needed...
WHAT WE BOTH KNOW

What happened that both people agree on? What still needs checking?

Stay curious“Could we check whether I understood you?” is more useful than guessing another person’s intent.

12 / Words That Help

What an adult or communication partner can say and do.

Try language that makes a response possible.

These are example partner phrases—not scripts the person must answer. Wait, offer alternatives, and verify understanding.

LESS HELPFUL
MORE HELPFUL
“Calm down.”
“I’m here. Would quiet, space, or help be useful?”
“Why are you doing that?”
“What happened? You can show me or tell me.”
“Just try harder.”
“Want a smaller first step or a break?”
“You’re fine.”
“I can see this is hard. What should I understand?”
