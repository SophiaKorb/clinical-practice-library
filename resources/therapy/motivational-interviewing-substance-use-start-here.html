Clinical Use

  • Separate readiness for one specific change from “motivation” as a global personality trait.
  • Use MI spirit and skills to explore ambivalence, not as a disguised persuasion script.
  • Match treatment intensity to withdrawal risk, overdose risk, severity, co-occurring conditions, environment, and level-of-care needs.
  • Contingency management is a structured behavioral intervention, not “bribing the patient.”
  • Harm reduction, abstinence-oriented treatment, medications, and recovery supports can address different needs and should not be treated as mutually exclusive by default.
  • Integrate overdose prevention, medication options, and medical referral when clinically indicated.
