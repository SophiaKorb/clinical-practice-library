Sleep, Pain & Health Psychology

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Original shareable tools for behavioral-health work with insomnia, chronic pain, and medical coping.

Clinician Tool: Insomnia Differential Before Sleep Advice

Clarify whether the problem is sleep onset, staying asleep, early waking, irregular schedule, excessive sleepiness, nightmares, or nonrestorative sleep. Review sleep opportunity, circadian pattern, snoring/apnea, restless legs, parasomnias, pain, substances, caffeine, medications, medical conditions, mood episodes, trauma symptoms, and daytime napping.

Use a sleep diary when appropriate. Avoid assuming generic sleep hygiene is sufficient treatment for chronic insomnia.

Clinician Tool: Sleep Diary Fields

Record bedtime, estimated sleep onset, awakenings, final waking time, time out of bed, naps, major substance/caffeine use, and a simple daytime functioning rating. Use patterns rather than one bad night to guide decisions.

Clinician Tool: Pain and Function Map

Pain pattern: What activities, positions, times, stressors, or medical factors change pain?

Protective behavior: What does the patient stop doing because of pain or fear of pain?

Boom-bust cycle: Does the patient overdo activity on better days and then require prolonged recovery?

Functional target: Choose a life activity worth rebuilding gradually.

Pacing plan: Define an amount that is sustainable rather than alternating between total avoidance and overexertion.

Patient Handout: Sleep Is a Process, Not a Performance

Trying harder to sleep can make sleep feel like a test. Your job is to create conditions that make sleep more likely and to follow a consistent plan. The body still does the sleeping.

Patient Handout: Pain and Function Can Move on Different Tracks

Pain intensity matters, but it is not the only outcome worth watching. Treatment may also aim to increase walking, sleep, work, parenting, social contact, exercise, or confidence in movement.

Pacing means finding a sustainable amount of activity and building gradually. It is different from avoiding all activity, and it is different from pushing until you crash.

Clinical Boundary

Possible sleep apnea, parasomnias, severe restless legs, mania, medication complications, new neurological symptoms, rapidly changing pain, or other medical red flags require appropriate medical evaluation.
