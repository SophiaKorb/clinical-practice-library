Clinician Tool: One-Page CBT Formulation

  1. Define the problem in observable terms. What happens, where does it happen, how often, and what is the patient unable to do because of it?
  2. Map the current loop. Situation or trigger; interpretation or prediction; emotion/body response; behavior or avoidance; short-term payoff; long-term cost.
  3. Pick the smallest useful treatment target. Choose one leverage point for the next week.
  4. Turn the intervention into an experiment. Prediction, action, observation, review.

Clinician Tool: Behavioral Activation Without the Pep Talk

Start with function, not motivation. Depression often narrows activity before motivation returns.

Ask what has become smaller in the patient's life, what used to provide pleasure, mastery, connection, movement, meaning, or structure, and which activities are realistically available now.

Build one action specific enough to occur. Track the effect on functioning rather than demanding an immediate mood improvement.

Clinician Tool: Between-Session Practice Troubleshooter

If practice did not happen, do not automatically call it resistance. Check whether the task was too large, too vague, easy to forget, poorly timed, emotionally costly, inaccessible, or disconnected from the patient's goal.

Repair the plan by shrinking it, adding a cue, changing the setting, practicing it once in session, or choosing a different intervention.

Patient Handout: When Your Brain Says “Wait Until You Feel Better”

Depression and anxiety often tell you to wait for the right feeling before you act. Unfortunately, the feeling may not arrive first.

A different experiment is to take one small useful action before motivation shows up. The goal is not to force happiness. The goal is to give your brain new information and keep your life from getting smaller.

Choose one action that takes about 5 to 20 minutes. Make it concrete. Decide when and where you will do it. Afterward, notice what happened to your energy, stress, sense of accomplishment, connection, or ability to do the next thing.
