Therapy Adaptation Checklist

  • Make the treatment agenda explicit and visible.
  • Reduce unnecessary language load and explain figurative or abstract language.
  • Externalize steps, plans, deadlines, and between-session practice.
  • Treat communication mode as access, not compliance.
  • Ask about sensory load, processing time, and recovery cost.
  • Distinguish “won't” from “can't yet,” “cannot under this load,” and “does not understand what is being asked.”
  • Build skills in the context where they are expected to occur.
  • Do not make eye contact, stillness, typical affect display, or suppression of harmless self-regulation into treatment goals without a specific functional reason.
  • For executive-function problems, change the environment and task architecture as well as teaching internal strategies.

Boundaries

Autism/ADHD adaptation is not a separate psychotherapy brand. Keep the evidence-based treatment target intact while changing access, pacing, communication, and implementation. Do not use screening scores or a preferred neurotype explanation to bypass appropriate differential assessment.
