Anxiety, Exposure & OCD

Created by Sophia Cohon, PhD

Original shareable clinical tools for approach-based treatment and ERP-informed planning.

Clinician Tool: Avoidance and Ritual Map

Name the trigger. Then ask what the patient predicts, what they do to reduce danger or uncertainty, what relief occurs immediately, and what the pattern teaches the brain over time.

Include visible avoidance, reassurance seeking, checking, researching, confessing, asking others to decide, mental review, neutralizing, distraction used as escape, and covert rituals when relevant.

Clinician Tool: Exposure Design

Feared outcome: What does the patient believe might happen?

Learning target: What would be useful to learn or practice?

Exposure: What situation, thought, image, object, sensation, or uncertainty will the patient approach voluntarily?

Response prevention: What ritual, reassurance, checking, escape, or mental neutralizing will the patient practice not doing?

Consent and safety: The patient should understand the rationale and agree to the exercise. Do not surprise, deceive, humiliate, or coerce the patient. Do not use exposure to override actual medical, legal, interpersonal, or environmental safety.

Review: Ask what the patient noticed, what they learned, what urge showed up, what they did instead of the ritual, and what should be repeated or adjusted.

Clinician Tool: Reassurance Check

Before answering a repeated “Are you sure?” question, ask whether the response will provide useful information once or briefly lower anxiety while strengthening the need for more reassurance.

A collaborative response can acknowledge uncertainty, name the OCD/anxiety loop when appropriate, and help the patient choose the next workable action without pretending absolute certainty exists.

Patient Handout: Practice Uncertainty, Not Certainty

Anxiety and OCD often demand a guarantee before you move forward. The more guarantees you seek, the more important certainty starts to feel.

Exposure practice is not about proving that nothing bad can ever happen. It is practice doing what matters while uncertainty is present and reducing the rituals that keep the alarm system in charge.

Clinical Boundary

OCD-specific ERP requires competent assessment of obsessions, compulsions, avoidance, family accommodation, insight, safety, and co-occurring conditions. Severe or complex presentations may require specialty consultation or a higher level of care.
