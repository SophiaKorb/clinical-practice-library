Assessment: question → evidence → useful feedback

A clinician and supervised-intern workflow for psychological, developmental, and neuropsychological assessment. Choose measures to answer a decision, rather than assembling a battery by diagnosis alone.

SeeingSpecify onset, trajectory, settings, and everyday impact.
ExplainingCompare developmental, psychiatric, neurological, medical, educational, and contextual hypotheses.
AssessingChoose evidence that can change the decision.
ActingOffer appropriate access supports and referrals now.
ToolsUse interviews, collateral, observation, and licensed measures where indicated.
ReviewingCheck whether recommendations changed everyday functioning.

Before selecting instruments
  1. Clarify who requested the evaluation, the decision it will inform, consent, confidentiality, and recipients.
  2. Determine competence, supervision, measure qualifications, language access, and accommodations.
  3. Collect relevant records with authorization; separate reported history from verified facts.
  4. Define competing hypotheses and what evidence would support or weaken each.
  5. Select the smallest adequate set of methods. Avoid duplicating constructs without a reason.

No universal battery is supplied here. ADOS-2 training does not itself establish competence for every autism evaluation; a score is one part of integrated assessment. Adaptive-functioning measures describe everyday performance in context. Use current publisher requirements and manuals; do not reproduce items, stimuli, or scoring rules.

Neuropsychology and communication: interpret the response channel

Low spoken output can affect performance on tasks requiring verbal responses. It is not by itself evidence of low comprehension or global cognitive impairment. Consider speech-language assessment, sensory and motor access, education, neurological history, fatigue, mood, and response reliability.

Document deviations from standardized procedures and their implications. A clinically useful adapted observation may not support a norm-referenced score. Mark qualitative findings separately. Sudden new aphasia or focal neurological symptoms belongs on an urgent medical pathway.

Fictional case: forgetting appointments

An adult misses appointments but accurately recalls conversations once cued. Possible mechanisms include prospective memory, initiation, routine instability, sleep, or avoidance. Ask how the plan is encoded, where reminders appear, and what happens at departure time. A memory complaint alone does not determine which memory system is affected.

A visible calendar and a departure cue can support access while assessment proceeds. Compare attendance, prompting, and the person's burden. Improvement does not establish etiology, and persistent misses require a revised formulation.

Feedback that changes functioning

Clinician / intern

For each conclusion, state supporting evidence, alternative explanations, confidence, and limits. Tie each recommendation to an identified problem, responsible person, feasible action, and review date.

Client / caregiver

“What makes sense to you? What does not fit? Which change would help first?” Use a written or visual summary and teach-back without making the person perform understanding.

Case manager / support staff

Coordinate authorized records and referrals, observe implementation, and close the loop. Staff gather observations and support the plan within their role; independent test interpretation requires appropriate qualifications.

Progress check

Track one real task, support level, participation, distress, and burden. Reassess if recommendations are unusable or functioning changes.
