What are these different therapy approaches?

A curious, accessible tour of psychotherapy approaches for anyone refreshing how they think about clinical practice.

CBT

Explores the interaction between thoughts, emotions and actions, and tests new responses.

Example: A person expecting rejection experiments with one small social interaction.

ACT

Builds psychological flexibility: moving toward values even when difficult thoughts and feelings are present.

DBT

Combines acceptance and change with skills for emotion regulation, distress tolerance and relationships.

Exposure and ERP

Helps people approach feared situations while reducing unnecessary avoidance and rituals.

Behavioral activation

Rebuild meaningful activity even when motivation is low.

Example: A person tries a manageable activity and reviews what happened.

Motivational interviewing

Explore ambivalence and the person's own reasons for change without pressure.

Example: A case manager reflects both sides of a decision.

Interpersonal therapy

Focus on grief, role changes, conflict and social connection.

Example: Someone adjusts to a major life transition.

Psychodynamic therapy

Explore repeating patterns, emotions and relational expectations.

Example: Someone notices a familiar expectation of criticism.

Supportive psychotherapy

Strengthen coping, understanding, choices and connection.

Example: During a crisis a clinician helps with stabilization.

Family and systemic therapy

Work with patterns between people, communication and context.

Example: A family practices a different response to conflict.

Trauma-focused approaches

Use condition-specific protocols delivered by trained clinicians.

Example: A person with PTSD considers appropriate evidence-based care.

Humanistic and person-centered therapy

Use empathy and collaborative exploration to foster self-understanding.

Example: A person clarifies important feelings and choices.

How to choose

Match the approach to the person's needs, preferences, age, setting and evidence for the condition, as well as the clinician's training. Different approaches overlap, but using a technique does not mean delivering a complete specialist protocol.
