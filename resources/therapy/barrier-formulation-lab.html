Clinical Practice · Layered tools

Barrier → hypothesis → support → learning

A layered formulation workbench: preserve competing explanations while choosing a small, observable change. A support trial informs a hypothesis; it does not establish a diagnosis.

What am I seeing?Describe behavior, demand, setting, and change from baseline.
What might explain it?Keep medical, developmental, relational, and learning explanations open.
What should I assess?Identify missing information and the next discriminating observation.
What can I do?Reduce a barrier while preserving a meaningful goal.
Which tool helps?Match format to access, skills, and preference.
How will I evaluate?Track functioning, autonomy, distress, and burden.
Change from baseline changes the route. Sudden loss of communication, new neurological signs, severe confusion, or acute safety concerns requires prompt medical or crisis assessment according to the situation and local procedures. Do not substitute a worksheet for urgent evaluation.

Worked case: silence after an open-ended question

An autistic adult provides detailed written answers but becomes silent when asked to summarize an upsetting event aloud. Language production load, uncertainty about what counts as an answer, trauma-related activation, and relational safety are all plausible; silence alone cannot distinguish them.

ExplanationDiscriminating informationSupport and outcome
Expressive or processing loadComprehension with low-output response; baseline communication profile; SLP collaboration if indicated.Offer writing or pointing, one prompt, and time. Measure accurate self-expression and preference.
Fear / uncertaintyAsk what feels risky; compare predictable and unexpected prompts without forcing disclosure.Preview the topic and permit pause/decline. Measure agency, distress, and return to the chosen task.
Trauma activation or unsafe relationshipExplore triggers, safety, consent, and context privately as appropriate.Stabilize safety and collaborate on pacing. Track tolerable engagement; do not use increased speech alone as success.

Improvement with writing supports access. It does not rule out anxiety or prove a language disorder. Failure to improve may reflect a poor trial, insufficient time, an unsafe context, or a different mechanism.

Advanced layer: avoid false causal certainty

Document predictions before a trial. Note simultaneous changes, expectancy effects, fatigue, medication changes, and measurement error. Do not withdraw a necessary accommodation simply to create a comparison. A null result is information, not evidence of unwillingness.

Distinguish task completion from independent access. Count prompting, recovery time, distress, and whether the person could decline. Apparent compliance can hide loss of agency.
