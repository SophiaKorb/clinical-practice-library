Doing What Matters in Times of Stress

Practical Clinical Frame

  • Name the stuck pattern in behavioral terms.
  • Clarify what the patient is trying not to feel, think, remember, or experience.
  • Distinguish symptom-control goals from life-direction goals.
  • Build willingness in service of a chosen value, not suffering for its own sake.
  • Use defusion to change the function of thoughts rather than argue every thought away.
  • Translate values into small, observable committed actions.
  • Return repeatedly to workability: “What happens when you do this, and does it move life in the direction you want?”

Do Not Turn Acceptance Into Endurance

Acceptance applies to internal experiences that cannot be usefully controlled in the moment. It does not require accepting abuse, discrimination, unsafe conditions, preventable pain, or unnecessary barriers. Ask whether the patient needs willingness toward an internal experience, problem solving in the environment, boundary setting, advocacy, or some combination.
