# CPL post-release audit — 77 externally linked resources

**Date audited:** November 16, 2026. **Production baseline SHA:** `af79333d1317c1d4dbce0798aafe14df49644c34`. **Audit candidate:** `audit/cpl-77-source-eligibility-20261116`. Production unchanged. **Scope:** 77 original external publisher URLs previously added in PR #44; 609 total CPL finder entries preserved.

## Method and confidence

Each of the 77 publisher destinations was individually submitted to the public-page fetcher. The result is **not** a universal 77/77 live-browser HTTP success claim: 67 yielded readable primary provider pages, 8 returned HTTP 403 but their identity/purpose were corroborated through independently indexed official provider pages, one official FindTreatment.gov root yielded only a minimal JavaScript shell while its official `/locator` works, and the original CDC ADHD route yielded no readable content. The CDC URL was replaced on this review branch with a fully readable and dated CDC page (`/adhd/php/communication-resources/index.html`, updated/reviewed October 21, 2026).

Publisher dates are recorded **only where the page explicitly exposed one**; a crawl date is not a publication or professional review date. An undated provider page has the literal status “Not shown in inspected source.” A website being readable does not imply that every linked service is currently taking clients or that every age/role combination is appropriate.

**Audit classification totals:** 67 directly readable; 8 blocked but independently corroborated; 1 minimal app shell independently corroborated; 1 CDC old page unreadable with verified replacement.

### Substantiated changes proposed (no mainline edits)

1. Restore tween discovery for Child Mind Institute's *Tweens, Teens, & Young Adults* (source includes approximately ages 9–12; an invented minimum age 13 incorrectly excluded them). Include adult users for young-adult material.
2. SAMHSA RecoverMe's own program description identifies ages **18–25** as primary audience; correct the child/adolescent filter, retain young-adult coverage.
3. Eldercare Locator explicitly serves **older adults and their families/caregivers**. Correct its overly broad across-ages indexing; don't make it look like a child resource.
4. Label ACL National Resource Centers, the Housing & Services Resource Center, ILRU and VA AboutFace professional tools as primarily professional/system-facing where the publisher says so; preserve individual support options through DIAL and Eldercare.
5. CYVYC reported that its funded initiative concluded, but its public youth-rights and alternatives-to-guardianship materials remain online. Label the continuing resource library accurately rather than suggesting an ongoing intake program.
6. Rename DIAL according to its currently displayed **Disability Information and Access Locator** name.
7. AHCCCS 988 grant implementation information is not the same as a crisis hotline, which has its own separate CPL listing. Correct role/title categorization.
8. Federal parent-center funding pages are not direct family enrollment forms. Clarify the funded-program role and expand early-childhood discovery in IDEA birth-to-26 materials.
9. Add early-childhood indexing only where the source describes infants, preschoolers, or toddlers (including relevant Child Mind Institute pages, NCTSN child-welfare resources). Preserve existing teen and clinician entries.

**Status:** Review-only correction branch and PR. Do not merge/deploy without explicit owner approval; clinical/legal/rights/native accessibility gates remain distinct.

## Remaining unresolved checks

- SAMHSA and ed.gov 403 responses can be access controls; an automatically blocked fetch is not evidence of site closure. Verify from a normal browser when practical, and do not replace with a conjectured URL.
- FindSupport.gov also blocked automated inspection, but the current SAMHSA Find Help publisher index names it. Keep it flagged for a human browser spot check.
- The FindTreatment.gov app home is JavaScript-heavy; its official treatment locator provides filtering by age, location, service and payment. Age of a *listing* is not proof that an individual facility accepts a given client; contact the facility.
- Availability, age eligibility, financial costs, staff credentialing, accessibility, language access and legal coverage may vary by program, provider and locality. Confirm individual referrals at the original provider, especially crisis and disability services.
- This audit verifies publisher navigation and corrects evidenced catalog framing. It is not a 77-item efficacy review, a clinical recommendation, or a full native assistive-technology test.
