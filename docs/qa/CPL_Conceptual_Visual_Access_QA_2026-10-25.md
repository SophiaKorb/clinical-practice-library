# CPL Visual Access — Conceptual QA

**Review date:** 25 October 2026  
**Scope:** local *CPL 17 Tools Black & White Print v3* and source `resources/therapy/communication-supports-toolkit.html` at `main`.  
**Status:** **NOT APPROVED FOR REPLACEMENT, MERGE, OR RELEASE**. Artwork approval is not tool/content approval.

## Main finding

The new layout is visually clearer, but **most symbols cannot be assumed to improve communication**. They generally illustrate an already written label. A communication support should let the person *express something*, *understand something*, or *make or refuse a genuine choice* that would otherwise be harder. Many v3 pages primarily organize instructions for a clinician or care partner, and must be labeled accordingly. There is no user comprehension testing yet.

**Provenance correction:** The source CPL includes *When Words Get Stuck* and *Conversation Repair Card*. It does **not** include a tool called *Repair the Message*. That title was introduced by the redesign, and was **not separately approved**. The user approved six illustration examples as a style benchmark, not the proposed 17-tool inventory.

## Audit questions

1. **Function:** Is this for comprehension, expression, choice/refusal, joint reflection, scheduling, or staff instruction? Do not claim a staff checklist is a visual communication aid.
2. **Transparent referent:** Without reading the label, can the intended individual plausibly tell what the image means? Symbols are not intrinsically universal.
3. **Contrast:** Do visually similar/identical pictures appear for distinct or opposing ideas? Does the contrast survive covering the labels?
4. **Direction and agency:** Who is speaking or acting? Does “Show me” mean “Please show me” or “I will show you”? Can the person initiate, refuse, correct and express something else?
5. **Access:** Does the tool actually reduce language, memory, sensory, visual or motor demands for its intended user? Does a tiny/detailed picture create extra load?
6. **Consequences:** Does using a symbol automatically determine a support, indicate assent, or imply capacity? It must not.
7. **Continuity:** Is each existing CPL tool and clinical function preserved, instead of being silently replaced by an unlike tool?

## Important preservation failure: the new 17 are not one-to-one replacements for CPL's original 17

The current main communication toolkit still contains distinct original tools not faithfully represented in the v3 set:

- Emotion / Overload Thermometer (intensity scale)
- Sensory + Visual Load Check (sensory access and CVI-relevant constraints)
- Body Clues → Needs Map (not equivalent to a heart/FEELING icon)
- Transition Countdown (predicting the time until a change)
- Plan B Flexibility Map (a change board may help but is not a complete equivalent)
- Free Recall vs Recognition (assessment-demand distinction)
- Generalization Bridge (transfer from learning to new contexts)
- Social Meaning Decoder (examines alternate interpretations; not the same as *Two Perspectives*)
- Decision Support Menu (two options, concrete consequences and values)

Therefore **do not replace old links or deprecate originals** on the strength of this review packet. Each replacement must have a verified source-to-target crosswalk and evidence of preserved function.

## Examples of real communication improvements

**What Helps Me?:** Offer concrete, mutually distinguishable support choices (e.g., alone versus person stays; help with a task versus more time; quiet space versus moving), plus always-visible none/other/stop. The person can point, look, gesture or use their existing AAC. **The support is offered, not assigned**.

**Change Board:** Use personalized photos/drawings of the original and new activity and keep unchanged positions aligned. The board conveys *a change in the plan*; it need not encode feelings or demand agreement.

**Communication repair, if retained:** A user-facing support needs messages like “Not that,” “Try another way,” “More time,” “Show choices,” “I mean something else,” clearly addressed to the communication partner. A separate *partner-facing* strip can explain: pause, show, ask, verify. Do not merge speaker roles.

## Strict recommendation

- **Approve no replacement pages from this audit.** Preserve the two strong formats as provisional visual-comprehension layouts, and keep the remaining concepts in review.
- **Pause the unapproved “Repair the Message” tool name.** Crosswalk the idea against the existing *Conversation Repair Card* and *When Words Get Stuck* before any redesign is designated a replacement.
- **Split the collection into three named groups:** (1) communication/access choices the person can use, (2) comprehension/predictability visuals, (3) clinician/care-partner guides and observation sheets. Do not call all of them “visual communication aids.”
- **Test symbol recognition without words and meaning with words** using the intended audience; when the image conveys no meaning or misleads, eliminate it or use a personally meaningful photo/scene. Record failures explicitly.
- **Keep existing CPL, main, colorful branch, and production unchanged.** PR #21 remains draft/blocked until mapping, accessibility, meaning, clinical/SLP review and user approval.
