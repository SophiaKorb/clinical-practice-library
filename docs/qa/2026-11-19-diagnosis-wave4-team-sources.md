## Nonduplicative choice and rationale

Audited extant comprehensive and case-management guides for tics/BFRBs, sleep disorders and persistent conflict; the separate seven-format clinical-topic sets for `tics-context-impact`, `bfrb-awareness-support`, `insomnia-cbti-readiness`, `sleep-loss-activation`, `child-aggression-caregiver-coaching` and `sibling-conflict-safety-repair`; and the standalone ADHD youth-CFT working sheet.

Existing tic/BFRB tools already distinguish motor/vocal tics from hair pulling/skin picking, comorbidity, impact, specialist CBIT/HRT referral and dignity. Existing sleep tools already distinguish insomnia, apnea, circadian mismatch, medical safety and mood activation. Existing conflict tools address individual functional context, caregiver coaching, sibling safety and youth meeting input. **Genuine gap chosen:** a differentiated cross-system meeting map that clearly separates young-person voice, caregiver support, school process, qualified safety and care-team handoff with an **unresolved-disagreement state**. It does NOT duplicate an ADHD youth meeting one-page worksheet or pretend to be a formal CFT/IEP/service or safety plan.

## Official source record and scope

1. **AHCCCS AMPM Policy 580 — Child and Family Team**: [official policy PDF](https://www.azahcccs.gov/shared/Downloads/MedicalPolicyManual/500/580.pdf), effective **December 13, 2023**, 15 pages, original PDF visually inspected on pages 1–2. Policy expressly applies to ACC, ACC-RBHA, ALTCS E/PD, DCS/CHP and DES/DDD contractors and is **optional for fee-for-service** programs; do not generalize it to every provider. Sections III.A–E establish child/family partnership and individualized coordination, and later sections address youth/family participation, team membership and coordination with school IEP and other processes. The page states youth voice is important; the lab does **not** claim a universal rule requiring youth attendance at every type of meeting. Check the policy, contractor, guardian status and program authority before applying legal/policy requirements.
2. **IDEA 34 CFR §300.321 and §300.322**, [student and team rules](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/d/300.321), [meeting notices](https://sites.ed.gov/idea/regs/b/d/300.322): the transition-goals/services provisions specifically require an invitation to the student; do not apply that rule to every IEP meeting. The official regulations indexed by the Department of Education were accessible as snippets; direct page retrieval was blocked 403 in the web review. Parent participation requirements also depend on the actual procedure.
3. **Center on PBIS [Tier 3 Comprehensive FBA Guide](https://www.pbis.org/resource/tier-3-comprehensive-functional-behavior-assessment-fba-guide)**, first published January 31, 2022: qualified teams use multiple sources to examine behavior and develop function-linked supports; an isolated ABC anecdote or youth worksheet is not a completed functional assessment.
4. **CDC/AAN tic guidance**: [CDC Tourette treatment](https://www.cdc.gov/tourette-syndrome/treatment/index.html) and [AAN tic guideline](https://www.neurology.org/doi/10.1212/WNL.0000000000007466) support impairment/pain/context and watchful waiting or trained CBIT when appropriate, explaining why no redundant tic tool was added.
5. **VA/DoD 2025 Insomnia/OSA guidance**: [official guideline overview](https://www.healthquality.va.gov/guidelines/CD/insomnia/index.asp); adult CBT-I and medical differential/safety are already covered in CPL, so the expansion did not create a duplicate sleep schedule or self-administered restriction tool.

**Limits and gates:** Original fictional discussion and visible thinking structure have no evidence for clinical effectiveness, diagnostic accuracy or legal authorization. Separate actual safety/safeguarding response, privacy consent (FERPA/HIPAA/Part2 as applicable), agency role, youth/guardian participation, actual provider contacts, school decision and follow-up evidence. No forced repair or mediation when coercion, violence or safety risk exists.
